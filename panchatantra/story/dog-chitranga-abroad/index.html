Chitranga the dog abroad

The monkey tells this as his last word before the crocodile goes to fight for his home.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a certain town lived a dog named Chitranga. A long famine fell on the place, and for want of food the dogs and other creatures began to die out. Chitranga, thin with hunger and afraid, went away to another country. In a town there he found a house where the mistress was easy-going, and every day he slipped in and ate his fill of all kinds of food. But whenever he came out, the local dogs, proud and well fed, surrounded him on every side and tore him with their teeth.

He thought, “My own country is better. Even in a famine one lives in peace there, and nobody picks a fight. I will go back to my own town.” And he set off for home.

When he arrived, all his kin gathered round and asked, “Chitranga, tell us about the foreign land. Tell us what the country is like, how the people behave, what there is to eat and how things are done there.” He said, “There is little to tell. Food is plentiful and of every kind, and the women of the houses are easy-going. A foreign land has one fault: your own kind is against you.”

Sanskrit verse 4.83

The monkey said to the crocodile, “Trouble is to be looked for from one’s own kind. That is why you must go and face him yourself.”

What this story shows

He had food abroad and peace at home, and he chose peace.

The dog finds what he went for. The trouble is waiting outside the door, and it comes from other dogs, who have the least reason to welcome one more mouth.

The monkey’s point is narrow. The crocodile’s house has been taken by another crocodile, and a rival of one’s own kind is the one who wants exactly what one has. The tale also carries a plainer thought, that a hard life among one’s own can be better than an easy one among strangers.

  • Plenty is not much use where one is attacked every day.
  • The hostility came from his own kind, who competed for the same food.
  • The monkey sends the crocodile home to face a rival of his own kind.

A long famine fell on a town, and a dog named Chitranga went abroad to escape it.

In a foreign town he found a house where he could slip in and eat his fill every day. But each time he came out, the local dogs surrounded him and attacked him.

He decided that home was better, famine or not, and went back. He told his kin that abroad the food was plentiful and the houses easy to enter, and that the one fault was his own kind. The monkey says trouble comes from one’s own kind, and sends the crocodile to face his rival.
