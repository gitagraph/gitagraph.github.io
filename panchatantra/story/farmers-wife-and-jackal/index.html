The woman and the jackal by the river

The crocodile tells this to the monkey to say he has lost both his home and his friend.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

A farmer and his wife lived in a certain town. The farmer was old, and his wife’s thoughts were always elsewhere. She would not stay at home and went about looking for other men. A rogue who lived by taking other people’s money noticed her. He found her alone and said, “Lady, my wife is dead, and the sight of you has filled me with love. Be kind to me.”

She said, “If that is so, my husband has a great deal of money, and he is too old even to walk. I will take it and come to you, and we will go somewhere else and live as we please.”

He said that suited him well, and told her to come to that same spot at dawn, so that they could go to some fine city together. She agreed and went home smiling. That night, when her husband was asleep, she took all his money, and at daybreak she hurried to the place. The rogue put her in front and set off quickly toward the south.

When they had gone two yojanas, a river lay before them. The rogue looked at it and thought, “She is no longer young, and I have no use for her. Besides, someone may come after her, and that would mean great trouble for me. I will take her money and go.”

He said, “My dear, this river is hard to cross. Let me carry the money over first and leave it on the far bank. Then I will come back and take you across on my back with nothing else to carry.” She agreed and gave him all the money.

Then he said, “Give me your clothes as well, so that you can go into the water without worry.” She did, and the rogue took the money and the clothes and went where he had meant to go all along. She sat down on the bank with her arms about herself, sick with worry.

Just then a she-jackal came by with a piece of meat in her mouth. She saw a big fish that had come out of the water and was lying on the bank. She dropped the meat and ran at the fish. At that moment a vulture came down from the sky, took the meat and flew up again. The fish saw the jackal and slipped back into the river.

The jackal stood there with nothing for her trouble, looking up at the vulture. The woman smiled and said, “The vulture has taken the meat, and the fish has gone into the water. You have lost both fish and meat, jackal, and there is nothing left to look at.”

Sanskrit verse 4.69

The jackal answered, “Whatever cleverness I have shown, you have shown twice as much. You have neither the lover nor the husband, so there is nothing left for you to look at either.”

Sanskrit verse 4.70

The crocodile said to the monkey, “That is how it is with me. Two misfortunes have come at once. My home is broken, and I have lost the heart of a friend like you.”

What this story shows

Each of them let go of what she had for something better that was never hers.

The woman and the jackal make the same mistake a few steps apart. Each gives up what is in hand for something that looks bigger, and each is left watching it go.

The two see each other’s loss more clearly than their own, and say so. The crocodile, who tells the tale, is the one person in the book who applies a story to himself. He has lost his wife and his friend, and he knows it.

  • What is in hand is let go for what only looks within reach.
  • Each sees the other’s foolishness at once and her own too late.
  • The crocodile tells this tale against himself.

A farmer’s wife, whose husband was old, was looking elsewhere. A rogue told her he loved her, and she promised to bring her husband’s money and go away with him.

She took the money and went with him. At a river he said he would carry the money over first, and then asked for her clothes as well to keep them dry. He took everything and did not come back.

As she sat on the bank a she-jackal came by with a piece of meat. She dropped it to chase a fish, a vulture took the meat, and the fish slipped back into the river.

The woman said the jackal had lost both fish and meat. The jackal answered that the woman had lost both lover and husband. The crocodile tells this of himself, for he has lost his home and his friend.
