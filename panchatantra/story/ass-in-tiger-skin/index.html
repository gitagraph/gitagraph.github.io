The ass in the tiger’s skin

The monkey tells this to say the crocodile was undone by his own words.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a certain town lived a washerman named Shuddhapata. He had one ass, and for want of fodder it had grown very weak. One day, wandering in the forest, the washerman came on a dead tiger. He thought, “This is a piece of luck. I will cover the ass with this tiger’s skin and turn him loose in the barley fields at night. The field guards will take him for a tiger and will not drive him out.”

He did so, and the ass ate barley to his heart’s content. Each morning the washerman brought him home again. As time went on the ass grew so fat that it was hard work to lead him to his tether.

One night, full of high spirits, he heard a she-ass bray far away. The moment he heard it he began to bray himself. The field guards knew then that this was an ass in a tiger’s skin, and they killed him with clubs, arrows and stones.

The monkey said to the crocodile, “He was well hidden and fearsome to look at in his tiger’s skin, and still he died because of his voice. You too gave yourself away by speaking.”

Sanskrit verse 4.47

What this story shows

The disguise held until he spoke in his own voice.

The washerman’s trick is cheap and it works for a long time. Nobody tests a tiger. The ass is safe as long as he is only looked at.

What ends it is one sound made without thinking. The monkey means the crocodile’s confession in mid-water, and the tale is as short as the mistake.

  • A borrowed appearance protects only while one stays silent.
  • The ass is betrayed by an impulse, when no one had suspected him.
  • The crocodile lost his chance the same way, by saying what he meant to do.

A washerman had an ass that was weak for want of fodder. He found a dead tiger in the forest and thought of covering the ass with its skin, so that it could graze in the barley fields at night.

It worked. The field guards took the ass for a tiger and left him alone, and he grew fat on barley.

One night he heard a she-ass far away and brayed in answer. The guards knew him for an ass then, and that was the end of him. The monkey says the crocodile, too, gave himself away by speaking.
