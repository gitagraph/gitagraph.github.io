The ass who would sing

The gold-finder tells this because his friend would not stop when told to.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a certain town lived an ass named Uddhata. All day he carried loads for a washerman, and at night he roamed where he pleased. At dawn he went back to the washerman’s house of his own accord, for fear of being caught and bound, and the washerman tied him up anyway.

On his rounds of the fields at night he made friends with a jackal. The ass was stout enough to break down a fence, and the two used to get into the cucumber fields, eat as much as they liked and go home at dawn.

One night the ass stood in the middle of a field, full of high spirits, and said, “Nephew, look how clear the night is. I am going to sing. Tell me which raga to choose.”

The jackal said, “Uncle, there is no call to stir up trouble. We are here as thieves, and thieves and lovers keep quiet. One who has a cough should not go thieving, if he wants to live.

Sanskrit verse 5.50

Besides, your singing is not sweet. It sounds like a conch and carries a long way. The guards are asleep in this field. They will get up and kill us or tie us up. Eat these cucumbers, which are as sweet as nectar, and leave singing alone.”

The ass said, “You live in the forest and do not know the pleasure of music. On a night when the autumn moon has driven the darkness far away, with a loved one near, the sweetness of song enters the ears of the fortunate.”

Sanskrit verse 5.51

The jackal said, “That is so, uncle. But you do not know how to sing. You only bray, and it will cost us what we came for.” The ass said, “Shame on you, fool. I know music well. Listen.

There are seven notes, three scales and twenty-one modes, forty-nine runs, three measures and three tempos, nine moods, thirty-six ragas and forty emotions. Bharata himself counted a hundred and eighty-five parts of song. Nothing is dearer than song, even to the gods. Ravana won over the three-eyed god with the sound of dried sinews. You have no reason to call me ignorant and stop me.”

Sanskrit verses 5.52, 5.53, 5.54, 5.55

The jackal said, “Uncle, in that case I will stand at the gap in the fence and watch for the guard. Sing as you please.” He went, and the ass began to bray. The field guard heard it and came running, grinding his teeth with rage. He beat the ass with his club until he fell to the ground. Then he tied a mortar with a hole in it round the ass’s neck and went back to sleep.

The ass got up in a moment, for the pain had left him, as it does with his kind. The text says that a dog, a horse and above all an ass do not feel a blow for longer than a moment. He broke through the fence with the mortar still on his neck and began to run.

Sanskrit verse 5.56

The jackal saw him from a distance and said with a smile, “Well sung, uncle. I told you to stop and you would not. That is a rare jewel they have hung on you, the reward of your singing.”

Sanskrit verse 5.57

The gold-finder said to the wheel-bearer, “You too would not stop, though I held you back.”

What this story shows

He knew the whole theory of music and could not hear his own voice.

The ass is not ignorant. He can recite every part of the science of song. What he lacks is any sense of where he is, what he sounds like, and who is asleep nearby.

The jackal tries reason, then gives up and looks after himself. The gold-finder draws the same line with his friend: he warned him at the gold, and he will not share the wheel.

  • Knowing a subject is no proof that one can do it, or that now is the time.
  • The friend who warned him stood clear when the warning was refused.
  • The ass’s reward was a weight tied round his neck.

An ass who worked for a washerman roamed at night with his friend the jackal. They used to break into the cucumber fields and eat their fill.

One clear night the ass said he would sing. The jackal warned that thieves keep quiet and that his voice carried like a conch, and the ass recited the whole science of music to prove he knew it.

The jackal went to stand at the gap in the fence, and the ass brayed. The guard woke and came running. He beat the ass and tied a heavy mortar round his neck.

The ass broke out through the fence with the mortar still hanging on him. The jackal called it a rare jewel, the reward of his singing. The gold-finder says his friend would not stop when told, either.

What this story shows

He knew the whole theory of music and could not hear his own voice.

  • Knowing a subject is no proof that one can do it, or that now is the time.
  • The friend who warned him stood clear when the warning was refused.
  • The ass’s reward was a weight tied round his neck.
