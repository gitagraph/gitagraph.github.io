The four sons-in-law who overstayed

The monkey tells this because the crocodile will not take a hint and go.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a city named Vikantaka lived a very rich merchant named Ishvara. His four sons-in-law came from Avanti to visit him, and he received them with great honour, with fine food and clothes. They stayed on in that way for six months.

At last Ishvara said to his wife, “These sons-in-law are so pleased with the honour we show them that they will not go home. They will not leave without a slight. So today, at mealtime, give them no water to wash their feet. They will see the insult and go.”

It was done. Garga left over the foot-washing. Soma left when he was given a low seat, and Datta left when he was served poor food. So three of them went. The fourth, Shyamalaka, still would not go, and he was taken by the neck and put out.

The monkey said to the crocodile, “Garga went for want of water for his feet, Soma for a low seat, Datta for poor food, and Shyamalaka with a hand on his neck. You have borne one insult after another and are still here. It seems you mean to leave the way Shyamalaka did.”

Sanskrit verse 4.48

What this story shows

Each guest had his own measure of what he could not bear, and one had none.

The merchant never tells them to go. He lowers the welcome one notch at a time, and each guest leaves at the notch he cannot bear.

The fourth is the point of the story. Someone who notices no slight can only be shown the door by hand. The monkey has refused, mocked and lectured the crocodile, and the crocodile is still under the tree.

  • A hint works only on someone who can feel it.
  • The three who left kept their dignity, and the fourth lost it at the door.
  • The monkey is telling the crocodile, as plainly as he can, to go.

A rich merchant’s four sons-in-law came to visit and were treated so well that they stayed six months. He told his wife to stop giving them water for their feet, so that they would feel the slight and go.

One left over the foot-water, one over a low seat and one over poor food. The fourth would not go and was put out by the neck. The monkey says the crocodile is like that fourth one.

What this story shows

Each guest had his own measure of what he could not bear, and one had none.
