The proud snake and the ants

Sthirajivi tells this to Meghavarna right after the story of the rogues. His point is that a quarrel with many is unwise, even when each of them is weak.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a certain anthill lived a huge black snake named Atidarpa, Great-Pride. His hole had its own regular way in and out.

One day he left that way and began to crawl out through another opening, a narrow one. His body was large, the hole was small and fate was against him. As he forced his way through, his body was torn and a wound opened.

Ants followed the smell of the blood from the wound. They covered him on every side and gave him no rest.

He killed some and struck at others, but there was no counting them. Because they were so many, his wounds spread until his whole body was torn. So Atidarpa died.

“That is why I say,” said Sthirajivi, “that one should not make enemies of the many, for a multitude is hard to overcome. Ants eat even a great snake, however he thrashes.”

Sanskrit verse 3.121

What this story shows

The snake could kill any ant he reached. There were too many to reach.

The story is only a few lines long in the source. A snake whose name means great pride leaves his usual way for a tight one and is hurt. Nothing that follows is a fair fight. He is strong and each ant is nothing, and their number decides it.

Sthirajivi tells it to his king as a reason for care. He is about to give up open battle with the owls for another way. The plain point is that strength has to be weighed against numbers too.

  • The wound came from his own choice of a narrow way.
  • He could kill any single ant and could not answer all of them.
  • A small hurt became fatal because so many came to it.

A huge black snake lived in an anthill. He always came and went by his own wide passage.

One day he forced his way out through a narrow hole and was wounded. Ants followed the smell and came at him from every side.

He killed some and struck at others. There were too many to count, and they kept coming.

The great snake died of it. The old crow minister tells this to say that a crowd is hard to fight, even a crowd of the small.
