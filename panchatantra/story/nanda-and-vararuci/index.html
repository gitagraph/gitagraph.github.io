The king who neighed

The monkey tells this to say the crocodile is ruled by his wife.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

There was a king named Nanda, famous for his strength and courage. The crowns of many kings lit up his footstool, his fame was as clear as the autumn moon, and he ruled the earth as far as the sea. His minister was Vararuci, who had mastered every science.

One day Vararuci’s wife was angry with him after a lovers’ quarrel. He loved her dearly and tried in every way to please her, and she would not be pleased. He said, “My dear, tell me what will make you happy and I will do it.” At last she said, “Shave your head and fall at my feet, and I will be gracious.” He did, and she was content.

Then Nanda’s queen was angry in the same way, and nothing he tried would please her. He said, “My dear, I cannot live a moment without you. I will fall at your feet.” She said, “Let me put a bit in your mouth and climb on your back and make you run. If you neigh like a horse as you run, I will be pleased.” The king did exactly that.

In the morning the king was seated in his assembly when Vararuci came in. The king looked at him and asked why he had shaved his head when it was not the day for it. Vararuci answered, “There is nothing a man will not give and nothing he will not do when women ask it of him. Where those who are not horses neigh, heads are shaved out of season.”

Sanskrit verse 4.45

The monkey said to the crocodile, “You too are ruled by your wife, like Nanda and Vararuci. That is why you set about killing me. And it was your own tongue that gave you away.”

What this story shows

Each knows the other’s secret, and the minister says so in one polite verse.

It is a comic tale and the text tells it lightly. Two powerful men do something silly to end a quarrel at home, and each hopes no one will know.

The joke is in the morning after. The king asks about the shaved head as if he had nothing to hide, and the minister answers without naming anyone. The monkey uses it to tease the crocodile, who went much further than a shaved head to please his wife.

  • Both men are powerful in public and helpless in a quarrel at home.
  • The minister answers the king’s question by hinting at the king’s own night.
  • The monkey’s jibe is that the crocodile’s wish to please went as far as murder.

A minister’s wife was angry with him and would not be soothed. At last she said he must shave his head and bow at her feet, and he did.

Then the king’s queen was angry in the same way. She asked to put a bridle on him, ride on his back and hear him neigh like a horse. The king did that too.

In the morning the king asked his minister why his head was shaved out of season. The minister answered that a man will do anything when women ask, and that where men neigh, heads are shaved. The monkey says the crocodile is ruled in the same way.
