The brahmin who asked a question

The chamberlains tell this to the king, to make him ask the brahmins before he acts.

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

In a certain forest lived a demon named Chandakarma. One day, roaming the woods, he came on a brahmin. He climbed onto the brahmin’s shoulders and said, “Walk on.” The brahmin was terrified and set off carrying him.

As he walked he noticed the demon’s feet, which were as soft as the inside of a lotus, and he asked why they were so tender. The demon said, “I am under a vow. I never touch the ground with wet feet.” The brahmin turned this over in his mind, looking for a way to get free.

Presently they came to a lake. The demon said, “I am going to bathe and worship the gods. Do not move from this spot until I come back.”

The brahmin thought, “As soon as his worship is done he will eat me. I will go as fast as I can now. His feet will be wet, and he will not come after me.” He ran, and the demon, afraid of breaking his vow, did not follow. The chamberlains said to the king, “That is why we say a wise person should always be one who asks.”

Sanskrit verse 5.88

What this story shows

One idle question gave him the only fact he needed.

The brahmin does not ask in order to escape. He asks because he notices something odd, and the answer happens to be the demon’s weak point.

The chamberlains use the tale to slow the king down. He has given an order about his daughter in the first moment, and they want him to ask before it is carried out. Asking does not save her from exile, but it does stop the first order.

  • Noticing an odd detail and asking about it cost nothing.
  • The demon was held by his own rule.
  • The chamberlains tell it so that the king will ask before acting.

A demon in a forest caught a brahmin, climbed onto his shoulders and made him walk.

The brahmin noticed how soft the demon’s feet were and asked why. The demon said he was under a vow never to touch the ground with wet feet. At a lake he got down to bathe and told the brahmin to wait.

The brahmin ran while the demon’s feet were wet, and the demon would not break his vow to follow. The chamberlains tell this to show the king the use of asking.
