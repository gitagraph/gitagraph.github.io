The sparrow and the elephant

The sandpiper hen tells this to her mate, to make him call the other birds before he takes on the sea.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

“A sparrow, a woodpecker, a fly and a frog,” said the sandpiper hen. “By making enemies of so many, an elephant went to his ruin.” “How was that?” asked her mate.

A pair of sparrows lived in a forest. They had built their nest in a tamala tree, and in time there were eggs in it.

One day a wild elephant in rut came to that tree, driven by the heat to look for shade. In his frenzy he caught the branch where the sparrows lived with the tip of his trunk, and broke it off. Every egg was smashed. The two sparrows had more life owing to them, and only just escaped.

The hen sparrow wept for her eggs and could find no comfort. A woodpecker, her dear friend, heard her and came, grieved by her grief.

“Lady, what use is this weeping?” he said. “The wise do not mourn what is lost, dead or past. A fool who grieves over what cannot be helped only adds one sorrow to another.”

“That may be so,” said the sparrow. “But that wicked elephant destroyed my children in his frenzy. If you are truly my friend, think of some way to kill him. Then this grief may leave me.”

“You are right, lady,” said the woodpecker. “A friend is one who stands by you in trouble, though he is of another kind. In good times everybody is everybody's friend. Watch what my wit can do. But I have a friend too, a fly named Vinarava. Let me fetch her, so that this wicked elephant may be killed.”

He took the sparrow to the fly. “Good lady, this sparrow is dear to me. A wicked elephant has wronged her and smashed her eggs. I am working out how to kill him. Please help me.” “There is nothing to discuss,” said the fly. “We do good to friends so that it comes back to us. What would a friend not do for a friend's friend? But I have a friend too, a frog named Meghanada. Let us call him as well and do this properly.”

The three went to Meghanada and told him the whole story. “What is that wretched elephant against many who are angry?” said the frog. “Do as I say.”

“Fly, go to him at noon and hum in his ear, sweet as a lute. He will close his eyes with the pleasure of listening. Then the woodpecker will put out his eyes with his beak. Blind and thirsty, he will hear me and my family croaking at the edge of a pit. He will take it for a pond and come. He will reach the pit, fall in and die. We must act together, and the score will be settled.”

So it was done. The elephant closed his eyes at the fly's song, and the woodpecker took his sight. At noon he wandered blind, following the sound of the frogs. He came to a great pit, fell in and died.

“That is why I say,” the hen finished, “a sparrow and a woodpecker, a fly and a frog: by making enemies of so many, the elephant went to his ruin.”

What this story shows

Many small hands, each doing one thing in turn, can bring down what none could touch alone.

None of the four could have hurt the elephant alone. The fly can only hum, the woodpecker can only peck, the frog can only croak. The plan is to put each small thing in the right order, at the right hour. It also runs on friendship of a particular kind: the woodpecker helps the sparrow, the fly helps the woodpecker's friend, the frog helps the fly's friend's friend. Nobody asks what is in it for them. The elephant, for his part, never knew he had enemies. He broke a branch because he was hot.

The sandpiper hen tells this to turn her mate from a war he would fight alone to one he might win with others, and it works: he calls the birds. The story is plain about what the four friends do. They blind a creature and lead him into a pit, and the text calls that settling the score. It shows what the careless strong can bring on themselves, and how far grief will go once it has friends. It does not stop to ask whether the score was fair.

  • The strong make enemies without noticing. The enemies notice.
  • A small skill used at the right moment can decide a large matter.
  • Before you join a friend's revenge, look at where the plan ends.
