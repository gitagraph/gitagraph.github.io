All 72 Panchatantra stories

Every story of the Panchatantra, book by book: 72 stories in English and Hindi, with the Sanskrit verses.

Book 1: Mitra Bhed

  • Mitra BhedThe book's own story: Losing friends: a lion, a bull and a jealous jackal
  • 1.1The monkey and the wedgeA monkey with nothing to do finds a wedge in a half-split log, and pulls.
  • 1.2The jackal and the drumA starving jackal hears a terrible booming on an old battlefield, and decides to look before he runs.
  • 1.3Dantila and the sweeper
  • 1.4Devasharma the ascetic
  • 1.5The weaver who came as Vishnu
  • 1.6The crows and the black snakeTwo crows cannot fight the snake that eats their young, so they ask a friend who thinks.
  • 1.7The heron and the crabAn old heron who can no longer fish finds another way to eat.
  • 1.8The lion and the hareA lion is fed one animal a day by agreement, until the day the turn falls to a hare.
  • 1.9The louse and the bugA louse lives well in a king's bed, until she lets a hungry stranger stay the night.
  • 1.10The blue jackalA jackal falls into a vat of indigo and walks out a king.
  • 1.11The lion's court and the camelA camel is promised safety at a lion's court. Then the lion is wounded, and the court grows hungry.
  • 1.12The sandpipers and the seaThe sea carries off a small bird's eggs, and the bird declares war on the sea.
  • 1.13The tortoise and the geeseTwo geese carry a tortoise through the sky on a stick. He has one thing to remember.
  • 1.14The three fishThree fish hear the fishermen say they will come back at dawn. Two leave that night.
  • 1.15The sparrow and the elephantAn elephant breaks a branch for shade and smashes a sparrow's eggs. She has a friend, who has a friend, who has a friend.
  • 1.16The jackal, the lion and the young camel
  • 1.17The monkeys and the birdFreezing monkeys blow on red berries, hoping for fire. A bird tells them the truth, and keeps telling them.
  • 1.18The sparrow's nest and the monkeyA sparrow, snug in her nest, asks a soaked monkey why he has not built a house.
  • 1.20Dharmabuddhi and PapabuddhiTwo friends bury their money in a forest. One comes back at midnight, and then calls a tree as his witness.
  • 1.21The herons and the mongooseA heron whose chicks were eaten by a snake asks a crab what to do. The crab has his own reasons for answering.
  • 1.22The iron scale and the miceA merchant says mice ate the iron scale left in his keeping. The owner says a hawk has carried off the merchant's son.
  • 1.23The king's monkey, and the thiefA king's pet monkey swings a sword at a fly. A thief travelling with four men he means to rob meets robbers on the road.

Book 2: Mitra Samprapti

  • Mitra SampraptiThe book's own story: Winning friends: a crow, a mouse, a tortoise and a deer
  • 2.1The mouse and the monk’s bowlA mouse can leap to a bowl hung on a high peg. Then a guest works out where the leap comes from.
  • 2.2Hulled sesame for unhulledA woman offers to give hulled sesame and take unhulled in return. The offer is too good, and a boy says so.
  • 2.3The jackal and the bowstringA starving jackal finds a dead boar and a dead hunter. He decides to save the meat and start with the bowstring.
  • 2.4The merchant’s son and his one verseA merchant’s son pays a hundred coins for a book with one verse in it. It becomes his name, his only answer and, by morning, his fortune.
  • 2.5Somilaka the weaver and his goldA fine weaver earns gold twice and twice finds his bundle empty. Then a voice in the sky offers him a choice between two kinds of wealth.
  • 2.6The jackal who followed the bullA jackal’s wife sees something on a passing bull that looks ready to drop. The two of them follow it for fifteen years.

Book 3: Kakolukiya

  • KakolukiyaThe book's own story: Crows and owls: two kingdoms of birds at war
  • 3.1The hare, the elephant and the Moon LakeA herd of thirsty elephants tramples a warren of hares on its way to a lake. One hare goes to the elephant king and says the Moon has sent him.
  • 3.2The hare, Kapinjala and the catA hare moves into a sparrow’s empty hollow, and the two quarrel over whose home it is. They take the case to a cat who is preaching by the river.
  • 3.3The brahmin, the goat and the three roguesA brahmin is carrying home a goat for his sacrifice. Three hungry rogues meet him one after another, and each tells him it is some other animal.
  • 3.4The proud snake and the antsA great black snake squeezes out through a narrow hole and is wounded. The smell of blood brings the ants.
  • 3.5The brahmin, the snake and the gold coinA poor brahmin farmer offers milk to a snake in his field and finds a gold coin in the bowl each morning. Then his son decides to take all the gold at once.
  • 3.6The golden swans of the Lotus LakeGolden swans live on a royal lake and pay for it with a feather each, twice a year. When a new bird arrives, they tell him there is no room.
  • 3.7The dove, his mate and the hunterA fowler caught in a storm asks for shelter under a tree. The dove who lives there is the husband of the bird in his cage.
  • 3.8The old merchant, his young wife and the thiefAn old merchant has married a young wife who cannot bear to look at him. One night a thief comes into their room.
  • 3.9The brahmin, the thief and the rakshasaA thief on his way to steal a poor brahmin’s two calves falls in with a rakshasa who means to eat the brahmin. At the house they argue over who goes first.
  • 3.10The prince, the princess and the two snakesA prince is wasting away because a snake lives in his belly. A princess is married off to him as a punishment for speaking her mind.
  • 3.11The carpenter who hid under the bedA carpenter hears talk about his wife and pretends to leave on a journey so that he can watch her. She notices him under the bed and makes up a story on the spot.
  • 3.12The mouse who became a maidenA sage saves a mouse from a hawk, turns her into a girl and raises her as his daughter. When she is grown he offers her the Sun for a husband.
  • 3.13Ekata, Dvita and TritaThree brothers have such power from their austerities that their wet cloths hang in the air. Then a vulture takes a frog, and two of them speak.
  • 3.14The bird whose droppings were goldA bird whose droppings turn to gold is caught by an old hunter and given to a king. A minister says such a thing cannot be, and the king lets it go.
  • 3.15The lion, the jackal and the talking caveA hungry lion hides in a cave to wait for whoever lives there. The jackal who owns it sees tracks going in and none coming out.
  • 3.16The old snake who carried the frogsAn old snake too weak to hunt comes to a pond and says a curse has made him the frogs’ mount. The frog king is delighted to ride him.
  • 3.17The brahmin who went blind on gheeA wife asks the Goddess how to make her husband blind. The voice that answers from behind the image is her husband’s.

Book 4: Labdha Pranash

  • Labdha PranashThe book's own story: Losing what you had: a monkey who trusted a crocodile
  • 4.1Gangadatta the frog kingA frog king invites a snake into his well to get rid of his rivals.
  • 4.2The ass with no ears and no heartAn ass escapes a lion once and is talked into going back.
  • 4.3Yudhishthira the potterA scar on a potter’s forehead is taken for a warrior’s wound.
  • 4.4The jackal cub reared by a lionessA lioness rears a jackal cub with her own two, and no one tells him.
  • 4.5The brahmin who gave half his lifeA brahmin gives half his own life to bring his wife back, and she leaves him for another.
  • 4.6The king who neighedA minister shaves his head and a king neighs like a horse, each to please an angry wife.
  • 4.7The ass in the tiger’s skinAn ass in a tiger’s skin grazes in safety until he hears a she-ass and answers.
  • 4.8The four sons-in-law who overstayedFour sons-in-law come to visit and are still there six months later.
  • 4.9The carpenter who carried his wifeA carpenter hides under his own bed to test his wife and comes out believing her.
  • 4.10The old merchant who thanked a thiefAn old merchant is so pleased by one frightened embrace that he offers a thief everything he owns.
  • 4.11The woman and the jackal by the riverA woman leaves her husband for a rogue, and a jackal drops her meat for a fish. Each ends with nothing.
  • 4.12The camel who wore a bellA pet camel wears a bell and likes to trail behind the herd.
  • 4.13The jackal who kept an elephantA jackal finds a dead elephant he cannot open, and four visitors arrive one after another.
  • 4.14Chitranga the dog abroadA dog leaves a famine for a land of plenty and comes home again.

Book 5: Aparikshit Karak

  • Aparikshit KarakThe book's own story: Acting without thinking: people who acted first and understood later
  • 5.1The brahmin’s wife and the mongooseA mother comes home, sees blood on the mongoose’s mouth and acts at once.
  • 5.2The scholars who made a lionThree scholars can bring a dead lion to life. The fourth can tell that they should not.
  • 5.3The four learned foolsFour scholars come home after twelve years of study and consult their books at every step.
  • 5.4The two clever fish and the frogTwo fish with many tricks decide to stay. A frog with one idea leaves that night.
  • 5.5The ass who would singAn ass stealing cucumbers by night decides the moon calls for a song.
  • 5.6The weaver who asked for a second headA weaver is offered any wish and asks two people what it should be.
  • 5.7The brahmin and the pot of mealA brahmin lies under a pot of barley meal and counts the fortune it will bring.
  • 5.8The monkey chief’s warningAn old monkey sees a kitchen quarrel and foresees the death of his whole troop.
  • 5.9The demon, the thief and the monkeyA demon overhears a word, takes it for the name of a rival and hides among the horses.
  • 5.10The blind man, the hunchback and the princessA plan to poison a blind husband ends by changing all three people in the house.
  • 5.11The brahmin who asked a questionA brahmin carrying a demon on his shoulders asks why its feet are so soft.
  • 5.12The bird with two headsA bird with one belly and two heads finds a fruit, and one head will not share.
  • 5.13The crab who went alongA mother will not let her son travel alone, so she sends a crab with him.
