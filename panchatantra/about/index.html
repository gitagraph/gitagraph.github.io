About this edition

What this is

This is the Panchatantra, retold in English and Hindi for general readers. It is being published one book at a time. Book 1, Losing friends, is here now. The other four books will follow.

The book

The Panchatantra is a Sanskrit book of stories set inside other stories. Its frame tells of a teacher, Vishnusharman, who promised to make three princes wise in six months and taught them through tales. It has five books, each built around one long story.

It was probably first composed around 300 CE, give or take a century or two. It has been retold in more than fifty languages. Many Sanskrit versions of it survive, and they differ in which stories they include and how the stories end.

The Sanskrit text

The Sanskrit follows the GRETIL e-text of the Panchatantra, entered by Jan Brzezinski from the edition of Ramchandra Jha (Chowkhamba Vidyabhavan, Varanasi, sixth edition, 1991), with the edition of M. R. Kale also consulted. GRETIL distributes it under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 licence.

The e-text is in roman letters. The Devanagari shown here is produced from it by rule, so it matches the e-text exactly, including its slips. The verses have not yet been checked against a printed edition. Where the e-text has an evident slip, the meaning given follows the likely sense.

The retellings

The stories are written fresh in each language from the Sanskrit. They are told as this edition has them, harsh endings included. Where a well-known telling ends differently, the story says so in a line.

Some stories are left out. This edition's Book 1 has twenty-two numbered stories, and eighteen are told here. A book's own long story runs through all of them, and each story opens from the place where a character begins to tell it.

After each story comes a short note on what it shows about how people behave, and then its Sanskrit verses with their meanings.

How it was made

The retellings, the verse meanings and the drawings were made with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, working from the Sanskrit text. Mistakes are possible. This is an early edition, and it will be corrected as it is checked.
