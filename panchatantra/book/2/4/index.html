The tortoise’s counsel

Mitra Samprapti: part 4 of 6

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

When the mouse had finished, Mantharaka spoke. “Friend, this crow is a true friend to you, there can be no doubt. He was thin with hunger, you are his natural food, and he carried you on his back and did not eat you even on the way.

A friend is one whose heart does not change over money, and one who is still a friend when trouble comes. In good times even a bad man turns into a friend. I have come to trust him too. So you are welcome. Live on the shore of this lake as if it were your own home.”

Sanskrit verses 2.114, 2.116

“Do not grieve that your wealth is gone and that you live far from home,” he went on. “The shade of a cloud, the affection of the wicked, youth and wealth all pass quickly.

Sanskrit verse 2.118

Riches may be hoarded and guarded like life itself, and they still will not walk five steps with a man on his last road. And a man with money is food everywhere, as meat is food for fish in the water, for beasts on land and for birds in the air.”

Sanskrit verses 2.119, 2.120

“To a steady mind no country is foreign. The lion makes his own whatever forest he walks into. Nothing is too heavy for the able or too far for the determined. Fortune herself comes looking for a home with one who is full of drive, quick to act, brave, grateful and firm in friendship. You are a storehouse of good sense, and you will not sink in a strange land.”

Sanskrit verses 2.124, 2.125, 2.126

“Besides, even wealth that has been won is lost when one’s deeds turn that way. It was yours for as many days as it was. What is not truly one’s own cannot be enjoyed even for a moment, and fate takes back even what came by itself. An unlucky man may earn wealth and still not get to enjoy it, as Somilaka found.” “How was that?” asked Hiranyaka.

“So, friend Hiranyaka, do not grieve over wealth,” said Mantharaka when the tale was told. “If money buried in a house made its owner rich, we could all call ourselves rich on that same money.

Sanskrit verse 2.151

Wealth that is not enjoyed is as good as not there. The way to keep what one has earned is to let it flow out, as a full tank is kept safe by its outlet. Wealth goes in one of three ways: it is given, it is enjoyed, or it is lost. Whoever neither gives nor enjoys is left with the third.”

Sanskrit verses 2.152, 2.154

“A wise person does not earn in order to hoard, for that way lies sorrow. Snakes live on air and are not weak. Wild elephants grow strong on dry grass. Sages pass their days on roots and fruit. Contentment is the greatest treasure a person has.

Sanskrit verse 2.156

The ease of those who are at peace and content is unknown to those who run here and there after money. It is better to keep clear of mud than to wash it off. There is no treasure like giving, no enemy like greed, no ornament like good conduct and no wealth like contentment. So be content, friend.”

Sanskrit verses 2.157, 2.162, 2.163

The crow had listened to all of it. “Friend, take to heart what Mantharaka says,” he told the mouse. “People who always say pleasant things are easy to find. One who will say what is unwelcome and good for you, and one who will listen to it, are both rare. Those who say such things are the real friends. The rest are friends in name.”

Sanskrit verses 2.166, 2.167

Wealth that is not given or enjoyed is as good as not there, the tortoise went on, and contentment is the greatest treasure. The crow told the mouse to take this to heart. A friend who says what is unwelcome and good, he said, is rare.

As they talked, a deer came running into the lake, driven by hunters. The crow flew up, the mouse hid in the reeds and the tortoise went under. The tortoise saw from the deer’s panting that he was frightened and told him to run for the thick forest.
