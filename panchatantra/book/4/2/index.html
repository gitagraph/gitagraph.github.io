I am no Lambakarna: the monkey refuses

Labdha Pranash: part 2 of 5

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

When he had heard it, the crocodile said, “Friend, then I will sit here and fast until I die, and my death will be on your head.” The monkey said, “Fool, I am not the foolish Lambakarna, who saw the danger with his own eyes and still went back to be killed. He came and went, and having gone he came again, a fool with no ears and no heart, and there he died.” The crocodile asked who Lambakarna was and how he had died after seeing the danger, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of an ass who went back to a lion a second time.

When he had told it, the monkey said, “So, fool, you laid your trap, and then you ruined it by telling the truth, as Yudhishthira did. One who drops his own interest and speaks the truth out of foolish pride loses what he was after, like a second Yudhishthira.” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of a potter whose scar was taken for a warrior’s wound.

Yudhishthira the potterStep inside

When he had told it, the monkey said, “Shame on you, fool. You set about this for the sake of a wife.” He added that women are never to be trusted, and he quoted a man who had left his family and lost half his life for a wife who then left him. The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of a brahmin who gave half his life for his wife.

Then the monkey said, “There is another tale that is rightly told. There is nothing a man will not give and nothing he will not do when women ask it of him. Where those who are not horses neigh, heads are shaved out of season.” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.
