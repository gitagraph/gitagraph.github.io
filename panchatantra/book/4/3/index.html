Betrayed by his own tongue

Labdha Pranash: part 3 of 5

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

When he had told it, the monkey said, “You too are ruled by your wife, wicked crocodile, like Nanda and Vararuci. That is why you came here and set about killing me.

But the fault of your own tongue gave you away. Parrots and mynas are caged because of what their own mouths say, and herons are left alone. Silence serves every purpose. An ass was well hidden and looked fearsome in a tiger’s skin, and he was killed for the sake of his voice.” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

When he had told it, the monkey said, “You have put up with insult after insult. It seems you will go only as Shyamalaka went, with a hand on your neck.” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of four sons-in-law who would not go home.

The four sons-in-law who overstayedStep inside

Then the monkey said, “I am not a fool like the carpenter. I have seen the change in you with my own eyes, and I will not trust you afterward. A fool is soothed with soft words even when the wrong is done before his eyes. The carpenter carried his own wife and her lover on his head.” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of a carpenter who carried his wife on his shoulder.

The carpenter who carried his wifeStep inside

When he had told it, the monkey said, “So, fool, I have seen you change, and I cannot go to your house. Yet it is no fault of yours that you try to win my trust.

Your kind is wicked by nature and does not grow gentle even in good company. That is the way of the wicked. A bad nature is not made clean by the advice of the good, as charcoal is not made white by rubbing.

Sanskrit verse 4.54

You are greedy for a wife’s favour and ruled by her, and others like you give up their work, their wealth and their friend for the same thing. A man once said to a thief, ‘She who always shrinks from me holds me close today. You have done me a kindness, and I wish you well. Take whatever I have.’” The crocodile asked how that had been, and the monkey told him.

The monkey tells of an old merchant who thanked a thief.

The old merchant who thanked a thiefStep inside

When he had told it, the monkey said, “In short, that man gave everything he had to the thief for a wife’s favour. You have done the same.”
