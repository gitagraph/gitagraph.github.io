Losing friends

A lion, a bull and a jealous jackal

A merchant's bull, Sanjivaka, sinks in the mud of a river bank and is left behind. He recovers, grows strong, and his bellow carries across the forest.

The lion king Pingalaka hears it and is afraid. Two jackals are watching him: Karataka, who is cautious, and Damanaka, who sees a chance.

Karataka tells a story

Damanaka goes to the lion and learns what frightens him. A sound by itself, he says, is nothing to fear.

Damanaka tells the lion a story

Damanaka brings the bull to the lion, and the two become close. Left out, he then sets about parting them.

Damanaka tells a story

The crows and the black snakeStep inside

Karataka is still unsure. The lion is fierce and the bull is clever. Damanaka answers that whoever has wit has strength.

Damanaka tells another story

The lion and the hareStep inside

Damanaka goes to the lion alone. The bull, he says, has told him in secret that he means to kill the lion and rule in his place. The lion is stunned. He cannot believe it of a friend he loves like his own life.

Damanaka warns the lion with a story

The louse and the bugStep inside

The lion will not turn on the bull. He gave him a promise of safety. Damanaka presses harder: a king who drops his own people for a stranger does not last.

Damanaka tells one more story

The blue jackalStep inside

Then Damanaka goes to the bull. The lion, he says, has decided to kill him in the morning and feed the court. The bull is shaken. He guesses that someone near the king has poisoned his mind, and he does not guess who.

The bull tells a story

The lion's court and the camelStep inside

The bull sees nothing left for him except to fight. That does not suit Damanaka: those horns could wound the lion. He would rather the bull ran. A servant has no business fighting his master, he says, and whoever starts a feud without knowing the other's strength comes off worse.
