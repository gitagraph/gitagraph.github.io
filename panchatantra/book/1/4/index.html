The jackal's plan

Mitra Bhed: part 4 of 8

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

The two jackals, thin with hunger, talked it over. “Karataka,” said Damanaka, “we count for nothing now. Pingalaka is so taken with Sanjivaka that he has turned from his own work. The whole court has drifted away. What is to be done?”

Karataka said, “A minister should tell the king his fault even when the king does not listen. And it was you who brought this eater of grass to our master. You raked out the hot coals with your own hand.”

Sanskrit verse 1.171

“That is true,” said Damanaka. “The fault is mine. The jackal was undone by the rams' fight, the ascetic by Ashadhabhuti, and the go-between by another woman's affair. All three did it to themselves.” “How was that?” asked Karataka.

“Then what should we do?” asked Karataka. “My wits will not fail me even now,” said Damanaka. “I will part Sanjivaka from our master. An arrow from an archer may kill one person, or none. A thought let fly by a clever mind can reach even the unborn. I will do it in secret, by a trick, so that neither of them knows.”

Sanskrit verse 1.215

“If either of them finds out,” said Karataka, “that is the end of you.” “Do not say so. Effort should not be given up even when luck has turned. And not even the creator sees to the bottom of a deception that is well made. A weaver once passed himself off as the god Vishnu.” “How was that?” asked Karataka.

Sanskrit verse 1.216

Karataka was still afraid. “Sanjivaka is intelligent and the lion is fierce. Your wits are bold, but you cannot part those two.” “Brother,” said Damanaka, “what looks beyond me is within my reach. What force cannot do, a plan can. A pair of crows once had a black snake killed with a gold chain.” “How was that?” asked Karataka.

“So nothing is out of reach for one who has wit,” Damanaka went on. “Whoever has wit has strength, and one without it has none. A lion in the forest, drunk with pride, was brought down by a hare.” “How was that?” asked Karataka.

“If you agree,” said Damanaka, “I will go and break their friendship with my wits.” “Go, then,” said Karataka. “May your road be a good one. Do as you have decided.”
