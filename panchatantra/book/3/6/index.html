The other ministers, and Raktaksha’s warning

Kakolukiya: part 6 of 9

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

After hearing it, Arimardana asked Diptaksha what he thought. Diptaksha said, “Lord, he is certainly not to be killed. A man once said to a thief, ‘She who always shrinks from me holds me close today. You have done me a kindness, and I wish you well. Take whatever I have.’

And the thief answered, ‘I see nothing of yours worth taking. If there is ever something to take, I will come again, provided she is not holding you.’” Arimardana asked who the woman was and who the thief was, and said he wished to hear it in full. Diptaksha told him.

Then Diptaksha said, “So people think well even of a thief who does them a good turn, and far more of one who has come for shelter. Besides, this crow has been wronged by his own side. He will add to our strength and show us where they are weak. For that reason he is not to be killed.”

Arimardana heard this and asked another minister, Vakranasa, what should be done as things stood. Vakranasa said, “Lord, he is not to be killed. Even enemies do us good when they quarrel with each other. A thief once gave a brahmin his life, and a rakshasa gave him his pair of cattle.” Arimardana asked how that had been, and Vakranasa told him.

The brahmin, the thief and the rakshasaStep inside

When he had heard it, Arimardana turned to Prakarakarna and asked what he thought. Prakarakarna said, “Lord, he is not to be killed. If he is spared, it may be that the days will pass pleasantly in friendship between us. Creatures who do not guard one another’s secrets perish, like the snake of the anthill and the snake of the belly.” Arimardana asked how that had been, and Prakarakarna told him.

Prakarakarna tells of the prince and the two snakes

The prince, the princess and the two snakesStep inside

After hearing it, Arimardana himself came to the same view, and so it was done. Raktaksha saw it and laughed to himself. Then he spoke again. “Alas. You have ruined our master with your bad counsel. Where those who deserve no honour are honoured and those who deserve it are slighted, three things follow: famine, death and fear.

Sanskrit verse 3.192

And a fool is calmed by soft words even when the wrong is done before his eyes. A carpenter once carried his own wife and her lover on his head.” The ministers asked how that had been, and Raktaksha told them.

Raktaksha tells of the carpenter Viravara

The carpenter who hid under the bedStep inside

When he had told it, Raktaksha said, “So we are torn up by the roots and lost. It is well said that the wise can tell enemies who wear the look of friends: they are the ones who set good advice aside and follow its opposite. Even gains already in hand are lost, at the wrong place and time, when a king’s ministers are without sense, as darkness is lost at sunrise.”
