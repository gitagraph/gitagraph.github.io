At the owls’ gate; Raktaksha leaves

Kakolukiya: part 7 of 9

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

The owls paid no heed to Raktaksha. They lifted Sthirajivi up and began to carry him to their fortress. As he was carried he said, “Lord, I am of no use to anyone in this state, and there is no point in taking me in. I wish to enter a blazing fire. Do me the kindness of giving me fire.”

Raktaksha understood what lay behind this and asked why he wished to go into a fire. Sthirajivi said, “It was for your sake that Meghavarna brought me to this. I want to be reborn as an owl, so that I can pay the crows back.” Raktaksha, who was skilled in the ways of kings, said, “Good sir, you are crooked, and clever with made-up words. Even born as an owl you would still think your own crow kind the best. There is a tale told of this. A mouse passed over the Sun, the Cloud, the Wind and the Mountain as husbands and went back to her own kind, for one’s own kind is hard to go beyond.” The ministers asked how that had been, and Raktaksha told them.

They paid no heed to Raktaksha’s words this time either, and, to the ruin of their own line, they brought Sthirajivi to their fortress. As he was carried along he laughed to himself and thought, “The one who said ‘let him be killed’ spoke for his master’s good, and he alone among them all knows what the science of policy truly means. If they had done as he said, not the least harm would have come to them.”

Sanskrit verse 3.215

At the gate of the fortress Arimardana said, “Give this well-wisher Sthirajivi whatever place he would like.” Sthirajivi heard this and thought, “I have to find a way to destroy them, and I cannot do it from the middle of the fortress. They would watch my every sign and be on their guard. I will stay at the gate and work my purpose from there.”

So he said to the owl king, “Lord, what you have said is kind and fitting. But I know policy too, and I come from your enemies. However loyal and honest I may be, it is not proper for me to live inside the fortress. I will stay here at the gate and serve you every day, made clean by the dust of your feet.” It was agreed. Every day the owl king’s servants ate their fill and then, by his order, gave Sthirajivi the best of the meat. In a few days he was as strong as a peacock.

Raktaksha saw Sthirajivi being fattened and was amazed. He said to the ministers and to the king, “This whole body of ministers is foolish, and so are you, lord. That is how I see it. As the saying goes: first I was a fool, next the bird-catcher, then the king and his minister, and it is a whole ring of fools.” They asked how that had been, and Raktaksha told them.

Fate was against them. Once again they set aside Raktaksha’s good advice and went on feeding Sthirajivi with plenty of meat and food of every kind. Then Raktaksha called his own followers together and spoke to them in private. “This is the end of our king’s safety, and of his fortress. I have said what a minister whose family has always served should say. Let us go now and find another fortress in the mountains. One who acts before trouble comes does well, and one who does not comes to grief. A jackal once said he had grown old in the forest and had never heard a cave speak.” They asked how that had been, and Raktaksha told them.

When he had told it, Raktaksha left for a distant country, and his own followers went with him.
