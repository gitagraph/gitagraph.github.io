Kill him or spare him: Raktaksha and Kruraksha

Kakolukiya: part 5 of 9

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

Raktaksha went on, “There is a story told that bears on this.” Arimardana asked how it went, and Raktaksha told him of the brahmin and the snake.

When he had told it, Raktaksha said, “So if this crow is killed, your kingdom is cleared of thorns with no effort at all.” Arimardana heard him and then asked Kruraksha what he thought.

Kruraksha said, “Lord, what he has said is without mercy. One who has come for shelter is not killed. There is a well-known tale of a dove who honoured, as a guest should be honoured, an enemy that came to him for shelter, and offered him his own flesh.” Arimardana asked how that had been, and Kruraksha told him.

The red-eyed minister told a story to show that an old injury is not forgotten. The second minister answered that one who comes for shelter must not be killed.
