Sense and learning

Aparikshit Karak: part 3 of 6

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

When the gold-finder had heard it he reproached him. “I told you again and again to stop, and you would not listen to me. There is nothing to be done now. You are learned and well born, and you have no sense.

It is rightly said that sense is better than learning and stands above it, and that those without sense perish, like the men who made the lion.” The wheel-bearer asked how that had been, and the gold-finder told him.

The gold-finder tells of three scholars who made a lion.

After that the gold-finder said, “Those who are skilled in the sciences and know nothing of how the world goes are all laughed at, like those learned fools.” The wheel-bearer asked how that had been, and the gold-finder told him.

The gold-finder tells of four scholars who trusted their books.

When the wheel-bearer had heard it he said, “That is not the cause of it. Those with the best of sense perish when a hostile fate destroys them, and one with little wit lives on happily in the same house.

Sanskrit verse 5.41

What fate guards stands firm with no one to guard it, and what fate strikes down is lost however well it is guarded. One left helpless in the forest lives, and one cared for at home does not. Hundred-wit rides on a head and Thousand-wit hangs down, and I, One-wit, play in clear water.” The gold-finder asked how that had been, and the wheel-bearer told him.
