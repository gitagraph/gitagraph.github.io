Greed, and a friend left behind

Aparikshit Karak: part 5 of 6

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

The gold-finder said, “So it is. And the fault is hardly yours, for greed makes a mockery of everyone and brings them to grief. One who acts out of greed and does not look to the end of it is made a mockery of, like King Chandra.” The wheel-bearer asked how that had been, and the gold-finder told him.

The gold-finder tells of a monkey chief who saw ruin coming.

The monkey chief’s warningStep inside

When he had told it, the gold-finder said, “Friend, give me leave, so that I can go home.” The wheel-bearer said, “Good sir, wealth and friends are gathered against the day of trouble. You cannot mean to leave me like this. One who leaves a friend in trouble and goes away hard-hearted is ungrateful, and for that wrong he goes to hell beyond doubt.”

Sanskrit verse 5.82

The gold-finder said, “That is true where there is something one can do. This is a place no human power can reach, and no one is able to set you free.

And the more I see your face twist with the pain of the turning wheel, the more I know that I must go quickly, before some harm comes to me too. From the look on your face, monkey, Vikala has got hold of you. Whoever runs will live.” The wheel-bearer asked how that had been, and the gold-finder told him.
