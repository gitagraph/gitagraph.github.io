A friend’s advice ignored

Aparikshit Karak: part 4 of 6

The cards tell it quickly, in pictures. The full story below has the voices and the verses, and is worth the time.

First time here: this story is from the Panchatantra. See what that is.

The gold-finder said, “That may be so. Even so, a friend’s word should not be overstepped. But there is nothing to be done. I held you back and you would not stop, out of greed and pride in your learning.

As the jackal said, ‘Well sung, uncle. I told you to stop and you would not. That is a rare jewel they have hung on you, the reward of your singing.’” The wheel-bearer asked how that had been, and the gold-finder told him.

The gold-finder tells of an ass who insisted on singing.

The ass who would singStep inside

When he had heard it the wheel-bearer said, “Friend, that is true. One who has no sense of his own and does not do what a friend tells him comes to ruin, like the weaver Mantharaka.” The gold-finder asked how that had been, and the wheel-bearer told him.

The wheel-bearer tells of a weaver who asked for a second head.

The weaver who asked for a second headStep inside

The wheel-bearer went on, “It is true. Everyone who is seized by the goblin of hope, which deserves no trust, ends by being laughed at. Someone has well said that one who makes plans about what has not yet come, and cannot come, ends up lying white with meal, like Somasharma’s father.” The gold-finder asked how that had been, and the wheel-bearer told him.

The wheel-bearer tells of a brahmin and his pot of meal.

The gold-finder said that a friend’s warning should be heeded. The wheel-bearer agreed, and added that hope makes fools of everyone.
