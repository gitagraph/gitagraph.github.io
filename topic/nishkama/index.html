Nishkama Karma

निष्कामकर्म

Desireless action

Action done without craving for its fruit; the core instruction of 2.47.

फल की लालसा के बिना किया गया कर्म; 2.47 का मूल उपदेश।

28 verses

  1. 2.38

    Treating pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, prepare for battle. In this way you will not incur sin.

  2. 2.47

    Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to inaction.

  3. 2.49

    Action done for its result is far inferior to the yoga of understanding, Dhananjaya. Seek refuge in that understanding; pitiable are those whose motive is the fruit.

  4. 2.51

    The wise, joined with this understanding, give up the fruit born of action, and, freed from the bondage of birth, reach the state beyond all suffering.

  5. 3.19

    So always do the work that must be done, without attachment; by acting unattached a person reaches the Supreme.

  6. 3.25

    Bharata, just as the unwise act out of attachment to their work, the wise should act without attachment, wishing to hold the world together.

  7. 3.30

    Hand over all your actions to me, with your mind fixed on the Self; free of craving and of the sense of "mine", and with your fever gone, fight.

  8. 4.14

    Actions do not stain me, nor do I long for their fruit. One who knows me in this way is not bound by actions.

  9. 4.19

    One whose every undertaking is free of desire and selfish intention, whose actions are burned up in the fire of knowledge, is called learned by the wise.

  10. 4.20

    Letting go of attachment to the fruit of action, always content and depending on nothing, such a person does nothing at all even while fully engaged in action.

  11. 4.21

    Without craving, with mind and body held in check, giving up all possessiveness, doing only the work the body needs, one incurs no sin.

  12. 4.22

    Content with whatever comes by chance, beyond the pairs of opposites, free of envy, and even-minded in success and failure, one is not bound even when acting.

  13. 5.10

    One who acts by placing all actions in Brahman and giving up attachment is not touched by sin, as a lotus leaf is not wetted by water.

  14. 5.12

    One who is steady gives up the fruit of action and attains lasting peace; one who is not, driven by desire and attached to the fruit, is bound.

  15. 6.1

    One who does the work that ought to be done without leaning on its fruits is both a renunciant and a yogi; not the one who has merely put out the sacred fire, nor the one who simply stops acting.

  16. 12.11

    And if you cannot do even this, then, taking refuge in my yoga and with self-control, give up the fruits of all your actions.

  17. 12.12

    Knowledge is better than mechanical practice; meditation surpasses knowledge; giving up the fruits of action surpasses meditation, and from that giving up, peace follows at once.

  18. 17.11

    Sacrifice offered according to scriptural rule by those who seek no reward, with the mind settled on the thought that it simply ought to be done, is sattvic.

  19. 17.17

    When this threefold austerity is practised with the highest faith by disciplined people who seek no reward, it is called sattvic.

  20. 17.20

    A gift given simply because giving is right, to someone who can do nothing in return, at the proper place and time and to a worthy recipient, is held to be sattvic.

  21. 17.25

    With the word 'Tat', and without aiming at any reward, those who seek liberation perform the various acts of sacrifice, austerity and charity.

  22. 18.2

    The wise understand sannyasa as setting aside actions driven by desire, while the discerning describe tyaga as giving up the fruits of every action.

  23. 18.6

    Yet even these acts should be done after giving up attachment and the fruits. This, Partha, is my settled and highest view.

  24. 18.9

    When a prescribed action is done simply because it ought to be done, Arjuna, giving up attachment and the fruit, that relinquishment is considered sattvic.

  25. 18.11

    No embodied being can give up actions completely. But one who gives up the fruits of action is truly called a relinquisher.

  26. 18.17

    One who has no sense of being the doer and whose understanding is not stained, even if he kills these people, does not kill and is not bound.

  27. 18.23

    An action that is prescribed, done without attachment, without liking or dislike, by one who does not seek its fruit, is called sattvic.

  28. 18.49

    One whose understanding is unattached everywhere, who has mastered himself and is free of craving, attains through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action.
