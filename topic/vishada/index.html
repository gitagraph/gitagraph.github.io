विषाद

Despondency

Arjuna's grief and confusion, which the Gita treats as the doorway to the teaching.

अर्जुन का शोक और भ्रम, जिसे गीता उपदेश का द्वार मानती है।

18 verses

  1. 1.28

    …overcome with deep pity and sinking in sorrow, said this. Arjuna said: Krishna, seeing my own people standing here eager to fight,

  2. 1.29

    my limbs give way and my mouth goes dry; my body trembles and my hair stands on end.

  3. 1.30

    Gandiva is slipping from my hand, my skin is burning all over; I cannot stand steady, and my mind seems to be reeling.

  4. 1.31

    I see ill omens, Keshava, and I see no good coming from killing my own people in battle.

  5. 1.32

    I do not want victory, Krishna, nor a kingdom, nor pleasures. What use is a kingdom to us, Govinda, or enjoyments, or even life itself?

  6. 1.33

    The very people for whose sake we wanted kingdom, enjoyments and pleasures stand here in battle, having staked their lives and wealth:

  7. 1.35

    I do not wish to kill them, Madhusudana, even if they kill me — not even for rule over the three worlds, let alone for this earth.

  8. 1.45

    Alas, what a great sin we have resolved to commit, setting out to kill our own people out of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom!

  9. 1.46

    It would be better for me if the sons of Dhritarashtra, weapons in hand, killed me in battle unarmed and unresisting.

  10. 1.47

    Having spoken thus on the battlefield, Arjuna cast aside his bow and arrows and sank down on the seat of the chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief.

  11. 2.1

    To him, overcome with pity, his eyes troubled and full of tears, sinking in despair, Madhusudana spoke these words.

  12. 2.2

    Where has this weakness come from, Arjuna, at such a critical moment? It is not fitting for a noble person; it leads neither to heaven nor to honour.

  13. 2.4

    Madhusudana, slayer of enemies, how can I fight back with arrows in battle against Bhishma and Drona, who deserve my reverence?

  14. 2.5

    Better to live in this world even on begged food than to kill these noble elders. If I kill them, though they seek worldly gain, every pleasure I enjoy here will be stained with blood.

  15. 2.6

    Nor do we know which is better for us — that we conquer them or that they conquer us. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and if we killed them we would not even wish to live.

  16. 2.7

    My nature is overcome by the flaw of faint-hearted pity, and my mind is confused about dharma. I ask you: tell me clearly what is truly good for me. I am your disciple; I have come to you for refuge — guide me.

  17. 2.8

    I see nothing that could drive away this grief that is drying up my senses — not even gaining an unrivalled, prosperous kingdom on earth, or lordship over the gods.

  18. 2.9

    Having spoken thus to Hrishikesha, Gudakesha, scorcher of foes, said to Govinda, “I will not fight,” and fell silent.
