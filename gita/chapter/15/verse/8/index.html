Bhagavad Gita 15.8

Krishna

शरीरं यदवाप्नोति यच्चाप्युत्क्रामतीश्वरः ।
गृहीत्वैतानि संयाति वायुर्गन्धानिवाशयात् ॥ १५.८ ॥

śarīraṃ yadavāpnoti yaccāpyutkrāmatīśvaraḥ ।
gṛhītvaitāni saṃyāti vāyurgandhānivāśayāt ॥

Whenever this lord of the body takes up a body or leaves one, it carries these along with it, the way the wind carries scents away from their source.

हिन्दीजब यह शरीर का स्वामी (जीव) कोई शरीर पाता है या उसे छोड़ता है, तब यह इन्हें अपने साथ लेकर जाता है, जैसे हवा फूलों से गंध लेकर चल देती है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you move to a new city, you carry your habits and moods with you, the way the wind carries scent from flowers. What you cultivate now is what travels with you.

Krishna says that whenever the individual self takes up a body or leaves one, it carries the mind and senses with it, as the wind carries scents away from flowers.

'These' refers to the mind and five senses named in 15.7. The self is called īśvara, 'lord', here because it governs the body it lives in. The image explains continuity: like a scent, something subtle travels on even when its first home is left behind.
