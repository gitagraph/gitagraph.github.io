Bhagavad Gita 15.18

Krishna

यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तमः ।
अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुषोत्तमः ॥ १५.१८ ॥

yasmātkṣaramatīto'hamakṣarādapi cottamaḥ ।
ato'smi loke vede ca prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ ॥

Because I am beyond the perishable and higher even than the imperishable, I am celebrated in the world and in the Veda as Purushottama, the Supreme Person.

हिन्दीक्योंकि मैं नाशवान से परे हूँ और अविनाशी से भी श्रेष्ठ हूँ, इसलिए संसार में और वेद में मैं पुरुषोत्तम के नाम से प्रसिद्ध हूँ।

Krishna explains why he is called Purushottama: he is beyond the perishable and higher even than the imperishable.

The name joins puruṣa, 'person', with uttama, 'highest'. Here the speaker identifies himself with the highest Person of the previous verse, which traditionally gives the chapter its title, the 'Yoga of the Supreme Person'. It is one of the Gita's clearest statements of a reality higher than both the changing and the unchanging.
