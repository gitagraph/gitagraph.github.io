Bhagavad Gita 5.21

Krishna

बाह्यस्पर्शेष्वसक्तात्मा विन्दत्यात्मनि यत् सुखम् ।
स ब्रह्मयोगयुक्तात्मा सुखमक्षयमश्नुते ॥ ५.२१ ॥

bāhyasparśeṣvasaktātmā vindatyātmani yat sukham ।
sa brahmayogayuktātmā sukhamakṣayamaśnute ॥

One whose self is unattached to outer contacts finds the happiness that lies within. United with Brahman through yoga, that person enjoys a happiness that never fades.

हिन्दीजिसका मन बाहरी विषयों के स्पर्श में आसक्त नहीं है, वह अपने भीतर के आनंद को पाता है। ब्रह्म में योग द्वारा जुड़ा हुआ वह व्यक्ति अक्षय सुख भोगता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

If a weekend feels empty without shopping or an outing planned, try leaving an hour with nothing in it. There is a quieter happiness to find that doesn't hang on anything outside.

Krishna points to a happiness that does not depend on bāhya-sparśa, 'outer contacts', the meeting of the senses with their objects.

One who is unattached to these finds a happiness within, and, joined to Brahman through yoga, enjoys sukham akṣayam, a joy that does not wear away. The contrast with the next verse is deliberate: pleasures from contact come and go, while this joy does not depend on anything that arrives or leaves.
