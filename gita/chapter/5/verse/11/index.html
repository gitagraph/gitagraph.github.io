Bhagavad Gita 5.11

Krishna

कायेन मनसा बुद्ध्या केवलैरिन्द्रियैरपि ।
योगिनः कर्म कुर्वन्ति सङ्गं त्यक्त्वात्मशुद्धये ॥ ५.११ ॥

kāyena manasā buddhyā kevalairindriyairapi ।
yoginaḥ karma kurvanti saṅgaṃ tyaktvātmaśuddhaye ॥

Giving up attachment, yogis act with the body, the mind, the intellect, and even the senses alone, for the purification of the self.

हिन्दीयोगीजन आसक्ति छोड़कर, केवल शरीर, मन, बुद्धि और इंद्रियों से भी, अपने अंतःकरण की शुद्धि के लिए कर्म करते हैं।

In your life

Teaching a neighbour's child to read on Sunday mornings, do it without wanting to be seen or thanked. Work offered that way slowly clears out the self rather than feeding it.

Krishna describes how yogis on the path of action actually work: with body, mind, intellect and senses, but giving up attachment, and for ātma-śuddhi, the purifying of oneself.

The word kevalaiḥ, 'alone' or 'merely', is read by some with the senses only and by others with all four instruments, meaning action done through them without a sense of 'I'. In plain terms, the same tasks can be done for a different reason: to grow clearer inside rather than to gain.
