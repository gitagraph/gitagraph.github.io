Chapter 5 · Renunciation of Action

Chapter 5 · 29 verses

Renunciation of Action

कर्मसंन्यासयोग

Renouncing action and performing action both lead to the highest good, but action without attachment is better. The one who acts like a lotus leaf on water, untouched, sees the same Self in a scholar, a cow, an elephant and a dog.

कर्म का त्याग और कर्म का आचरण, दोनों परम कल्याण तक ले जाते हैं, पर अनासक्त कर्म श्रेष्ठ है। जो जल में कमल के पत्ते की तरह अछूता रहकर कर्म करता है, वह विद्वान, गाय, हाथी और कुत्ते में एक ही आत्मा देखता है।

  1. 5.1

    Krishna, you praise the renunciation of action, and then again the yoga of action. Tell me with certainty which one of the two is better.

  2. 5.2

    Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good, but of the two, the yoga of action is better than renouncing action.

  3. 5.3

    Know that person as a true renunciant at all times who neither hates nor craves; free of the pairs of opposites, mighty-armed one, they are easily released from bondage.

  4. 5.4

    Only the naive, not the wise, speak of the path of knowledge and the path of yoga as different. One who is truly settled in either one gains the fruit of both.

  5. 5.5

    The state reached by the followers of knowledge is reached by the followers of yoga as well. One who sees knowledge and yoga as one truly sees.

  6. 5.6

    But renunciation, mighty-armed one, is hard to attain without yoga; a sage who is steady in yoga reaches Brahman before long.

  7. 5.7

    Steady in yoga, pure in heart, master of mind and senses, whose self has become the self of all beings, such a person is not stained even while acting.

  8. 5.8

    One who is steady and knows the truth thinks, "I do nothing at all," even while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping and breathing…

  9. 5.9

    …while speaking, letting go, taking hold, opening and closing the eyes, holding firmly that it is only the senses moving among their objects.

  10. 5.10

    One who acts by placing all actions in Brahman and giving up attachment is not touched by sin, as a lotus leaf is not wetted by water.

  11. 5.11

    Giving up attachment, yogis act with the body, the mind, the intellect, and even the senses alone, for the purification of the self.

  12. 5.12

    One who is steady gives up the fruit of action and attains lasting peace; one who is not, driven by desire and attached to the fruit, is bound.

  13. 5.13

    Having given up all actions with the mind, the self-controlled embodied one dwells at ease in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor causing anything to be done.

  14. 5.14

    The Lord does not create agency for the world, nor actions, nor the link between action and its fruit; it is nature that carries all this on.

  15. 5.15

    The all-pervading one takes on no one's sin and no one's good deed either. Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and that is why beings are deluded.

  16. 5.16

    But for those whose ignorance has been destroyed by knowledge of the Self, that knowledge shines like the sun and reveals the Supreme.

  17. 5.17

    With their intellect on That, their self in That, their devotion in That and That as their final goal, their impurities shaken off by knowledge, they go to the state from which there is no return.

  18. 5.18

    The wise look with an equal eye on a learned and humble Brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste who eats dogs.

  19. 5.19

    Those whose mind rests in equality have conquered the cycle of birth here and now. Brahman is flawless and the same in all, so they are established in Brahman.

  20. 5.20

    With steady understanding and free of delusion, the knower of Brahman, established in Brahman, does not rejoice on getting what is pleasant or recoil on meeting what is unpleasant.

  21. 5.21

    One whose self is unattached to outer contacts finds the happiness that lies within. United with Brahman through yoga, that person enjoys a happiness that never fades.

  22. 5.22

    The pleasures born of contact with objects are only sources of sorrow; they have a beginning and an end, son of Kunti, and the wise take no delight in them.

  23. 5.23

    One who can withstand, right here before leaving the body, the surge that comes from desire and anger is steady in yoga; that person is happy.

  24. 5.24

    One whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, and whose light is within, that yogi becomes one with Brahman and attains the peace of Brahman.

  25. 5.25

    Seers whose faults are worn away, whose doubts are cut, who are self-controlled and devoted to the welfare of all beings, attain the peace of Brahman.

  26. 5.26

    For the self-disciplined who are free of desire and anger, who have mastered their thoughts and know the Self, the peace of Brahman is near on every side.

  27. 5.27

    Shutting out external contacts, fixing the gaze between the eyebrows, and evening out the in-breath and out-breath as they move within the nostrils…

  28. 5.28

    …the sage who has mastered the senses, mind and intellect, whose highest aim is liberation, and who is free of longing, fear and anger, is truly free forever.

  29. 5.29

    Knowing me as the one who receives all sacrifice and austerity, the great Lord of all the worlds and the friend of every being, one attains peace.
