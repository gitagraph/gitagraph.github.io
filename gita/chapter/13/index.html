Chapter 13 · The Field and its Knower

Chapter 13 · 34 verses

The Field and its Knower

क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञविभागयोग

The body is the field (kshetra); what knows it is the knower of the field (kshetrajna), and Krishna is the knower in all fields. Prakriti and purusha are both beginningless. Lists the qualities that make up knowledge.

शरीर क्षेत्र है; जो इसे जानता है वह क्षेत्रज्ञ है, और सभी क्षेत्रों में क्षेत्रज्ञ कृष्ण हैं। प्रकृति और पुरुष दोनों अनादि हैं। ज्ञान के लक्षणों की सूची भी दी गई है।

  1. 13.1

    This body, Kaunteya, is called the field; and the one who knows it is called the knower of the field by those who understand these matters.

  2. 13.2

    Know me too as the knower of the field in every field, Bharata. In my view, true knowledge is the knowledge of the field and its knower.

  3. 13.3

    Hear from me in brief what that field is, what it is like, what changes it undergoes and where each thing in it comes from; and who its knower is and what his powers are.

  4. 13.4

    Sages have sung of this in many ways and separately in varied Vedic hymns, and it is also set out conclusively, with reasoned argument, in the verses of the Brahma Sutras.

  5. 13.5

    The great elements, the ego-sense, the intellect and the unmanifest; the ten senses and the one mind, and the five domains of the senses…

  6. 13.6

    …desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the bodily aggregate, awareness and steadfastness — this, together with its changes, is the field described in brief.

  7. 13.7

    Freedom from pride and from pretence, non-violence, patience, straightforwardness, service of the teacher, purity, steadiness and self-restraint…

  8. 13.8

    …dispassion toward the objects of the senses, absence of ego, and seeing clearly the suffering and flaw in birth, death, old age and disease…

  9. 13.9

    …non-attachment, not being entangled with son, wife, home and the like, and constant evenness of mind whether what is wanted or unwanted comes about…

  10. 13.10

    …unwavering devotion to me through undivided yoga, a liking for solitary places, and no taste for crowds…

  11. 13.11

    …constancy in knowledge of the Self, and keeping in view the purpose of knowing the truth — this is declared to be knowledge; whatever is contrary to it is ignorance.

  12. 13.12

    I will now tell you what is to be known, knowing which one tastes immortality: the beginningless supreme Brahman, of which it is said neither that it is nor that it is not.

  13. 13.13

    Its hands and feet are everywhere, its eyes, heads and faces everywhere, its ears everywhere in the world; it stands enveloping all.

  14. 13.14

    It shines through the workings of all the senses yet is free of every sense; unattached, yet sustaining all; beyond the gunas, yet the experiencer of the gunas.

  15. 13.15

    It is outside and inside all beings, unmoving and also moving; being so subtle, it cannot be grasped; it is far away and also near.

  16. 13.16

    Undivided, it yet seems to stand divided among beings. It is to be known as the sustainer of beings, the one who devours them and the one who brings them forth.

  17. 13.17

    It is the light of all lights, said to lie beyond darkness. It is knowledge, the thing to be known, and what knowledge leads to, seated in the heart of everyone.

  18. 13.18

    So the field, knowledge and what is to be known have been described in brief. My devotee, understanding this, becomes fit to attain my state of being.

  19. 13.19

    Know that Prakriti and Purusha are both without beginning, and know that the changes of form and the gunas arise from Prakriti.

  20. 13.20

    Prakriti is said to be the cause behind effect, instrument and agency; Purusha is said to be the cause behind the experiencing of pleasure and pain.

  21. 13.21

    Dwelling in Prakriti, Purusha experiences the gunas born of Prakriti; its attachment to the gunas is the cause of its births in good and evil wombs.

  22. 13.22

    The supreme Purusha in this body is also called the witness, the one who consents, the sustainer, the experiencer, the great Lord and the supreme Self.

  23. 13.23

    One who knows Purusha and Prakriti together with the gunas in this way is not born again, however he may be living.

  24. 13.24

    Some see the Self within themselves by their own self through meditation; others by the path of Sankhya; and still others by the yoga of action.

  25. 13.25

    Still others, not knowing this themselves, worship after hearing it from others; they too, devoted to what they have heard, cross beyond death.

  26. 13.26

    Whatever being comes into existence, still or moving, know, best of the Bharatas, that it arises from the union of the field and the knower of the field.

  27. 13.27

    One who sees the supreme Lord abiding equally in all beings, the imperishable within the perishing — that person truly sees.

  28. 13.28

    Seeing the Lord present equally everywhere, he does not harm the Self by the self, and so he reaches the highest goal.

  29. 13.29

    One who sees that all actions are carried out entirely by Prakriti alone, and that the Self is not the doer, truly sees.

  30. 13.30

    When one perceives the separate existence of beings as resting in the One, and their spreading out as coming from that alone, one then attains Brahman.

  31. 13.31

    Being without beginning and beyond the gunas, this imperishable supreme Self, Kaunteya, though dwelling in the body, neither acts nor is tainted.

  32. 13.32

    Just as all-pervading space is not tainted because of its subtlety, so the Self, present throughout the body, is not tainted.

  33. 13.33

    Just as the one sun lights up this whole world, so the owner of the field lights up the whole field, Bharata.

  34. 13.34

    Those who see with the eye of knowledge the difference between the field and its knower, and how beings are freed from Prakriti, reach the Supreme.
