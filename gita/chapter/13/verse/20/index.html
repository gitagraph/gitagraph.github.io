Bhagavad Gita 13.20

Krishna

कार्यकरणकर्तृत्वे हेतुः प्रकृतिरुच्यते ।
पुरुषः सुखदुःखानां भोक्तृत्वे हेतुरुच्यते ॥ १३.२० ॥

kāryakaraṇakartṛtve hetuḥ prakṛtirucyate ।
puruṣaḥ sukhaduḥkhānāṃ bhoktṛtve heturucyate ॥

Prakriti is said to be the cause behind effect, instrument and agency; Purusha is said to be the cause behind the experiencing of pleasure and pain.

हिन्दीकार्य, करण और कर्तापन का कारण प्रकृति कही जाती है; और सुख-दुःख के भोगने का कारण पुरुष कहा जाता है।

Krishna divides the work between the two principles: Prakriti accounts for everything that is done, and Purusha for the experiencing of pleasure and pain.

Kārya is the effect, often read as the body; karaṇa, the instruments such as senses and mind; kartṛtva, the sense of being the doer. All three belong to nature. What belongs to Purusha is bhoktṛtva, being the one who undergoes the experience. Even the thought 'I am doing this' is, on this account, part of nature's working.
