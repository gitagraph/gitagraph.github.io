Bhagavad Gita 13.25

Krishna

अन्ये त्वेवमजानन्तः श्रुत्वान्येभ्य उपासते ।
तेऽपि चातितरन्त्येव मृत्युं श्रुतिपरायणाः ॥ १३.२५ ॥

anye tvevamajānantaḥ śrutvānyebhya upāsate ।
te'pi cātitarantyeva mṛtyuṃ śrutiparāyaṇāḥ ॥

Still others, not knowing this themselves, worship after hearing it from others; they too, devoted to what they have heard, cross beyond death.

हिन्दीकुछ और लोग, जो स्वयं इसे नहीं जानते, दूसरों से सुनकर उपासना करते हैं; वे भी सुनी हुई बात पर निष्ठा रखकर मृत्यु को पार कर जाते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Most of us first learned what matters from a grandmother or a teacher, not by discovering it ourselves. Holding sincerely to what you were taught is a real path, not a lesser one.

Krishna adds that even people who cannot see this for themselves, but worship after hearing it from others, also cross beyond death.

The key word is śruti-parāyaṇa, holding fast to what one has heard. The verse honours a very ordinary path: most people meet such ideas first through a teacher, a family or a text rather than through their own insight, and it says that sincere trust in what is heard can carry them across.
