Bhagavad Gita 13.28

Krishna

समं पश्यन्हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितमीश्वरम् ।
न हिनस्त्यात्मनात्मानं ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ १३.२८ ॥

samaṃ paśyanhi sarvatra samavasthitamīśvaram ।
na hinastyātmanātmānaṃ tato yāti parāṃ gatim ॥

Seeing the Lord present equally everywhere, he does not harm the Self by the self, and so he reaches the highest goal.

हिन्दीईश्वर को सब जगह समान रूप से स्थित देखते हुए वह अपने द्वारा अपनी आत्मा की हानि नहीं करता, और इसलिए परम गति को प्राप्त होता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Envying a cousin's success quietly hurts you more than it touches them. Seeing the same life in both of you takes the sting out of comparison.

Krishna says that one who sees the Lord equally present everywhere no longer harms himself, and so reaches the highest goal.

The striking phrase is na hinasty ātmanātmānam, 'he does not harm the Self by the self'. The thought seems to be that taking oneself to be only a body, and others as separate and lesser, is a quiet self-injury that keeps one bound. Seeing the same presence in all ends that injury, toward oneself and, by extension, toward others.
