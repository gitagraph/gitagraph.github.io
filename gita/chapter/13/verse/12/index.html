Bhagavad Gita 13.12

Krishna

ज्ञेयं यत्तत्प्रवक्ष्यामि यज्ज्ञात्वामृतमश्नुते ।
अनादि मत्परं ब्रह्म न सत्तन्नासदुच्यते ॥ १३.१२ ॥

jñeyaṃ yattatpravakṣyāmi yajjñātvāmṛtamaśnute ।
anādi matparaṃ brahma na sattannāsaducyate ॥

I will now tell you what is to be known, knowing which one tastes immortality: the beginningless supreme Brahman, of which it is said neither that it is nor that it is not.

हिन्दीअब मैं वह बताऊँगा जो जानने योग्य है, जिसे जानकर मनुष्य अमरता पाता है — वह अनादि परम ब्रह्म, जिसे न 'सत्' कहा जाता है, न 'असत्'।

Having described knowledge, Krishna turns to what is to be known, the beginningless supreme Brahman, and says that knowing it brings immortality.

The striking phrase is na sat tan nāsad ucyate: it is called neither sat, 'what is', nor asat, 'what is not'. Our words sort things into existing or not existing; Brahman, the verse suggests, fits neither box. Readings differ on one word: some divide it as anādimat param, others as anādi mat-param, 'having me as highest'.
