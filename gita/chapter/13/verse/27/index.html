Bhagavad Gita 13.27

Krishna

समं सर्वेषु भूतेषु तिष्ठन्तं परमेश्वरम् ।
विनश्यत्स्वविनश्यन्तं यः पश्यति स पश्यति ॥ १३.२७ ॥

samaṃ sarveṣu bhūteṣu tiṣṭhantaṃ parameśvaram ।
vinaśyatsvavinaśyantaṃ yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati ॥

One who sees the supreme Lord abiding equally in all beings, the imperishable within the perishing — that person truly sees.

हिन्दीजो परमेश्वर को सब प्राणियों में समान रूप से स्थित, और नाशवान वस्तुओं में अविनाशी रूप में देखता है — वही सच में देखता है।

In your life

At a funeral, everything reminds you that bodies pass. The verse asks you to see, too, what does not pass, in the person you lost and in everyone still in the room.

Krishna says that whoever sees the supreme Lord present equally in all beings, imperishable within everything that perishes, is the one who truly sees.

The closing words, yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati, 'whoever sees this, sees', imply that ordinary seeing misses something. Bodies come and go before our eyes; the verse asks us to notice what stays the same in all of them. Samam, 'equally', matters too: the Lord is no more present in the grand than in the humble.
