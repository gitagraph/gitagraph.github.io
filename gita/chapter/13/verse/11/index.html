Bhagavad Gita 13.11

Krishna

अध्यात्मज्ञाननित्यत्वं तत्त्वज्ञानार्थदर्शनम् ।
एतज्ज्ञानमिति प्रोक्तमज्ञानं यदतोऽन्यथा ॥ १३.११ ॥

adhyātmajñānanityatvaṃ tattvajñānārthadarśanam ।
etajjñānamiti proktamajñānaṃ yadato'nyathā ॥

↑ The sentence begins in 13.7

…constancy in knowledge of the Self, and keeping in view the purpose of knowing the truth — this is declared to be knowledge; whatever is contrary to it is ignorance.

हिन्दी…आत्म-ज्ञान में निरंतर लगे रहना, और तत्त्व-ज्ञान के उद्देश्य को सामने रखना — इसे ज्ञान कहा गया है; जो इससे विपरीत है, वह अज्ञान है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Keep your learning pointed at what it is for. Ten books on mindfulness are worth less than one honest look at why you snapped at your sister this morning.

Krishna ends his list of the qualities that together make up true knowledge (13.7–11) with steady attention to the Self and to the purpose of knowing the truth.

The key terms are adhyātma-jñāna, knowledge concerning the Self, and tattva, the truth of things. What stands out in the whole list is that 'knowledge' here means a way of living, with humility, patience and steadiness, rather than information; whatever runs against it the verse calls ignorance.
