Bhagavad Gita 13.15

Krishna

बहिरन्तश्च भूतानामचरं चरमेव च ।
सूक्ष्मत्वात्तदविज्ञेयं दूरस्थं चान्तिके च तत् ॥ १३.१५ ॥

bahirantaśca bhūtānāmacaraṃ carameva ca ।
sūkṣmatvāttadavijñeyaṃ dūrasthaṃ cāntike ca tat ॥

It is outside and inside all beings, unmoving and also moving; being so subtle, it cannot be grasped; it is far away and also near.

हिन्दीवह सब प्राणियों के बाहर भी है और भीतर भी, अचल भी है और चल भी; सूक्ष्म होने के कारण वह जाना नहीं जा सकता; वह दूर भी है और पास भी।

Krishna describes Brahman as both outside and inside all beings, unmoving and moving, far away and near.

The reason given for why it cannot be grasped is sūkṣmatva, subtlety: it is too fine to become an object that the senses or the mind can hold. The pairs make one point from different sides. What is everywhere is never simply 'over there', so it is as close as anything can be, and still missed because it is never in front of us.
