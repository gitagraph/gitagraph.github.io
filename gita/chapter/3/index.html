Chapter 3 · Karma Yoga

Chapter 3 · 43 verses

Karma Yoga

कर्मयोग

Why act at all, if knowledge is higher? Because no one can be without action even for a moment. Work done as yajna (offering) does not bind. The wise act to hold the world together (lokasangraha). Desire and anger are named as the enemy.

यदि ज्ञान श्रेष्ठ है तो कर्म क्यों करें? क्योंकि कोई क्षण भर भी बिना कर्म के नहीं रह सकता। यज्ञ (अर्पण) के रूप में किया गया कर्म बाँधता नहीं। ज्ञानी लोकसंग्रह, यानी संसार को सँभाले रखने, के लिए कर्म करते हैं। काम और क्रोध को शत्रु बताया गया है।

  1. 3.1

    Janardana, if you hold that understanding is higher than action, then why, Keshava, do you push me into this terrible deed?

  2. 3.2

    Your words seem to pull in two directions, and they leave my understanding confused. Tell me plainly the one path, settled for certain, by which I may reach the highest good.

  3. 3.3

    Sinless one, long ago I taught that in this world there are two ways of steady practice: the path of knowledge for those given to reflection, and the path of action for those given to yoga.

  4. 3.4

    A person does not reach freedom from action merely by not starting any work, nor does one attain perfection by renunciation alone.

  5. 3.5

    No one can stay without acting even for a moment; every being is driven to act, whether it wants to or not, by the qualities (gunas) born of nature (Prakriti).

  6. 3.6

    One who holds back the organs of action but sits dwelling in the mind on the objects of the senses is deluded, and is called a pretender.

  7. 3.7

    But one who reins in the senses with the mind, Arjuna, and without attachment takes up the yoga of action through the organs of action, stands far above.

  8. 3.8

    Do the work that is yours to do, for action is better than inaction; without acting you could not even keep your body going.

  9. 3.9

    This world is bound by action, except action done as sacrifice. So, son of Kunti, act for that purpose, free of attachment.

  10. 3.10

    In the beginning, the Lord of creatures brought forth beings together with sacrifice and said: "Through this you will multiply; let this be the cow that yields all you wish for."

  11. 3.11

    "Nourish the gods with this, and let the gods nourish you. Sustaining one another, you will reach the highest good."

  12. 3.12

    "Pleased by sacrifice, the gods will give you the enjoyments you desire." One who enjoys their gifts without offering anything back to them is nothing but a thief.

  13. 3.13

    The good, who eat what remains after sacrifice, are released from all faults; but the wicked who cook only for themselves eat their own sin.

  14. 3.14

    Beings arise from food, food comes from rain, rain comes from sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of action.

  15. 3.15

    Know that action springs from the Veda, and the Veda springs from the Imperishable. So the all-pervading Brahman is always present in sacrifice.

  16. 3.16

    Partha, whoever in this world does not keep turning the wheel set in motion this way, living in sin and taking delight only in the senses, lives in vain.

  17. 3.17

    But for the person whose joy is in the Self alone, who is fulfilled in the Self and content in the Self, there remains no duty to perform.

  18. 3.18

    Such a one gains nothing here by acting and loses nothing by not acting, and depends on no being for any purpose.

  19. 3.19

    So always do the work that must be done, without attachment; by acting unattached a person reaches the Supreme.

  20. 3.20

    Janaka and others reached perfection through action alone. And looking to the welfare of the world too, you should act.

  21. 3.21

    Whatever a leading person does, others do the same; whatever standard such a person sets, the world follows.

  22. 3.22

    Partha, there is nothing in the three worlds that I must do, nothing I lack that I need to gain; and yet I go on acting.

  23. 3.23

    For if I ever stopped acting tirelessly, Partha, people everywhere would follow my path.

  24. 3.24

    If I did not work, these worlds would fall into ruin; I would be the cause of confusion and would bring these beings to destruction.

  25. 3.25

    Bharata, just as the unwise act out of attachment to their work, the wise should act without attachment, wishing to hold the world together.

  26. 3.26

    The wise should not unsettle the minds of the ignorant who are attached to action. Acting with discipline themselves, they should lead others to take joy in all their work.

  27. 3.27

    All actions are carried out entirely by the qualities of nature, but one whose mind is clouded by ego thinks, "I am the doer."

  28. 3.28

    But one who knows the truth about how the qualities and their actions are divided, mighty-armed one, understands that it is qualities acting upon qualities, and does not become attached.

  29. 3.29

    Those deluded by the qualities of nature cling to the workings of those qualities. One who knows the whole should not unsettle such slow people who know only in part.

  30. 3.30

    Hand over all your actions to me, with your mind fixed on the Self; free of craving and of the sense of "mine", and with your fever gone, fight.

  31. 3.31

    Those who always follow this teaching of mine with faith and without finding fault are also released from the bonds of action.

  32. 3.32

    But those who scoff at it and do not follow my teaching, know them to be confused about all knowledge, senseless and lost.

  33. 3.33

    Even a knowing person acts in keeping with their own nature. All beings follow their nature; what can forcing it achieve?

  34. 3.34

    Liking and disliking are firmly seated in each sense with regard to its objects. Do not fall under their sway, for they are enemies on one's path.

  35. 3.35

    Better one's own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Better to die in one's own dharma; another's dharma brings danger.

  36. 3.36

    Then what drives a person to do wrong, Varshneya, even against their own wish, as if pushed into it by force?

  37. 3.37

    It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of passion; it devours without end and is the great evil. Know it as the enemy here.

  38. 3.38

    As fire is hidden by smoke, as a mirror by dust, and as an embryo by its membrane, so is this covered by desire.

  39. 3.39

    Son of Kunti, knowledge is veiled by this constant enemy of the wise, which takes the form of desire, never satisfied, like fire.

  40. 3.40

    The senses, the mind and the intellect are said to be its dwelling. Through them it veils knowledge and deludes the embodied one.

  41. 3.41

    So, best of the Bharatas, first bring the senses under control, and then destroy this evil thing that ruins both knowledge and realisation.

  42. 3.42

    The senses are called higher than the body; higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the intellect; and higher than the intellect is the Self.

  43. 3.43

    Knowing in this way what lies beyond the intellect, steady yourself by the Self, mighty-armed one, and kill this enemy in the form of desire, so hard to overcome.
