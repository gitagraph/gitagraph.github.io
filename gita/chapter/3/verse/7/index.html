Bhagavad Gita 3.7

Krishna

यस्त्विन्द्रियाणि मनसा नियम्यारभतेऽर्जुन ।
कर्मेन्द्रियैः कर्मयोगमसक्तः स विशिष्यते ॥ ३.७ ॥

yastvindriyāṇi manasā niyamyārabhate'rjuna ।
karmendriyaiḥ karmayogamasaktaḥ sa viśiṣyate ॥

But one who reins in the senses with the mind, Arjuna, and without attachment takes up the yoga of action through the organs of action, stands far above.

हिन्दीपरंतु हे अर्जुन, जो मन से इंद्रियों को वश में करके, आसक्ति के बिना कर्मेंद्रियों द्वारा कर्मयोग करता है, वह श्रेष्ठ है।

In your life

When training for a long run, let your mind, not every passing twinge, decide when to stop; then let your legs simply do the work without fretting over the finishing time.

Krishna sets out the alternative to the pretender of 3.6: one who governs the senses through the mind and, without attachment, takes up work through the organs of action stands far higher.

The key term is karmayoga, the yoga of action, defined here by two marks, inner control and asakti, freedom from clinging. Plainly, the ideal is not to stop acting but to act fully while the mind stays steady and unhooked.
