Bhagavad Gita 3.40

Krishna

इन्द्रियाणि मनो बुद्धिरस्याधिष्ठानमुच्यते ।
एतैर्विमोहयत्येष ज्ञानमावृत्य देहिनम् ॥ ३.४० ॥

indriyāṇi mano buddhirasyādhiṣṭhānamucyate ।
etairvimohayatyeṣa jñānamāvṛtya dehinam ॥

The senses, the mind and the intellect are said to be its dwelling. Through them it veils knowledge and deludes the embodied one.

हिन्दीइंद्रियाँ, मन और बुद्धि इसके रहने के स्थान कहे जाते हैं। इन्हीं के द्वारा यह ज्ञान को ढककर देहधारी जीव को मोह में डाल देता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

At a party, the eyes want the second drink, the mind replays how good it felt, and the intellect supplies 'just this once'. Notice how desire works through all three.

Krishna identifies where desire takes up residence: in the senses, the mind and the intellect (buddhi).

The word adhiṣṭhāna means a seat or base of operations, and from there desire veils knowledge and leaves the embodied one (dehin) confused. Naming these three seats prepares the remedy in 3.41, which starts with the senses. It is a practical map: desire is fought not in the abstract but where it actually works in us.
