Bhagavad Gita 3.14

Krishna

अन्नाद्भवन्ति भूतानि पर्जन्यादन्नसम्भवः ।
यज्ञाद्भवति पर्जन्यो यज्ञः कर्मसमुद्भवः ॥ ३.१४ ॥

annādbhavanti bhūtāni parjanyādannasambhavaḥ ।
yajñādbhavati parjanyo yajñaḥ karmasamudbhavaḥ ॥

Beings arise from food, food comes from rain, rain comes from sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of action.

हिन्दीसब प्राणी अन्न से उत्पन्न होते हैं, अन्न वर्षा से होता है, वर्षा यज्ञ से होती है और यज्ञ कर्म से उत्पन्न होता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

The rice on your plate came from a farmer, the rain, the truck driver and the shopkeeper. Remembering that chain, even once a day, makes it harder to take anything for granted.

Krishna traces a chain that ties every creature to action: beings come from food, food from rain, rain from sacrifice, and sacrifice from action.

The key word is parjanya, the rain-cloud, here the link between human effort and the earth's fertility. Whatever one makes of rain coming from ritual, the aim is clear: no one stands outside this cycle of dependence, and action keeps it going. The chain continues in the next verse.
