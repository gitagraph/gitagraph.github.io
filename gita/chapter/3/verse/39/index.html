Bhagavad Gita 3.39

Krishna

आवृतं ज्ञानमेतेन ज्ञानिनो नित्यवैरिणा ।
कामरूपेण कौन्तेय दुष्पूरेणानलेन च ॥ ३.३९ ॥

āvṛtaṃ jñānametena jñānino nityavairiṇā ।
kāmarūpeṇa kaunteya duṣpūreṇānalena ca ॥

Son of Kunti, knowledge is veiled by this constant enemy of the wise, which takes the form of desire, never satisfied, like fire.

हिन्दीहे कुंतीपुत्र, ज्ञानियों के इस सदा के शत्रु, काम रूपी कभी न तृप्त होने वाली आग ने ज्ञान को ढक रखा है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

The new gadget was meant to settle it, and within weeks the next model looks better. Wanting grows like fire with every log you feed it, so stop feeding it so often.

Krishna now names what desire covers, knowledge itself, and calls desire the wise person's nityavairin, constant enemy.

Two words sharpen the picture: duṣpūra, 'hard to fill', and anala, fire, which grows the more fuel it is fed. The observation is a familiar one: satisfying a craving rarely ends it and often feeds it, which is why the Gita treats desire as something to understand and master rather than to appease.
