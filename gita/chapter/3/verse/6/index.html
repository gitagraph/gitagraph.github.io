Bhagavad Gita 3.6

Krishna

कर्मेन्द्रियाणि संयम्य य आस्ते मनसा स्मरन् ।
इन्द्रियार्थान्विमूढात्मा मिथ्याचारः स उच्यते ॥ ३.६ ॥

karmendriyāṇi saṃyamya ya āste manasā smaran ।
indriyārthānvimūḍhātmā mithyācāraḥ sa ucyate ॥

One who holds back the organs of action but sits dwelling in the mind on the objects of the senses is deluded, and is called a pretender.

हिन्दीजो कर्मेंद्रियों को रोककर बैठा रहता है पर मन ही मन इंद्रियों के विषयों का चिंतन करता रहता है, वह मूढ़ व्यक्ति पाखंडी कहलाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Skipping dessert while thinking about it all evening isn't really restraint. If the mind is still at the table, admit it, and work on the wanting rather than just the plate.

Krishna warns against outward restraint that hides inner indulgence: someone who holds the body back but keeps dwelling on sense objects in the mind is deluded and only pretending.

The key word is mithyācāra, 'false conduct', rendered here as a pretender. For a reader, sitting still proves little; what the mind keeps returning to matters more than what the hands and feet are doing, an idea first traced in 2.62.
