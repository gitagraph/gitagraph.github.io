Bhagavad Gita 3.43

Krishna

एवं बुद्धेः परं बुद्ध्वा संस्तभ्यात्मानमात्मना ।
जहि शत्रुं महाबाहो कामरूपं दुरासदम् ॥ ३.४३ ॥

evaṃ buddheḥ paraṃ buddhvā saṃstabhyātmānamātmanā ।
jahi śatruṃ mahābāho kāmarūpaṃ durāsadam ॥

Knowing in this way what lies beyond the intellect, steady yourself by the Self, mighty-armed one, and kill this enemy in the form of desire, so hard to overcome.

हिन्दीइस प्रकार बुद्धि से परे आत्मा को जानकर, अपने आप को आत्मा के बल से स्थिर करके, हे महाबाहो, इस कामरूपी कठिन शत्रु को मार डालो।

Krishna closes the third chapter by turning the ladder of 3.42 into a call to act: knowing what lies beyond the intellect, Arjuna should steady himself and kill the enemy that takes the form of desire.

The phrase saṃstabhya ātmānam ātmanā uses ātman twice, read here as steadying oneself, mind and senses, by the Self. Desire is durāsada, 'hard to get near', yet the verse assumes it can be overcome by someone who knows where they truly stand.
