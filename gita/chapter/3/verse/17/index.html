Bhagavad Gita 3.17

Krishna

यस्त्वात्मरतिरेव स्यादात्मतृप्तश्च मानवः ।
आत्मन्येव च सन्तुष्टस्तस्य कार्यं न विद्यते ॥ ३.१७ ॥

yastvātmaratireva syādātmatṛptaśca mānavaḥ ।
ātmanyeva ca santuṣṭastasya kāryaṃ na vidyate ॥

But for the person whose joy is in the Self alone, who is fulfilled in the Self and content in the Self, there remains no duty to perform.

हिन्दीपरंतु जो मनुष्य आत्मा में ही रमता है, आत्मा में ही तृप्त है और आत्मा में ही संतुष्ट है, उसके लिए कोई कर्तव्य शेष नहीं रहता।

In your life

Some people are so at ease within that they no longer need the next achievement to feel whole. You've likely met one; notice how little they chase, and how much they still give.

Krishna names an exception to the duty to act: a person whose joy, fulfilment and contentment lie in the Self alone has no duty left to perform.

Three words build the picture, rati, delight, tṛpta, satisfied, and santuṣṭa, content, all centred on ātman. Such a person needs nothing from outside; yet, as the following verses show, Krishna still urges action for the sake of the world.
