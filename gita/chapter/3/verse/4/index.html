Bhagavad Gita 3.4

Krishna

न कर्मणामनारम्भान्नैष्कर्म्यं पुरुषोऽश्नुते ।
न च संन्यसनादेव सिद्धिं समधिगच्छति ॥ ३.४ ॥

na karmaṇāmanārambhānnaiṣkarmyaṃ puruṣo'śnute ।
na ca saṃnyasanādeva siddhiṃ samadhigacchati ॥

A person does not reach freedom from action merely by not starting any work, nor does one attain perfection by renunciation alone.

हिन्दीकर्मों को आरंभ न करने से मनुष्य निष्कर्मता को नहीं पाता, और केवल संन्यास से ही उसे सिद्धि नहीं मिलती।

In your life

Moving to the hills to escape a stressful city life won't by itself bring peace, and neither will simply refusing to begin anything. The restlessness tends to pack its bags and come along.

Krishna begins his answer by ruling out an easy escape: freedom from action does not come from simply not starting any work, nor perfection from renunciation alone.

The key word is naiṣkarmya, a state in which action no longer binds, which is different from mere inactivity. Put simply, stepping back from one's duties does not by itself free anyone; what matters is the inner state in which one acts.
