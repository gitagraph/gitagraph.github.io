Bhagavad Gita 3.38

Krishna

धूमेनाव्रियते वह्निर्यथादर्शो मलेन च ।
यथोल्बेनावृतो गर्भस्तथा तेनेदमावृतम् ॥ ३.३८ ॥

dhūmenāvriyate vahniryathādarśo malena ca ।
yatholbenāvṛto garbhastathā tenedamāvṛtam ॥

As fire is hidden by smoke, as a mirror by dust, and as an embryo by its membrane, so is this covered by desire.

हिन्दीजैसे धुएँ से आग ढक जाती है, धूल से दर्पण ढक जाता है और गर्भाशय की झिल्ली से गर्भ ढका रहता है, वैसे ही इस काम से यह ज्ञान ढका रहता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you badly want a deal or someone's approval, you lose sight of what you would normally know. Your good sense isn't gone, only covered, like a mirror under dust; let the wanting settle and it shows again.

Krishna uses three images to show how desire, named in 3.37, hides what we know: fire under smoke, a mirror under dust, an embryo inside its membrane.

The verb āvṛ, 'to cover', carries the point: what lies beneath is not destroyed, only obscured, and 3.39 names it as knowledge. Traditionally, the three are read as layers of covering from the lightest to the hardest to remove; the hopeful side is that what is covered can still be uncovered.
