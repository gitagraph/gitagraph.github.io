Bhagavad Gita 3.42

Krishna

इन्द्रियाणि पराण्याहुरिन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनः ।
मनसस्तु परा बुद्धिर्यो बुद्धेः परतस्तु सः ॥ ३.४२ ॥

indriyāṇi parāṇyāhurindriyebhyaḥ paraṃ manaḥ ।
manasastu parā buddhiryo buddheḥ paratastu saḥ ॥

The senses are called higher than the body; higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the intellect; and higher than the intellect is the Self.

हिन्दीकहते हैं कि इंद्रियाँ शरीर से श्रेष्ठ हैं, इंद्रियों से श्रेष्ठ मन है, मन से श्रेष्ठ बुद्धि है, और जो बुद्धि से भी परे है वह आत्मा है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When a craving takes hold, remember the order: senses, then mind, then judgement, then what you truly are. Lean on the higher one each time a lower one starts to drag you.

To show Arjuna that desire can be mastered, Krishna sets out a ladder of what is higher and subtler within a person: the senses, then the mind (manas), then the intellect (buddhi), and beyond the intellect 'that one' (saḥ).

The verse leaves 'that' unnamed, but it is generally taken to be the Self. The ladder matters because desire lives on the lower rungs (3.40), while something in us stands above all of them.
