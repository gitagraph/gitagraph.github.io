Bhagavad Gita 3.28

Krishna

तत्त्ववित्तु महाबाहो गुणकर्मविभागयोः ।
गुणा गुणेषु वर्तन्त इति मत्वा न सज्जते ॥ ३.२८ ॥

tattvavittu mahābāho guṇakarmavibhāgayoḥ ।
guṇā guṇeṣu vartanta iti matvā na sajjate ॥

But one who knows the truth about how the qualities and their actions are divided, mighty-armed one, understands that it is qualities acting upon qualities, and does not become attached.

हिन्दीपरंतु हे महाबाहो, जो गुणों और कर्मों के विभाग का तत्त्व जानता है, वह यह समझकर कि "गुण ही गुणों में बरत रहे हैं", उनमें आसक्त नहीं होता।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When a friend snaps at you in a hard week, it is often tiredness and worry meeting your words, not a verdict on you. Seeing it that way, you needn't take it home.

Krishna explains why a person who sees clearly can act without becoming attached: such a person knows it is only 'qualities acting upon qualities'.

The guṇas are the three strands of nature (prakṛti) named in 3.27, and the phrase is often read as the senses and mind, themselves made of the guṇas, meeting objects made of the same stuff. Seen this way, events look like a play of forces rather than something 'I' alone drive, and the grip of attachment loosens.
