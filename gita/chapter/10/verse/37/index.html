Bhagavad Gita 10.37

Krishna

वृष्णीनां वासुदेवोऽस्मि पाण्डवानां धनंजयः ।
मुनीनामप्यहं व्यासः कवीनामुशना कविः ॥ १०.३७ ॥

vṛṣṇīnāṃ vāsudevo'smi pāṇḍavānāṃ dhanaṃjayaḥ ।
munīnāmapyahaṃ vyāsaḥ kavīnāmuśanā kaviḥ ॥

Among the Vrishnis I am Vasudeva (Krishna); among the Pandavas, Dhananjaya (Arjuna); among sages I am Vyasa; and among poets, the poet Ushana (Shukracharya).

हिन्दीवृष्णिवंशियों में मैं वासुदेव हूँ, पांडवों में धनंजय हूँ, मुनियों में व्यास हूँ और कवियों में कवि उशना (शुक्राचार्य) हूँ।

Krishna names himself as Vasudeva among the Vrishnis, Dhananjaya among the Pandavas, Vyasa among sages and Ushana among seer-poets.

From the Mahabharata: the Vrishnis are Krishna's own clan, so Vasudeva is Krishna himself, and Dhananjaya is Arjuna, the listener, so two of the four are present in this very conversation. Traditionally Vyasa is the sage credited with composing the epic, and Ushana is Shukracharya, the wise teacher of the asuras. Kavi means a seer-poet, not just a versifier.
