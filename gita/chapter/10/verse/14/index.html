Bhagavad Gita 10.14

Arjuna

सर्वमेतदृतं मन्ये यन्मां वदसि केशव ।
न हि ते भगवन्व्यक्तिं विदुर्देवा न दानवाः ॥ १०.१४ ॥

sarvametadṛtaṃ manye yanmāṃ vadasi keśava ।
na hi te bhagavanvyaktiṃ vidurdevā na dānavāḥ ॥

All that you tell me, Keshava, I accept as true. Indeed, Lord, neither the gods nor the demons understand your manifestation.

हिन्दीहे केशव, आप मुझसे जो कुछ कहते हैं, उस सबको मैं सत्य मानता हूँ। हे भगवन, आपके प्रकट रूप को न देवता जानते हैं, न दानव।

Arjuna declares that he accepts as true everything Krishna has told him, and adds that neither gods nor demons understand his manifestation.

The key word is vyakti, 'manifestation', the way he becomes visible and takes form; Arjuna echoes Krishna's own words in 10.2 that the gods do not know his origin. Traditionally, the name Keshava is read as 'the one with beautiful hair'. If no one else can know, Krishna must tell, and the request follows.
