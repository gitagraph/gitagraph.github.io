Bhagavad Gita 10.27

Krishna

उच्चैःश्रवसमश्वानां विद्धि माममृतोद्भवम् ।
ऐरावतं गजेन्द्राणां नराणां च नराधिपम् ॥ १०.२७ ॥

uccaiḥśravasamaśvānāṃ viddhi māmamṛtodbhavam ।
airāvataṃ gajendrāṇāṃ narāṇāṃ ca narādhipam ॥

Among horses know me as Uchchaihshravas, born of the churning for nectar; among lordly elephants, Airavata; and among men, the king.

हिन्दीघोड़ों में अमृत-मंथन से उत्पन्न उच्चैःश्रवा मुझे जानो, श्रेष्ठ हाथियों में ऐरावत और मनुष्यों में राजा मुझे ही जानो।

Krishna tells Arjuna to know him as Uchchaihshravas among horses, Airavata among lordly elephants, and the king among men.

The key word is amṛtodbhava, 'born of the nectar', which the site renders as born of the churning for nectar. Traditionally, both the horse and the elephant rose from the ocean when gods and demons churned it, and Airavata became Indra's mount. Placing the king beside them treats rightful rule among people as one more form of his excellence.
