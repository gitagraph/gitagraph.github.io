Bhagavad Gita 10.5

Krishna

अहिंसा समता तुष्टिस्तपो दानं यशोऽयशः ।
भवन्ति भावा भूतानां मत्त एव पृथग्विधाः ॥ १०.५ ॥

ahiṃsā samatā tuṣṭistapo dānaṃ yaśo'yaśaḥ ।
bhavanti bhāvā bhūtānāṃ matta eva pṛthagvidhāḥ ॥

non-violence, evenness, contentment, austerity, generosity, fame and disgrace: all these varied states of beings arise from me alone.

हिन्दीअहिंसा, समता, संतोष, तप, दान, यश और अपयश: प्राणियों के ये अलग-अलग भाव मुझसे ही उत्पन्न होते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Fame and disgrace, contentment and fear all arise from the same source. When a colleague's praise or a stranger's insult comes, neither is the whole story of you.

Krishna completes his list of the inner states of beings, from non-violence to fame and disgrace, and says that all of them arise from him alone.

The key word is bhāva, a state or disposition of a being: virtues such as ahiṃsā, non-violence, stand in the same line as fame and its loss, ayaśas. For a reader, the verse widens the idea of the divine beyond the pleasant: whatever we pass through, honour or disgrace, is placed within one reality, not outside it.
