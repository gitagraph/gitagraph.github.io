Chapter 17 · Three Kinds of FaithVerse 28 of 28

Bhagavad Gita 17.28

Krishna

अश्रद्धया हुतं दत्तं तपस्तप्तं कृतं च यत् ।
असदित्युच्यते पार्थ न च तत्प्रेत्य नो इह ॥ १७.२८ ॥

aśraddhayā hutaṃ dattaṃ tapastaptaṃ kṛtaṃ ca yat ।
asadityucyate pārtha na ca tatpretya no iha ॥

Whatever is offered, given, practised as austerity or done without faith is called 'asat', Partha; it is of no value, either after death or here.

हिन्दीहे पार्थ, बिना श्रद्धा के जो हवन किया जाए, जो दान दिया जाए, जो तप किया जाए या जो भी कर्म किया जाए, वह 'असत्' कहलाता है; उसका न मरने के बाद कोई फल है, न यहाँ।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A donation given grudgingly, a visit made without any care, a fast kept without any meaning: done without faith, they leave little behind. If you do it, mean it.

Krishna ends the chapter by saying that whatever is offered, given, endured as austerity or done without faith is called 'asat', and is of no value either here or after death.

The key word is asat, 'not-real', the opposite of the sat just explained. It answers Arjuna's opening question (17.1) from the other side: faith is what gives religious acts their worth, and without it even correct forms are empty.
