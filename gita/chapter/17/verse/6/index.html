Bhagavad Gita 17.6

Krishna

कर्षयन्तः शरीरस्थं भूतग्राममचेतसः ।
मां चैवान्तःशरीरस्थं तान्विद्ध्यासुरनिश्चयान् ॥ १७.६ ॥

karṣayantaḥ śarīrasthaṃ bhūtagrāmamacetasaḥ ।
māṃ caivāntaḥśarīrasthaṃ tānviddhyāsuraniścayān ॥

↑ The sentence begins in 17.5

…senselessly tormenting the elements that make up the body, and me as well, who dwell within the body: know them to be of demonic resolve.

हिन्दी…वे विवेकहीन लोग शरीर में स्थित तत्त्वों को और शरीर के भीतर रहने वाले मुझको भी कष्ट देते हैं। ऐसे लोगों को आसुरी निश्चय वाला जानो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Crash-fasting to look thin for a function, or punishing yourself with workouts after a lapse, isn't the discipline the Gita praises. It calls tormenting the body ego, because the body houses something that deserves care.

Krishna says that people who torment their bodies through unsanctioned, ego-driven austerities also torment him, the one who dwells within the body, and he calls their resolve āsura, 'demonic'.

This completes the previous verse. The key phrase is antaḥ-śarīra-stha, 'dwelling inside the body': harming the body is treated as harming the divine presence within it. Plainly, the Gita rejects self-torture as a spiritual achievement; the body is to be disciplined, not abused.
