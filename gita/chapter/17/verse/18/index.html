Bhagavad Gita 17.18

Krishna

सत्कारमानपूजार्थं तपो दम्भेन चैव यत् ।
क्रियते तदिह प्रोक्तं राजसं चलमध्रुवम् ॥ १७.१८ ॥

satkāramānapūjārthaṃ tapo dambhena caiva yat ।
kriyate tadiha proktaṃ rājasaṃ calamadhruvam ॥

Austerity performed to gain respect, honour and reverence, and done with hypocrisy, is called rajasic here; it is unsteady and does not last.

हिन्दीजो तप सत्कार, मान और पूजा पाने के लिए और पाखंड के साथ किया जाता है, उसे यहाँ राजसिक कहा गया है; वह चंचल और अस्थायी होता है।

In your life

A gym routine kept up for compliments from colleagues usually ends when the compliments do. Discipline done to be admired is the shaky kind that doesn't last.

Krishna says that austerity practised to win respect, honour and reverence, and done for show, is rajasic, and that it is unsteady and does not last.

The key words are calam adhruvam, 'shaky and impermanent'. The reasoning is practical: discipline that depends on an audience lasts only as long as the admiration does, and tends to fall away once no one is watching.
