Bhagavad Gita 17.21

Krishna

यत्तु प्रत्युपकारार्थं फलमुद्दिश्य वा पुनः ।
दीयते च परिक्लिष्टं तद्दानं राजसं स्मृतम् ॥ १७.२१ ॥

yattu pratyupakārārthaṃ phalamuddiśya vā punaḥ ।
dīyate ca parikliṣṭaṃ taddānaṃ rājasaṃ smṛtam ॥

But a gift given to get something back, or with a reward in view, or given grudgingly, is held to be rajasic.

हिन्दीपर जो दान बदले में कुछ पाने के लिए, या फल को ध्यान में रखकर, या मन मारकर दिया जाता है, वह राजसिक दान माना गया है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A Diwali gift for the boss, timed just before appraisals, is closer to a purchase than a gift. The verse looks less at the amount than at what you hoped to get back.

Krishna says that a gift is rajasic when it is given to get something back, with a reward in view, or grudgingly.

The key word is parikliṣṭa, 'given with strain', handing something over while inwardly resenting it. Set beside the previous verse, the difference is not the amount given but the inner bookkeeping: a gift that is really a transaction, or a gift that hurts to let go.
