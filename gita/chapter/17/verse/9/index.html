Bhagavad Gita 17.9

Krishna

कट्वम्ललवणात्युष्णतीक्ष्णरूक्षविदाहिनः ।
आहारा राजसस्येष्टा दुःखशोकामयप्रदाः ॥ १७.९ ॥

kaṭvamlalavaṇātyuṣṇatīkṣṇarūkṣavidāhinaḥ ।
āhārā rājasasyeṣṭā duḥkhaśokāmayapradāḥ ॥

Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, overly hot, pungent, dry and burning are favoured by rajasic people; they bring pain, grief and illness.

हिन्दीकड़वे, खट्टे, नमकीन, बहुत गरम, तीखे, रूखे और जलन पैदा करने वाले भोजन राजसिक लोगों को अच्छे लगते हैं; ये दुःख, शोक और रोग देते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

If you notice yourself always reaching for the spiciest, saltiest, most fiery dish, ask what mood it is feeding. Often the craving says more about restlessness than hunger.

Krishna describes the food rajasic people favour: bitter, sour, salty, overly hot, pungent, dry and burning, food that brings pain, grief and illness.

The key word is vidāhin, 'burning', food that leaves a burning feeling behind. The pattern is intensity, food chosen for its jolt rather than its benefit, which mirrors a rajasic temperament that chases stimulation and pays for it afterwards in discomfort.
