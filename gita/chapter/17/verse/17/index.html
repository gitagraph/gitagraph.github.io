Bhagavad Gita 17.17

Krishna

श्रद्धया परया तप्तं तपस्तत्त्रिविधं नरैः ।
अफलाकाङ्क्षिभिर्युक्तैः सात्त्विकं परिचक्षते ॥ १७.१७ ॥

śraddhayā parayā taptaṃ tapastattrividhaṃ naraiḥ ।
aphalākāṅkṣibhiryuktaiḥ sāttvikaṃ paricakṣate ॥

When this threefold austerity is practised with the highest faith by disciplined people who seek no reward, it is called sattvic.

हिन्दीजब यह तीन प्रकार का तप फल की इच्छा न रखने वाले, संयमी लोग परम श्रद्धा के साथ करते हैं, तो उसे सात्त्विक कहा जाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Keeping your word, speaking kindly and staying calm, done with real faith and no audience in mind, is the steadiest kind of discipline there is.

Krishna says that the threefold austerity of body, speech and mind is sattvic when disciplined people practise it with the highest faith and seek no reward.

The key word is yukta, 'steady' or 'disciplined', one whose effort is collected rather than erratic. The practices of 17.14–16 can be carried out in different spirits; it is faith and the absence of a hoped-for payoff that make them sattvic.
