Chapter 17 · Three Kinds of Faith

Chapter 17 · 28 verses

Three Kinds of Faith

श्रद्धात्रयविभागयोग

A person is made of their faith. Faith, food, sacrifice, austerity and charity each come in sattvic, rajasic and tamasic forms. Om Tat Sat is the threefold name of Brahman.

मनुष्य श्रद्धामय है। श्रद्धा, आहार, यज्ञ, तप और दान, सबके सात्त्विक, राजसिक और तामसिक रूप होते हैं। ॐ तत् सत् ब्रह्म का त्रिविध नाम है।

  1. 17.1

    Krishna, what about those who set aside the rules of scripture yet worship with faith? What is their standing: sattva, rajas or tamas?

  2. 17.2

    The faith of embodied beings is of three kinds, born of each one's own nature: sattvic, rajasic and tamasic. Hear about it.

  3. 17.3

    Everyone's faith takes the shape of their inner nature, Bharata. A person is made of faith: whatever one's faith is, that is what one is.

  4. 17.4

    Sattvic people worship the gods; rajasic people worship yakshas and rakshasas; and tamasic people worship ghosts and hosts of spirits.

  5. 17.5

    Those people who undergo harsh austerities not sanctioned by scripture, driven by hypocrisy and ego, and carried along by the force of desire and attachment…

  6. 17.6

    …senselessly tormenting the elements that make up the body, and me as well, who dwell within the body: know them to be of demonic resolve.

  7. 17.7

    Even the food each person likes is of three kinds, and so are sacrifice, austerity and charity. Hear how they differ.

  8. 17.8

    Foods that increase life, vitality, strength, health, happiness and contentment, and that are tasty, rich, substantial and pleasing to the heart, are dear to sattvic people.

  9. 17.9

    Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, overly hot, pungent, dry and burning are favoured by rajasic people; they bring pain, grief and illness.

  10. 17.10

    Food that is stale, tasteless, putrid, kept overnight, left over by others, or impure is dear to tamasic people.

  11. 17.11

    Sacrifice offered according to scriptural rule by those who seek no reward, with the mind settled on the thought that it simply ought to be done, is sattvic.

  12. 17.12

    But sacrifice performed with an eye on the reward, or merely for show, know that to be rajasic, best of the Bharatas.

  13. 17.13

    Sacrifice that ignores the rules, distributes no food, lacks mantras, gives no fee to the priests and is devoid of faith is called tamasic.

  14. 17.14

    Honouring the gods, the twice-born, teachers and the wise; purity, straightforwardness, celibacy and non-violence: this is called austerity of the body.

  15. 17.15

    Speech that causes no distress, that is true, pleasant and beneficial, together with regular recitation of scripture: this is called austerity of speech.

  16. 17.16

    Serenity of mind, gentleness, silence, self-control and purity of feeling: this is called austerity of the mind.

  17. 17.17

    When this threefold austerity is practised with the highest faith by disciplined people who seek no reward, it is called sattvic.

  18. 17.18

    Austerity performed to gain respect, honour and reverence, and done with hypocrisy, is called rajasic here; it is unsteady and does not last.

  19. 17.19

    Austerity undertaken out of a foolish obstinacy, through torment of oneself, or in order to ruin another, is declared tamasic.

  20. 17.20

    A gift given simply because giving is right, to someone who can do nothing in return, at the proper place and time and to a worthy recipient, is held to be sattvic.

  21. 17.21

    But a gift given to get something back, or with a reward in view, or given grudgingly, is held to be rajasic.

  22. 17.22

    A gift given at the wrong place and time, to unworthy people, without respect and with contempt, is declared tamasic.

  23. 17.23

    'Om Tat Sat' is remembered as the threefold designation of Brahman. By it, in ancient times, the brahmins, the Vedas and the sacrifices were ordained.

  24. 17.24

    That is why those who speak of Brahman always begin their acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity, performed as the scriptures direct, by uttering 'Om'.

  25. 17.25

    With the word 'Tat', and without aiming at any reward, those who seek liberation perform the various acts of sacrifice, austerity and charity.

  26. 17.26

    The word 'Sat' is used in the sense of reality and of goodness; likewise, Partha, the word 'Sat' is applied to any praiseworthy act.

  27. 17.27

    Steadfastness in sacrifice, austerity and charity is also called 'Sat', and any action done for that purpose is likewise named 'Sat'.

  28. 17.28

    Whatever is offered, given, practised as austerity or done without faith is called 'asat', Partha; it is of no value, either after death or here.
