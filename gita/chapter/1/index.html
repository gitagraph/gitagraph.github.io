Chapter 1 · 47 verses

Arjuna's Despondency

अर्जुनविषादयोग

On the field of Kurukshetra the two armies face each other. Sanjaya narrates to the blind king Dhritarashtra. Arjuna asks Krishna to drive his chariot between the armies, sees teachers, elders and kinsmen on the other side, and collapses in grief. He puts down his bow and refuses to fight. Every teaching that follows answers this crisis.

कुरुक्षेत्र के मैदान में दोनों सेनाएँ आमने-सामने खड़ी हैं। संजय अंधे राजा धृतराष्ट्र को सब सुनाते हैं। अर्जुन कृष्ण से रथ को दोनों सेनाओं के बीच ले चलने को कहते हैं, सामने गुरुजनों, बड़ों और सगे-संबंधियों को देखते हैं और शोक में डूब जाते हैं। वे धनुष रख देते हैं और युद्ध से इनकार कर देते हैं। आगे की सारी शिक्षा इसी संकट का उत्तर है।

  1. 1.1

    On the field of dharma, at Kurukshetra, my sons and the sons of Pandu gathered there eager to fight. What did they do, Sanjaya?

  2. 1.2

    Seeing the Pandava army drawn up in battle order, King Duryodhana then went up to his teacher and spoke these words.

  3. 1.3

    Duryodhana said: Teacher, look at this vast army of the sons of Pandu, arrayed by the son of Drupada, your own gifted pupil.

  4. 1.4

    Here are heroes, mighty archers equal in battle to Bhima and Arjuna: Yuyudhana, Virata, and Drupada, a great chariot-warrior;

  5. 1.5

    Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana and the valiant king of Kashi; Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Shaibya, foremost among men;

  6. 1.6

    the bold Yudhamanyu and the valiant Uttamauja, the son of Subhadra, and the sons of Draupadi — every one of them a great chariot-warrior.

  7. 1.7

    Now hear, best of the twice-born, about the most distinguished on our side, the leaders of my army. I name them for you so you have them in mind.

  8. 1.8

    Yourself, Bhishma, Karna, and Kripa, ever victorious in battle; Ashvatthama, Vikarna, and the son of Somadatta as well.

  9. 1.9

    And many other heroes, ready to give their lives for my sake, armed with many kinds of weapons and all skilled in war.

  10. 1.10

    That strength of ours, guarded by Bhishma, is beyond measure, while their strength, guarded by Bhima, is limited.

  11. 1.11

    So all of you, holding your assigned places at every point of entry, must above all protect Bhishma.

  12. 1.12

    Then, to cheer him, the mighty grandsire, eldest of the Kurus, roared like a lion and blew his conch loudly.

  13. 1.13

    At once conches, kettledrums, tabors, drums and horns blared out together, and the sound was deafening.

  14. 1.14

    Then Madhava and the son of Pandu, standing in their great chariot yoked to white horses, blew their divine conches.

  15. 1.15

    Hrishikesha blew Panchajanya, Dhananjaya blew Devadatta, and Vrikodara, doer of fearsome deeds, blew the great conch Paundra.

  16. 1.16

    King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, blew Anantavijaya, and Nakula and Sahadeva blew Sughosha and Manipushpaka.

  17. 1.17

    The king of Kashi, a superb archer, Shikhandi the great chariot-warrior, Dhrishtadyumna, Virata, and the unconquered Satyaki;

  18. 1.18

    Drupada, the sons of Draupadi, and the strong-armed son of Subhadra — all of them, lord of the earth, blew their own conches one after another.

  19. 1.19

    That tumultuous roar, echoing through sky and earth, tore at the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons.

  20. 1.20

    Then, seeing Dhritarashtra's sons drawn up in position, as the clash of weapons was about to begin, the son of Pandu whose banner bears the monkey raised his bow…

  21. 1.21

    …and, O king, spoke these words to Hrishikesha. Arjuna said: Achyuta, place my chariot between the two armies,

  22. 1.22

    so that I may look over those who stand here eager for battle, and see whom I must fight in this coming war,

  23. 1.23

    and see those gathered here ready to fight, wishing in this war to please the wicked-minded son of Dhritarashtra.

  24. 1.24

    Addressed in this way by Gudakesha, O Bharata, Hrishikesha set the finest of chariots between the two armies,

  25. 1.25

    facing Bhishma, Drona and all the rulers of the earth, and said: Partha, see these Kurus assembled here.

  26. 1.26

    There Partha saw standing fathers and grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons and friends,

  27. 1.27

    fathers-in-law and well-wishers, in both the armies. Seeing all these kinsmen arrayed, the son of Kunti…

  28. 1.28

    …overcome with deep pity and sinking in sorrow, said this. Arjuna said: Krishna, seeing my own people standing here eager to fight,

  29. 1.29

    my limbs give way and my mouth goes dry; my body trembles and my hair stands on end.

  30. 1.30

    Gandiva is slipping from my hand, my skin is burning all over; I cannot stand steady, and my mind seems to be reeling.

  31. 1.31

    I see ill omens, Keshava, and I see no good coming from killing my own people in battle.

  32. 1.32

    I do not want victory, Krishna, nor a kingdom, nor pleasures. What use is a kingdom to us, Govinda, or enjoyments, or even life itself?

  33. 1.33

    The very people for whose sake we wanted kingdom, enjoyments and pleasures stand here in battle, having staked their lives and wealth:

  34. 1.34

    teachers, fathers, sons, and grandfathers too; maternal uncles, fathers-in-law, grandsons, brothers-in-law and other relatives.

  35. 1.35

    I do not wish to kill them, Madhusudana, even if they kill me — not even for rule over the three worlds, let alone for this earth.

  36. 1.36

    What joy could we have, Janardana, from killing the sons of Dhritarashtra? Only sin would cling to us for slaying these aggressors.

  37. 1.37

    So it is not right for us to kill the sons of Dhritarashtra, our own kinsmen. How could we be happy after killing our own people, Madhava?

  38. 1.38

    Even if they, their minds overpowered by greed, do not see the evil in destroying a family or the sin in betraying friends,

  39. 1.39

    why should we, who clearly see the evil of destroying a family, not know enough to turn away from this sin, Janardana?

  40. 1.40

    When a family is destroyed, its age-old family traditions perish; and when dharma is lost, lawlessness overwhelms the whole family.

  41. 1.41

    When lawlessness prevails, Krishna, the women of the family go astray; and when the women go astray, Varshneya, the social order is confused.

  42. 1.42

    This confusion leads both the family and its destroyers to hell, for their ancestors fall, deprived of the offerings of rice and water.

  43. 1.43

    Through these wrongs of the family-destroyers, which cause confusion of the social order, the enduring duties of caste and family are swept away.

  44. 1.44

    We have heard, Janardana, that people whose family traditions have been destroyed dwell in hell for an indefinite time.

  45. 1.45

    Alas, what a great sin we have resolved to commit, setting out to kill our own people out of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom!

  46. 1.46

    It would be better for me if the sons of Dhritarashtra, weapons in hand, killed me in battle unarmed and unresisting.

  47. 1.47

    Having spoken thus on the battlefield, Arjuna cast aside his bow and arrows and sank down on the seat of the chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief.
