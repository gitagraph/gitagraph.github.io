Bhagavad Gita 1.32

Arjuna

न काङ्क्षे विजयं कृष्ण न च राज्यं सुखानि च ।
किं नो राज्येन गोविन्द किं भोगैर्जीवितेन वा ॥ १.३२ ॥

na kāṅkṣe vijayaṃ kṛṣṇa na ca rājyaṃ sukhāni ca ।
kiṃ no rājyena govinda kiṃ bhogairjīvitena vā ॥

I do not want victory, Krishna, nor a kingdom, nor pleasures. What use is a kingdom to us, Govinda, or enjoyments, or even life itself?

हिन्दीहे कृष्ण, मुझे न विजय चाहिए, न राज्य, न सुख। हे गोविंद, हमें राज्य से, भोगों से या जीवन से भी क्या लेना?

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When a quarrel with your own siblings, say over the family house, leaves you not wanting to win anything at all, notice it honestly: the prize has lost its meaning, and that is the moment to ask what really matters.

Arjuna tells Krishna that he no longer wants victory, a kingdom or pleasures, and asks what use any of them would be, even life itself.

The three words vijaya, rājya and sukha are the very prizes the war was meant to win. He calls Krishna Govinda, traditionally read as 'protector of cows'. Arjuna is not yet refusing to fight on principle; he is saying the reward has lost its meaning.
