श्वशुरान्सुहृदश्चैव सेनयोरुभयोरपि ।
तान्समीक्ष्य स कौन्तेयः सर्वान्बन्धूनवस्थितान् ॥ १.२७ ॥

śvaśurānsuhṛdaścaiva senayorubhayorapi ।
tānsamīkṣya sa kaunteyaḥ sarvānbandhūnavasthitān ॥

fathers-in-law and well-wishers, in both the armies. Seeing all these kinsmen arrayed, the son of Kunti…

Arjuna's gaze takes in fathers-in-law and well-wishers too, and Sanjaya stresses that they stand in both armies.

The word bandhu, 'kinsman', gathers them all into one family, whichever side they are on. The sentence breaks off at 'the son of Kunti', and the next verse tells what this sight of his own people does to him.
