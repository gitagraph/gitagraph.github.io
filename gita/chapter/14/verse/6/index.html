Chapter 14 · The Three GunasVerse 6 of 27

Bhagavad Gita 14.6

Krishna

तत्र सत्त्वं निर्मलत्वात्प्रकाशकमनामयम् ।
सुखसङ्गेन बध्नाति ज्ञानसङ्गेन चानघ ॥ १४.६ ॥

tatra sattvaṃ nirmalatvātprakāśakamanāmayam ।
sukhasaṅgena badhnāti jñānasaṅgena cānagha ॥

Of these, sattva, being pure, is illuminating and free from affliction; yet it binds through attachment to happiness and attachment to knowledge, sinless one.

हिन्दीहे निष्पाप, इनमें सत्त्व निर्मल होने के कारण प्रकाश देने वाला और विकार-रहित है; फिर भी वह सुख की आसक्ति और ज्ञान की आसक्ति से बाँधता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Being the well-read one among friends feels good, but it can quietly turn into pride. If you need to win every argument with facts, the knowing has started to bind you.

Krishna describes sattva, the clearest of the gunas, as pure, illuminating and free from affliction, yet still binding, through attachment to happiness and to knowledge.

This is the subtle point of the verse. Calm, clarity and understanding are good in themselves, but one can cling to them, and the clinging, saṅga, is what binds. A person can be quietly proud of being serene or well-informed, and that pride is still a tie.
