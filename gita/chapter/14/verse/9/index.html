Bhagavad Gita 14.9

Krishna

सत्त्वं सुखे संजयति रजः कर्मणि भारत ।
ज्ञानमावृत्य तु तमः प्रमादे संजयत्युत ॥ १४.९ ॥

sattvaṃ sukhe saṃjayati rajaḥ karmaṇi bhārata ।
jñānamāvṛtya tu tamaḥ pramāde saṃjayatyuta ॥

Sattva attaches one to happiness and rajas to action, Bharata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches one to carelessness.

हिन्दीहे भारत, सत्त्व सुख में लगाता है और रज कर्म में; परंतु तम ज्ञान को ढककर असावधानी में लगा देता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Clarity can tie you to feeling pleased with yourself, drive to always doing more, dullness to just not caring. Notice which knot you're tied in today.

Krishna sums up what each guna attaches a person to: sattva to happiness, rajas to action, and tamas to carelessness.

The verb saṃjayati means to make something stick, so each guna is a kind of glue rather than simply a mood. Tamas works differently from the other two: it first covers knowledge, āvṛtya, and only then pulls one into neglect. The point for a reader is that even the pleasant pull of sattva is still a pull.
