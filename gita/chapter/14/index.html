Chapter 14 · The Three Gunas

Chapter 14 · 27 verses

The Three Gunas

गुणत्रयविभागयोग

Sattva binds through attachment to happiness and knowledge, rajas through attachment to action, tamas through heedlessness and sleep. The one who has gone beyond the gunas (gunatita) is unmoved when they arise or subside.

सत्त्व सुख और ज्ञान की आसक्ति से बाँधता है, रजस् कर्म की आसक्ति से, और तमस् प्रमाद और निद्रा से। जो गुणों से परे चला गया है (गुणातीत), वह उनके उठने और शांत होने पर विचलित नहीं होता।

  1. 14.1

    I will again set out the supreme knowledge, the finest of all knowledge, by knowing which all the sages passed from this world to the highest perfection.

  2. 14.2

    Those who take refuge in this knowledge come to share my nature; they are not born even at the creation of the world, nor shaken at its dissolution.

  3. 14.3

    My womb is the great Brahman — primal Prakriti — and in it I place the seed; from that, Bharata, all beings come into being.

  4. 14.4

    Whatever forms are born in any womb, Kaunteya, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

  5. 14.5

    Sattva, rajas and tamas — these gunas born of Prakriti bind the imperishable embodied self to the body, mighty-armed one.

  6. 14.6

    Of these, sattva, being pure, is illuminating and free from affliction; yet it binds through attachment to happiness and attachment to knowledge, sinless one.

  7. 14.7

    Know rajas to be of the nature of passion, springing from craving and attachment; it binds the embodied self, Kaunteya, through attachment to action.

  8. 14.8

    Know tamas to be born of ignorance, deluding all embodied beings; it binds them, Bharata, through carelessness, sloth and sleep.

  9. 14.9

    Sattva attaches one to happiness and rajas to action, Bharata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches one to carelessness.

  10. 14.10

    Sattva prevails by overpowering rajas and tamas, Bharata; rajas prevails by overpowering sattva and tamas; and tamas likewise by overpowering sattva and rajas.

  11. 14.11

    When the light of knowledge shines through every gateway of this body, then one should know that sattva has grown strong.

  12. 14.12

    Greed, busy activity, the launching of ventures, restlessness and longing — these arise when rajas has grown strong, best of the Bharatas.

  13. 14.13

    Darkness of mind, inertia, carelessness and delusion — these arise when tamas has grown strong, joy of the Kurus.

  14. 14.14

    If the embodied one meets death when sattva is dominant, he goes to the pure worlds of those who know the highest.

  15. 14.15

    Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; dying in tamas, one is born in deluded wombs.

  16. 14.16

    The fruit of good action, it is said, is sattvic and pure; the fruit of rajas is pain, and the fruit of tamas is ignorance.

  17. 14.17

    From sattva comes knowledge, and from rajas greed; from tamas come carelessness and delusion, and ignorance as well.

  18. 14.18

    Those established in sattva rise upward; the rajasic stay in the middle; the tamasic, sunk in the lowest of the gunas, go downward.

  19. 14.19

    When the seer perceives no doer other than the gunas, and knows what lies beyond the gunas, he attains my state of being.

  20. 14.20

    Going beyond these three gunas from which the body arises, the embodied one is freed from the sorrows of birth, death and old age, and tastes immortality.

  21. 14.21

    By what marks is one known who has gone beyond these three gunas, Lord? How does such a person behave, and how does one pass beyond these three gunas?

  22. 14.22

    Pandava, one who does not resent illumination, activity or delusion when they arise, nor long for them when they subside…

  23. 14.23

    …who sits as if indifferent, unshaken by the gunas, who stays steady knowing simply that it is the gunas that are at work, and does not waver…

  24. 14.24

    …who is the same in pain and pleasure, rests in the self, regards a clod, a stone and gold alike, treats the pleasant and unpleasant equally, is steady, and takes blame and praise of himself alike…

  25. 14.25

    …who is the same in honour and dishonour, the same toward friend and foe, and has given up every self-driven undertaking — such a one is said to have gone beyond the gunas.

  26. 14.26

    And one who serves me with the yoga of unwavering devotion goes beyond these gunas and becomes fit to become Brahman.

  27. 14.27

    For I am the foundation of Brahman — of the immortal and imperishable, of eternal dharma, and of absolute bliss.
