Bhagavad Gita 18.78

Sanjaya

यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः ।
तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम ॥ १८.७८ ॥

yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanurdharaḥ ।
tatra śrīrvijayo bhūtirdhruvā nītirmatirmama ॥

Wherever there is Krishna, the lord of yoga, and wherever there is Partha, the archer, there, I am sure, are fortune, victory, prosperity and steady good conduct.

हिन्दीजहाँ योगेश्वर कृष्ण हैं और जहाँ धनुर्धारी पार्थ हैं, वहीं सौभाग्य, विजय, समृद्धि और अटल नीति है — ऐसा मेरा मत है।

In the last words of the Gita, Sanjaya tells King Dhritarashtra that wherever Krishna and Arjuna are found, there are fortune, victory, prosperity and steady good conduct.

The key words are yogeśvara, 'lord of yoga', for Krishna, and dhanurdhara, 'bow-bearer', for Arjuna, answering the king who opened the poem asking what happened on the battlefield. For the story it is Sanjaya's quiet verdict on the war; for readers it is often taken to mean that lasting success comes where insight and capable action stand together.
