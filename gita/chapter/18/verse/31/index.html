Bhagavad Gita 18.31

Krishna

यया धर्ममधर्मं च कार्यं चाकार्यमेव च ।
अयथावत्प्रजानाति बुद्धिः सा पार्थ राजसी ॥ १८.३१ ॥

yayā dharmamadharmaṃ ca kāryaṃ cākāryameva ca ।
ayathāvatprajānāti buddhiḥ sā pārtha rājasī ॥

The understanding by which one judges dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done, in a distorted way, that understanding, Partha, is rajasic.

हिन्दीहे पार्थ, जिस बुद्धि से मनुष्य धर्म और अधर्म को, तथा करने और न करने योग्य को ठीक से नहीं, बल्कि गलत ढंग से समझता है, वह बुद्धि राजस है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

You know the expense rules perfectly well, yet a family dinner starts to look like a 'client meeting'. That is understanding that sees right and wrong, only bent by what you want.

Rajasic understanding, Krishna says, is the kind that does see right and wrong, and what should and should not be done, but sees them askew.

The key word is ayathāvat, 'not as it truly is': unlike the clear understanding of 18.30, this one is not blind, only bent. Plainly, it is knowing the rules well enough while letting what we want quietly decide how they apply to us.
