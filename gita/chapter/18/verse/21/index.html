Bhagavad Gita 18.21

Krishna

पृथक्त्वेन तु यज्ज्ञानं नानाभावान्पृथग्विधान् ।
वेत्ति सर्वेषु भूतेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि राजसम् ॥ १८.२१ ॥

pṛthaktvena tu yajjñānaṃ nānābhāvānpṛthagvidhān ।
vetti sarveṣu bhūteṣu tajjñānaṃ viddhi rājasam ॥

But the knowledge that sees in all beings separate entities of various kinds, each distinct from the others, know that knowledge to be rajasic.

हिन्दीपरंतु जो ज्ञान सभी प्राणियों में अलग-अलग प्रकार की भिन्न-भिन्न सत्ताओं को अलग करके देखता है, उस ज्ञान को राजस जानो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Seeing a new office only as separate camps, the sales lot, the tech lot, the bosses, is knowledge of a narrow kind. It sorts people well and connects them badly.

Krishna describes rājasa knowledge: the outlook that sees in all beings only separate entities, each of a different kind and walled off from the rest.

The key word is pṛthaktva, 'separateness'. It is not wrong about differences, which are real enough, but it stops there, so that every being becomes a rival or a means to an end. Set beside 18.20, it shows the same world seen without the thread that joins it.
