Bhagavad Gita 18.36

Krishna

सुखं त्विदानीं त्रिविधं शृणु मे भरतर्षभ ।
अभ्यासाद्रमते यत्र दुःखान्तं च निगच्छति ॥ १८.३६ ॥

sukhaṃ tvidānīṃ trividhaṃ śṛṇu me bharatarṣabha ।
abhyāsādramate yatra duḥkhāntaṃ ca nigacchati ॥

Now hear from me, best of the Bharatas, about the three kinds of happiness: the happiness in which one finds delight through practice and arrives at the end of sorrow…

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

The joy of a friendship that deepens over years comes slowly, through practice, and ends a good deal of loneliness. Not all happiness arrives in a rush; some is built.

Krishna begins his account of the three kinds of happiness with the first, a happiness one comes to enjoy through practice and which leads to the end of sorrow; the description continues into 18.37.

The key word is abhyāsa, practice: this joy is not found all at once but grows the more it is lived. In everyday terms, it is the kind of contentment that deepens with steady habit rather than fading with repetition.
