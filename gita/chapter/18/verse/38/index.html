Bhagavad Gita 18.38

Krishna

विषयेन्द्रियसंयोगाद्यत्तदग्रेऽमृतोपमम् ।
परिणामे विषमिव तत्सुखं राजसं स्मृतम् ॥ १८.३८ ॥

viṣayendriyasaṃyogādyattadagre'mṛtopamam ।
pariṇāme viṣamiva tatsukhaṃ rājasaṃ smṛtam ॥

The happiness that comes from the contact of the senses with their objects, nectar-like at first but like poison in the end, is remembered as rajasic.

हिन्दीजो सुख इंद्रियों और उनके विषयों के संयोग से होता है, जो पहले अमृत जैसा पर अंत में विष जैसा होता है, वह राजस माना गया है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A cutting remark that wins an argument feels great for a minute and sours the relationship for weeks. Pleasures that are sweet first and bitter later are worth noticing before you speak.

Rajasic happiness, Krishna says, arises from the contact of the senses with their objects: nectar at the start, poison by the end.

It is the exact mirror of the sattvic happiness of 18.37, with the order of sweet and bitter reversed. The key phrase is viṣaya-indriya-saṃyoga, the meeting of sense and object. Plainly, it describes pleasures that peak in the moment and leave a cost behind, in health, money, relationships or simply in craving more.
