Bhagavad Gita 18.73

Arjuna

नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत ।
स्थितोऽस्मि गतसन्देहः करिष्ये वचनं तव ॥ १८.७३ ॥

naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtirlabdhā tvatprasādānmayācyuta ।
sthito'smi gatasandehaḥ kariṣye vacanaṃ tava ॥

My delusion is gone; by your grace, Achyuta, my memory has been restored. I stand firm, my doubts are cleared, and I will do as you say.

हिन्दीहे अच्युत, आपकी कृपा से मेरा मोह नष्ट हो गया है और मुझे अपनी स्मृति फिर से मिल गई है। मैं दृढ़ हूँ, मेरे संदेह दूर हो गए हैं, और मैं आपकी आज्ञा का पालन करूँगा।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When a long confusion finally lifts, about a career, a relationship, a move, say so plainly and then act on it. Clarity is meant to be used, not just enjoyed.

In his last words in the Gita, Arjuna tells Krishna that his delusion is gone, his memory restored by Krishna's grace, and he will do as Krishna says.

Answering Krishna's question whether he heard with full attention, he names smṛti, 'memory' or 'recollection': he has recovered his sense of who he is and what the moment asks of him. For the story it closes the arc begun in chapter 1, where he dropped his bow in despair; now he stands firm.
