Chapter 18 · Liberation through RenunciationVerse 1 of 78

Bhagavad Gita 18.1

Arjuna

संन्यासस्य महाबाहो तत्त्वमिच्छामि वेदितुम् ।
त्यागस्य च हृषीकेश पृथक्केशिनिषूदन ॥ १८.१ ॥

saṃnyāsasya mahābāho tattvamicchāmi veditum ।
tyāgasya ca hṛṣīkeśa pṛthakkeśiniṣūdana ॥

Mighty-armed one, Hrishikesha, slayer of the demon Keshi, I want to understand the true nature of renunciation (sannyasa) and of relinquishment (tyaga), each one separately.

हिन्दीहे महाबाहु, हे हृषीकेश, हे केशी दैत्य का वध करने वाले! मैं संन्यास और त्याग, दोनों का वास्तविक स्वरूप अलग-अलग जानना चाहता हूँ।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Is quitting your job to care for a parent giving something up, or just letting go of what the job meant to you? Asking what each word really means can clear up a hard choice.

Arjuna opens the Gita's last chapter by asking Krishna to explain, each one separately, the true nature of saṃnyāsa, renunciation, and tyāga, relinquishment.

Both words mean giving something up, and both have come up before (5.1–2, 6.1–2), so he wants the line between them drawn clearly. The chapter that follows gathers the whole teaching together, and it begins with this question of what letting go really means.
