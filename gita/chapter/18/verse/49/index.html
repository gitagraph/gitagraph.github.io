Chapter 18 · Liberation through RenunciationVerse 49 of 78

Bhagavad Gita 18.49

Krishna

असक्तबुद्धिः सर्वत्र जितात्मा विगतस्पृहः ।
नैष्कर्म्यसिद्धिं परमां संन्यासेनाधिगच्छति ॥ १८.४९ ॥

asaktabuddhiḥ sarvatra jitātmā vigataspṛhaḥ ।
naiṣkarmyasiddhiṃ paramāṃ saṃnyāsenādhigacchati ॥

One whose understanding is unattached everywhere, who has mastered himself and is free of craving, attains through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action.

हिन्दीजिसकी बुद्धि सब जगह अनासक्त है, जिसने अपने आप को जीत लिया है और जिसकी इच्छाएँ मिट गई हैं, वह संन्यास के द्वारा नैष्कर्म्य की परम सिद्धि प्राप्त करता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

After retirement, when the title and the busy diary are gone, the freedom this verse describes comes not from doing nothing, but from no longer needing the work to tell you who you are.

One who is unattached everywhere, self-mastered and free of craving reaches, through renunciation, the highest perfection of freedom from action.

The key word is naiṣkarmya, 'actionlessness', which the Gita has already said cannot be had by simply not acting (3.4); it means acting without being bound by what one does. Here saṃnyāsa, renunciation, carries the sense this chapter has built up (18.2–11): letting go of attachment and of the fruit, not necessarily of one's work.
