Bhagavad Gita 18.66

Krishna

सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज ।
अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥ १८.६६ ॥

sarvadharmānparityajya māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja ।
ahaṃ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ ॥

Letting go of all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.

हिन्दीसभी धर्मों को छोड़कर केवल मेरी शरण में आ जाओ। मैं तुम्हें सभी पापों से मुक्त कर दूँगा; शोक मत करो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you have tried everything to mend things with a sibling who won't respond, this verse invites you to set down the weight of fixing it alone and trust what you hold highest.

In what is often read as the Gita's final word of teaching, Krishna tells Arjuna to let go of all dharmas and come to him alone for refuge, promising to free him from all sins.

The key word is śaraṇa, refuge, paired with the puzzling sarva-dharmān parityajya: some traditions read it as giving up reliance on every other means, others as renouncing action and the sense of being its doer. The closing mā śucaḥ, 'do not grieve', answers the grief that began the dialogue.
