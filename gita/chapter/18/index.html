Chapter 18 · Liberation through Renunciation

Chapter 18 · 78 verses

Liberation through Renunciation

मोक्षसंन्यासयोग

The summing up. Sannyasa is giving up desire-driven acts; tyaga is giving up the fruit of all acts. Better one's own dharma done imperfectly than another's done well. Then the final word: abandon all dharmas and take refuge in me alone. Arjuna's doubts are gone; he will do as told. Sanjaya closes the dialogue.

उपसंहार। संन्यास है कामना से प्रेरित कर्मों का त्याग; त्याग है सभी कर्मों के फल का त्याग। दूसरे का धर्म भली-भाँति करने से अपना धर्म अपूर्ण रूप से करना श्रेष्ठ है। फिर अंतिम वचन: सब धर्मों को छोड़कर केवल मेरी शरण में आओ। अर्जुन के संशय मिट जाते हैं; वे आज्ञा का पालन करेंगे। संजय संवाद का समापन करते हैं।

  1. 18.1

    Mighty-armed one, Hrishikesha, slayer of the demon Keshi, I want to understand the true nature of renunciation (sannyasa) and of relinquishment (tyaga), each one separately.

  2. 18.2

    The wise understand sannyasa as setting aside actions driven by desire, while the discerning describe tyaga as giving up the fruits of every action.

  3. 18.3

    Some thinkers hold that action itself should be abandoned because it is tainted with fault; others say that acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity must never be abandoned.

  4. 18.4

    Now hear my firm conclusion about relinquishment, best of the Bharatas; for relinquishment, tiger among men, is declared to be of three kinds.

  5. 18.5

    Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up; they ought to be performed, for sacrifice, giving and austerity purify even the wise.

  6. 18.6

    Yet even these acts should be done after giving up attachment and the fruits. This, Partha, is my settled and highest view.

  7. 18.7

    It is not right to renounce an action that is one's duty. Giving it up out of delusion is called tamasic relinquishment.

  8. 18.8

    Whoever gives up an action because it is painful, or out of fear of bodily hardship, makes a rajasic relinquishment and does not gain the reward of relinquishment.

  9. 18.9

    When a prescribed action is done simply because it ought to be done, Arjuna, giving up attachment and the fruit, that relinquishment is considered sattvic.

  10. 18.10

    The relinquisher filled with sattva, wise and free of doubt, neither resents unpleasant work nor clings to pleasant work.

  11. 18.11

    No embodied being can give up actions completely. But one who gives up the fruits of action is truly called a relinquisher.

  12. 18.12

    The fruit of action is threefold after death for those who have not relinquished: unwanted, wanted and mixed. For those who have renounced, there is none at all.

  13. 18.13

    Mighty-armed one, learn from me these five causes for the accomplishment of every action, as set out in the Sankhya teaching that brings action to its end.

  14. 18.14

    They are the seat of action (the body), the doer, the various instruments (the senses), the many distinct kinds of effort, and fifth among them, the divine factor (daiva).

  15. 18.15

    Whatever action a person begins with body, speech or mind, whether right or wrong, these five are its causes.

  16. 18.16

    That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees the pure Self alone as the doer, that dull-minded person does not see truly.

  17. 18.17

    One who has no sense of being the doer and whose understanding is not stained, even if he kills these people, does not kill and is not bound.

  18. 18.18

    Knowledge, the object known and the knower are the threefold impulse to action; the instrument, the act and the doer are the threefold basis of action.

  19. 18.19

    Knowledge, action and the doer are each said to be of three kinds, differing according to the gunas, in the analysis of the gunas. Hear about them as they are.

  20. 18.20

    The knowledge by which one sees a single imperishable reality in all beings, undivided among the divided, know that knowledge to be sattvic.

  21. 18.21

    But the knowledge that sees in all beings separate entities of various kinds, each distinct from the others, know that knowledge to be rajasic.

  22. 18.22

    And the knowledge that clings to one single thing as if it were the whole, without reason, without grasp of the truth, and narrow, is declared tamasic.

  23. 18.23

    An action that is prescribed, done without attachment, without liking or dislike, by one who does not seek its fruit, is called sattvic.

  24. 18.24

    But an action done by one craving desires, or with ego, involving a great deal of strain, is called rajasic.

  25. 18.25

    An action undertaken out of delusion, without regard for its consequences, loss, harm to others, or one's own capacity, is called tamasic.

  26. 18.26

    A doer who is free of attachment, does not talk of 'I', is full of steadiness and zeal, and is unmoved by success or failure, is called sattvic.

  27. 18.27

    A doer who is driven by passion, eager for the fruits of action, greedy, harmful by nature, impure, and swept by joy and grief, is called rajasic.

  28. 18.28

    A doer who is undisciplined, crude, stubborn, deceitful, malicious, lazy, gloomy and given to delay, is called tamasic.

  29. 18.29

    Now hear, Dhananjaya, the threefold division of understanding and of resolve according to the gunas, which I will explain fully and one by one.

  30. 18.30

    The understanding that knows engagement and withdrawal, what should and should not be done, what is to be feared and what is not, and what binds and what frees, that understanding, Partha, is sattvic.

  31. 18.31

    The understanding by which one judges dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done, in a distorted way, that understanding, Partha, is rajasic.

  32. 18.32

    The understanding that, wrapped in darkness, takes adharma for dharma and sees everything upside down, that understanding, Partha, is tamasic.

  33. 18.33

    The resolve by which one holds steady the workings of the mind, the life-breath and the senses through unwavering yoga, that resolve, Partha, is sattvic.

  34. 18.34

    But the resolve by which one holds on to dharma, pleasure and wealth, Arjuna, craving their fruits out of attachment, that resolve, Partha, is rajasic.

  35. 18.35

    The resolve by which a dull-witted person refuses to let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency and arrogance, that resolve, Partha, is tamasic.

  36. 18.36

    Now hear from me, best of the Bharatas, about the three kinds of happiness: the happiness in which one finds delight through practice and arrives at the end of sorrow…

  37. 18.37

    …which is like poison at first but like nectar in the end, born of the clarity of one's own understanding of the Self: that happiness is called sattvic.

  38. 18.38

    The happiness that comes from the contact of the senses with their objects, nectar-like at first but like poison in the end, is remembered as rajasic.

  39. 18.39

    The happiness that deludes the self both at the beginning and in its outcome, arising from sleep, sloth and carelessness, is declared tamasic.

  40. 18.40

    There is no being on earth, nor among the gods in heaven, that is free of these three gunas born of Prakriti.

  41. 18.41

    The duties of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, scorcher of foes, are divided according to the gunas that arise from their own nature.

  42. 18.42

    Calm, self-restraint, austerity, purity, forbearance, straightforwardness, knowledge, realised wisdom and faith in the sacred are the natural work of a brahmin.

  43. 18.43

    Valour, vigour, steadiness, skill, not fleeing in battle, generosity and the bearing of a leader are the natural work of a kshatriya.

  44. 18.44

    Farming, tending cattle and trade are the natural work of a vaishya; work that consists of service is the natural work of a shudra.

  45. 18.45

    A person devoted to their own work attains perfection. Hear how one who is intent on their own work reaches that perfection.

  46. 18.46

    By worshipping, through his own work, the one from whom all beings come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a person attains perfection.

  47. 18.47

    Better one's own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Doing the work set by one's own nature, one incurs no sin.

  48. 18.48

    Kaunteya, one should not abandon the work that comes naturally to one, even if it has flaws; for every undertaking is wrapped in flaws, as fire is wrapped in smoke.

  49. 18.49

    One whose understanding is unattached everywhere, who has mastered himself and is free of craving, attains through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action.

  50. 18.50

    Learn from me in brief, Kaunteya, how one who has attained that perfection also attains Brahman, the highest culmination of knowledge.

  51. 18.51

    Endowed with a purified understanding, firmly restraining himself with resolve, setting aside sound and the other sense objects, and casting off attraction and aversion…

  52. 18.52

    …living in solitude, eating lightly, with speech, body and mind controlled, always devoted to the yoga of meditation, and taking refuge in dispassion…

  53. 18.53

    …letting go of ego, force, arrogance, desire, anger and possessiveness, free of the sense of 'mine' and at peace, such a person is fit to become Brahman.

  54. 18.54

    Having become Brahman, serene in spirit, he neither grieves nor longs. Equal towards all beings, he attains supreme devotion to me.

  55. 18.55

    Through devotion he comes to know me in truth, how great I am and who I am. Then, having known me in truth, he enters into me at once.

  56. 18.56

    Though always performing all actions, one who takes refuge in me reaches, by my grace, the eternal, imperishable abode.

  57. 18.57

    Mentally offering all actions to me, holding me as your highest goal, and resting on the yoga of understanding, keep your mind fixed on me always.

  58. 18.58

    With your mind on me, you will cross every obstacle by my grace. But if, out of ego, you will not listen, you will be lost.

  59. 18.59

    If, relying on ego, you think 'I will not fight', that resolve of yours is futile; your own nature will compel you.

  60. 18.60

    Kaunteya, bound by your own work born of your nature, you will do, even against your will, what you now refuse to do out of delusion.

  61. 18.61

    The Lord dwells in the heart of all beings, Arjuna, turning them all by his maya as if they were mounted on a machine.

  62. 18.62

    Take refuge in him alone with your whole being, Bharata. By his grace you will attain supreme peace and the eternal abode.

  63. 18.63

    Thus I have set out for you this wisdom, more secret than any secret. Reflect on it fully, and then do as you wish.

  64. 18.64

    Hear once more my supreme word, the most secret of all. You are dearly loved by me, so I will tell you what is for your good.

  65. 18.65

    Fix your mind on me, be devoted to me, offer worship to me, bow down to me. You will come to me; I promise you truly, for you are dear to me.

  66. 18.66

    Letting go of all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.

  67. 18.67

    Never speak of this to one without austerity, nor to one without devotion, nor to one who will not listen, nor to one who finds fault with me.

  68. 18.68

    Whoever passes on this supreme secret to my devotees, having shown the highest devotion to me, will without doubt come to me.

  69. 18.69

    No one among people does work dearer to me than he, and there will be no one on earth dearer to me than he.

  70. 18.70

    And whoever studies this sacred dialogue of ours, by him I will have been worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge; that is my view.

  71. 18.71

    And a person who simply listens to it with faith and without finding fault, he too will be freed and will reach the auspicious worlds of those whose deeds are pure.

  72. 18.72

    Partha, have you heard this with a fully focused mind? Dhananjaya, has your delusion born of ignorance been dispelled?

  73. 18.73

    My delusion is gone; by your grace, Achyuta, my memory has been restored. I stand firm, my doubts are cleared, and I will do as you say.

  74. 18.74

    Thus I heard this wondrous dialogue between Vasudeva and the great-souled Partha, which made my hair stand on end.

  75. 18.75

    By the grace of Vyasa I heard this supreme secret, this yoga, directly from Krishna himself, the lord of yoga, as he spoke it.

  76. 18.76

    O King, as I recall again and again this wondrous and sacred dialogue between Keshava and Arjuna, I rejoice over and over.

  77. 18.77

    And as I recall again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again.

  78. 18.78

    Wherever there is Krishna, the lord of yoga, and wherever there is Partha, the archer, there, I am sure, are fortune, victory, prosperity and steady good conduct.
