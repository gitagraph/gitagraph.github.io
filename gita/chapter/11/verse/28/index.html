Bhagavad Gita 11.28

Arjuna

यथा नदीनां बहवोऽम्बुवेगाः ।
समुद्रमेवाभिमुखा द्रवन्ति ।
तथा तवामी नरलोकवीरा ।
विशन्ति वक्त्राण्यभिविज्वलन्ति ॥ ११.२८ ॥

yathā nadīnāṃ bahavo'mbuvegāḥ ।
samudramevābhimukhā dravanti ।
tathā tavāmī naralokavīrā ।
viśanti vaktrāṇyabhivijvalanti ॥

As the many currents of rivers race headlong toward the sea, so these heroes of the human world are entering your flaming mouths.

हिन्दीजैसे नदियों की अनेक धाराएँ समुद्र की ओर ही दौड़ती हैं, वैसे ही मनुष्य लोक के ये वीर आपके जलते हुए मुखों में प्रवेश कर रहे हैं।

Arjuna uses the image of rivers racing to the sea to describe the heroes of the human world streaming into the cosmic form's blazing mouths.

The key word is abhimukhāḥ, 'facing toward': the currents all point one way and cannot turn back. The simile stresses inevitability more than horror, and it pairs with the next verse, where the same rush is pictured as moths flying into a flame.
