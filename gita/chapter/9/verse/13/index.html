Bhagavad Gita 9.13

Krishna

महात्मानस्तु मां पार्थ दैवीं प्रकृतिमाश्रिताः ।
भजन्त्यनन्यमनसो ज्ञात्वा भूतादिमव्ययम् ॥ ९.१३ ॥

mahātmānastu māṃ pārtha daivīṃ prakṛtimāśritāḥ ।
bhajantyananyamanaso jñātvā bhūtādimavyayam ॥

But great souls, Partha, who take refuge in the divine nature, worship me with minds turned to nothing else, knowing me as the imperishable source of all beings.

हिन्दीपरंतु हे पार्थ, दैवी प्रकृति का आश्रय लेने वाले महात्मा मुझे सब प्राणियों का आदि और अविनाशी जानकर अनन्य मन से मेरा भजन करते हैं।

In your life

People who turn to something higher with a single mind often show it in plain ways: patience in queues, gentleness with the old. Their devotion is visible in how they treat others.

Krishna describes the great souls, mahātmās, who take refuge in the divine nature and worship him with minds turned to nothing else, in contrast to the deluded of 9.11–12.

Daivī, 'divine', is set against the fiendish and demonic disposition just named, a pairing chapter 16 develops. The key phrase is ananya-manas, 'a mind with no other': devotion here means the whole attention gathered in one direction, grounded in knowing him as the unchanging source of beings.
