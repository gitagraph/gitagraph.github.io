Bhagavad Gita 9.27

Krishna

यत्करोषि यदश्नासि यज्जुहोषि ददासि यत् ।
यत्तपस्यसि कौन्तेय तत्कुरुष्व मदर्पणम् ॥ ९.२७ ॥

yatkaroṣi yadaśnāsi yajjuhoṣi dadāsi yat ।
yattapasyasi kaunteya tatkuruṣva madarpaṇam ॥

Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in the fire, whatever you give and whatever discipline you practise, Kaunteya, do it as an offering to me.

हिन्दीहे कौंतेय, तुम जो कुछ करते हो, जो खाते हो, जो हवन करते हो, जो दान देते हो और जो तप करते हो, वह सब मुझे अर्पित करके करो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Folding laundry, paying the bills, the school run: these needn't be chores to rush through. Done with care, as an offering to what you hold highest, even ordinary work takes on meaning.

Krishna asks Arjuna to make the whole of life an offering: whatever he does, eats, offers in the fire, gives or practises as discipline.

The key phrase is mad-arpaṇam, 'as an offering to me', which widens the simple gift of a leaf or water to ordinary doing and even eating. Read plainly, there is no separate religious corner of the day; any act can be done in the spirit of offering, and the next verse says this loosens the bonds that action creates.
