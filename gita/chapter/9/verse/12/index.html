Bhagavad Gita 9.12

Krishna

मोघाशा मोघकर्माणो मोघज्ञाना विचेतसः ।
राक्षसीमासुरीं चैव प्रकृतिं मोहिनीं श्रिताः ॥ ९.१२ ॥

moghāśā moghakarmāṇo moghajñānā vicetasaḥ ।
rākṣasīmāsurīṃ caiva prakṛtiṃ mohinīṃ śritāḥ ॥

Their hopes are empty, their actions futile and their knowledge useless; senseless, they cling to the deluding nature of fiends and demons.

हिन्दीउनकी आशाएँ व्यर्थ, कर्म व्यर्थ और ज्ञान भी व्यर्थ होता है; ऐसे विवेकहीन लोग मोह में डालने वाली राक्षसी और आसुरी प्रकृति का सहारा लेते हैं।

In your life

Hopes built only on getting more, and work done only to feed that hope, tend to leave you empty however much you get. Notice when your plans have no deeper aim than more.

Krishna says that people who dismiss him (9.11) find their hopes, actions and knowledge all coming to nothing, because they rely on a nature that deludes.

The word mogha, 'empty, in vain', is repeated three times for weight. Rākṣasī and āsurī, 'fiendish' and 'demonic', here qualify prakṛti, so they describe a nature or disposition; chapter 16 describes it at length. The point is that effort built on a mistaken view of things, however busy, leads nowhere.
