Bhagavad Gita 9.33

Krishna

किं पुनर्ब्राह्मणाः पुण्या भक्ता राजर्षयस्तथा ।
अनित्यमसुखं लोकमिमं प्राप्य भजस्व माम् ॥ ९.३३ ॥

kiṃ punarbrāhmaṇāḥ puṇyā bhaktā rājarṣayastathā ।
anityamasukhaṃ lokamimaṃ prāpya bhajasva mām ॥

How much more so, then, pure Brahmins and devoted royal sages! Having come into this passing and joyless world, worship me.

हिन्दीफिर पुण्यात्मा ब्राह्मणों और भक्त राजर्षियों का तो कहना ही क्या! इसलिए इस क्षणभंगुर और दुःखभरे संसार में आकर तुम मेरा भजन करो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Life can feel short and uncertain; a lost job, a sudden illness. That is exactly why it's worth turning towards what matters most now, instead of waiting for a calmer time.

Krishna adds that if even those named in 9.32 reach the highest, then how much more so pure Brahmins and devoted royal sages, and he urges Arjuna, born into this passing and joyless world, to worship him.

A rājarṣi, 'royal sage', is a king who is also a seer, like the royal sages who, in 4.1–2, handed this yoga down. The world is called anitya and asukha, impermanent and without lasting happiness, and that is offered as the reason to turn to him now.
