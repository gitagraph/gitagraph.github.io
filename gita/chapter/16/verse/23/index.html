Bhagavad Gita 16.23

Krishna

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः ।
न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम् ॥ १६.२३ ॥

yaḥ śāstravidhimutsṛjya vartate kāmakārataḥ ।
na sa siddhimavāpnoti na sukhaṃ na parāṃ gatim ॥

One who sets aside the guidance of scripture and acts on the impulse of desire attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest goal.

हिन्दीजो शास्त्र की विधि को छोड़कर अपनी इच्छा के अनुसार मनमाना आचरण करता है, उसे न सिद्धि मिलती है, न सुख और न परम गति।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Doing whatever you feel like, ignoring every guide and principle, feels like freedom at first. It rarely brings the happiness or the steadiness it promised.

Krishna warns that a person who drops the guidance of scripture and simply follows desire gains nothing worth having: not perfection, not happiness, not the highest goal.

He says this as he finishes describing the 'demonic' traits and the three gates of desire, anger and greed (16.21–22). The key phrase is kāma-kārataḥ, 'acting as desire dictates'. The point is less about rule-keeping for its own sake than about the cost of letting impulse be the only guide.
