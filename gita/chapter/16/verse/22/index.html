Bhagavad Gita 16.22

Krishna

एतैर्विमुक्तः कौन्तेय तमोद्वारैस्त्रिभिर्नरः ।
आचरत्यात्मनः श्रेयस्ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ १६.२२ ॥

etairvimuktaḥ kaunteya tamodvāraistribhirnaraḥ ।
ācaratyātmanaḥ śreyastato yāti parāṃ gatim ॥

A person released from these three gates of darkness, Kaunteya, does what is truly good for the self, and so reaches the highest goal.

हिन्दीहे कौंतेय, अंधकार के इन तीन द्वारों से छूटा हुआ मनुष्य वही करता है जो आत्मा के लिए सचमुच कल्याणकारी है, और इससे परम गति पाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Giving up a habit you have fed for years, like the nightly drink or the cigarette after lunch, feels at first like losing a pleasure. The lasting good often lies just past what is merely pleasant.

Krishna says that a person freed from the three gates of darkness does what is truly good for the self and so reaches the highest goal.

The three gates, named in 16.21, are lust, anger and greed. Śreyas is the lasting good, as against what is merely pleasant. After the dark portrait of 16.7–20, this verse opens a way out: the descent is not fixed, and stepping back from these three is where the turn begins.
