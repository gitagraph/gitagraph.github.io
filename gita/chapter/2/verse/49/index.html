Bhagavad Gita 2.49

Krishna

दूरेण ह्यवरं कर्म बुद्धियोगाद्धनंजय ।
बुद्धौ शरणमन्विच्छ कृपणाः फलहेतवः ॥ २.४९ ॥

dūreṇa hyavaraṃ karma buddhiyogāddhanaṃjaya ।
buddhau śaraṇamanviccha kṛpaṇāḥ phalahetavaḥ ॥

Mere action is far inferior to the yoga of understanding, Dhananjaya. Seek refuge in that understanding; pitiable are those whose motive is the fruit.

हिन्दीहे धनंजय, केवल कर्म बुद्धियोग से बहुत नीचा है। उसी बुद्धि की शरण लो; फल के लिए कर्म करने वाले दीन होते हैं।

Krishna says mere action is far below buddhiyoga, the yoga of understanding, and urges Arjuna to take refuge in that understanding.

Buddhiyoga means acting from a clear, even mind rather than from craving. Those whose motive (hetu) is only the fruit are called kṛpaṇa, 'pitiable', echoing the kārpaṇya, faint-heartedness, that Arjuna confessed in 2.7. The verse suggests that working only for the payoff makes a person poorer, not richer.
