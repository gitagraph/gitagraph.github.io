Bhagavad Gita 2.64

Krishna

रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् ।
आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥ २.६४ ॥

rāgadveṣaviyuktaistu viṣayānindriyaiścaran ।
ātmavaśyairvidheyātmā prasādamadhigacchati ॥

But the self-controlled person, moving among sense objects with senses free from attraction and aversion and under the control of the self, attains inner calm.

हिन्दीपर जिसने अपने मन को वश में कर लिया है, वह राग और द्वेष से मुक्त और अपने वश में रहने वाली इंद्रियों से विषयों के बीच विचरता हुआ मन की प्रसन्नता पाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

At a family gathering full of people you like and a few you don't, you needn't stay home. Go, but without clinging to some or bristling at others, and you'll come back calm.

Krishna describes the alternative to the slide into ruin traced in 2.62–63: the self-controlled person moves among sense objects with senses free of attraction and aversion, and attains inner calm.

The vidheyātmā, one whose mind answers to him, does not run from the world; his senses are free of rāga and dveṣa, and what he gains is prasāda, a clear, settled calm. The difference lies not in what one meets but in whether liking and disliking push one around.
