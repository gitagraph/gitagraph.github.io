Bhagavad Gita 2.28

Krishna

अव्यक्तादीनि भूतानि व्यक्तमध्यानि भारत ।
अव्यक्तनिधनान्येव तत्र का परिदेवना ॥ २.२८ ॥

avyaktādīni bhūtāni vyaktamadhyāni bhārata ।
avyaktanidhanānyeva tatra kā paridevanā ॥

Beings are unmanifest in their beginning, manifest in their middle, and unmanifest again at their end, Bharata. What is there to lament in that?

हिन्दीहे भारत, सभी प्राणी आरंभ में अव्यक्त होते हैं, बीच में व्यक्त होते हैं और अंत में फिर अव्यक्त हो जाते हैं। फिर इसमें विलाप किस बात का?

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A friendship that began in a hostel room and faded after a move abroad had no shape before it and none after. Treasure the years in the middle rather than grieving that they had edges.

Krishna points out that beings are avyakta, unmanifest, at their beginning and at their end, and manifest only in the middle, so what is there to lament?

The argument works even for someone unsure about an eternal self. Put simply, the span we can see and touch is the middle of a longer story, and mourning that middle as if it were the whole is what he questions.
