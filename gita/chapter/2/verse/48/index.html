Bhagavad Gita 2.48

Krishna

योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनंजय ।
सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते ॥ २.४८ ॥

yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā dhanaṃjaya ।
siddhyasiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṃ yoga ucyate ॥

Established in yoga, do your work, Dhananjaya, giving up attachment and staying even-minded in success and failure. This evenness is called yoga.

हिन्दीहे धनंजय, योग में स्थित होकर, आसक्ति छोड़कर और सफलता-असफलता में समान रहकर कर्म करो। यही समता योग कहलाती है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Cook the family dinner with full care, then let the praise or the complaints be. The even mind that remains, whichever comes, is what the Gita calls yoga.

Krishna defines yoga as samatva, evenness: do the work steadily and without attachment, staying the same whether it succeeds or fails.

Coming straight after the verse on one's right to action but not its fruits, this is the Gita's first definition of yoga; siddhi and asiddhi are success and failure. Yoga here is not a posture or a retreat but a way of working, with care for the task and without one's balance depending on its outcome.
