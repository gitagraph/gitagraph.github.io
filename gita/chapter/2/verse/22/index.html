Bhagavad Gita 2.22

Krishna

वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्यन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही ॥ २.२२ ॥

vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya navāni gṛhṇāti naro'parāṇi ।
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇānyanyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī ॥

As a person discards worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self discards worn-out bodies and enters new ones.

हिन्दीजैसे मनुष्य पुराने कपड़े उतारकर नए कपड़े पहन लेता है, वैसे ही देहधारी आत्मा पुराने शरीर छोड़कर नए शरीरों में चली जाती है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When someone you love dies, the Gita offers an image to hold: the body was worn out like old clothes, but what made them who they were was never just the body.

In perhaps the Gita's best-known image, Krishna compares the self moving to new bodies with a person changing clothes.

Just as one drops vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni, worn-out clothes, and puts on new ones, the dehin, the embodied self, leaves worn-out bodies and enters new ones. The comparison makes rebirth feel ordinary rather than frightening: changing clothes changes the covering, not the wearer, and Arjuna's grief rests on mistaking the person for the body.
