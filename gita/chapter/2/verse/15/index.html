Bhagavad Gita 2.15

Krishna

यं हि न व्यथयन्त्येते पुरुषं पुरुषर्षभ ।
समदुःखसुखं धीरं सोऽमृतत्वाय कल्पते ॥ २.१५ ॥

yaṃ hi na vyathayantyete puruṣaṃ puruṣarṣabha ।
samaduḥkhasukhaṃ dhīraṃ so'mṛtatvāya kalpate ॥

The steady person whom these do not disturb, finest of men, who stays the same in pain and pleasure, is fit for immortality.

हिन्दीहे पुरुषश्रेष्ठ, जिस धीर मनुष्य को ये विचलित नहीं करते, जो दुख और सुख में एक-सा रहता है, वही अमरता का अधिकारी होता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

If you run a shop or a small business, there will be record months and empty ones. Staying the same person through both, not swollen by one or crushed by the other, is the steadiness this verse praises.

Krishna says that the steady person who stays sama-duḥkha-sukha, 'the same in pain and pleasure', and is not shaken by them, is fit for amṛtatva.

The word literally means 'deathlessness', and here points to freedom from the round of birth and death rather than a long life. Being 'the same' does not mean feeling nothing; it means not being pushed around by what one feels.
