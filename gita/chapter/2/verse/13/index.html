Bhagavad Gita 2.13

Krishna

देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।
तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ २.१३ ॥

dehino'sminyathā dehe kaumāraṃ yauvanaṃ jarā ।
tathā dehāntaraprāptirdhīrastatra na muhyati ॥

Just as the embodied self passes through childhood, youth and old age in this body, so it passes on to another body. The steady person is not confused by this.

हिन्दीजैसे देहधारी आत्मा इस शरीर में बचपन, जवानी और बुढ़ापे से गुज़रती है, वैसे ही वह दूसरा शरीर पाती है। धीर पुरुष इससे मोहित नहीं होता।

In your life

Look at an old photo: the child, the young adult and today's face are all you. The body ages, but the one who looks out through it hasn't, so grey hair need not unsettle you.

Krishna compares death to ageing: as the embodied self passes through childhood, youth and old age, so it passes on to another body, and the steady are not troubled by this.

The dehin, 'the one who has a body', goes through those stages without becoming someone else, and dehāntara-prāpti, 'reaching another body', is presented as the same kind of change; the dhīra is the steady, clear-minded person. The comparison asks us to notice what stays constant through our own ageing.
