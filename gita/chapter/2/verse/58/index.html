Bhagavad Gita 2.58

Krishna

यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः ।
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ २.५८ ॥

yadā saṃharate cāyaṃ kūrmo'ṅgānīva sarvaśaḥ ।
indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyastasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā ॥

When he draws his senses back from their objects on every side, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, his wisdom is firmly established.

हिन्दीजैसे कछुआ अपने अंगों को सब ओर से समेट लेता है, वैसे ही जब वह अपनी इंद्रियों को विषयों से हटा लेता है, तब उसकी बुद्धि स्थिर होती है।

In your life

At a wedding buffet that tempts you well past hunger, practise drawing your senses back the way a tortoise pulls in its limbs. You decide when to engage, not the spread.

Krishna compares the person of steady wisdom to a tortoise: as it draws in its limbs, the wise draw the indriyas, the senses, back from their objects on every side.

The verse is about the ability to withdraw at will, not about never using the senses: attention that can be gathered in when needed instead of being dragged out by everything around.
