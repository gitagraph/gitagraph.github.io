Bhagavad Gita 2.2

Krishna

कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २.२ ॥

kutastvā kaśmalamidaṃ viṣame samupasthitam ।
anāryajuṣṭamasvargyamakīrtikaramarjuna ॥

Where has this confusion come from, Arjuna, at such a critical moment? It is not fitting for a noble person; it leads neither to heaven nor to honour.

हिन्दीहे अर्जुन, इस कठिन घड़ी में यह मोह तुम्हें कहाँ से आ घेरा? यह श्रेष्ठ लोगों को शोभा नहीं देता, न स्वर्ग दिलाता है, न यश।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you freeze on the day a hard decision is due, a good friend may ask plainly, 'Where did this come from, right now?' That blunt question is often kinder than comfort.

Krishna's first reply to Arjuna's collapse is a sharp question, not comfort.

He calls Arjuna's state kaśmala, a clouding or stain on the mind, and tests it against three standards Arjuna himself respects: it is not ārya, worthy of a noble person; it does not lead to heaven; it brings no honour. Before offering any philosophy, Krishna weighs the feeling by Arjuna's own values and finds it out of place at this critical moment.
