Bhagavad Gita 2.71

Krishna

विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान् पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः ।
निर्ममो निरहंकारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥ २.७१ ॥

vihāya kāmānyaḥ sarvān pumāṃścarati niḥspṛhaḥ ।
nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ sa śāntimadhigacchati ॥

The person who gives up all desires and moves about free from longing, without any sense of 'mine' and without ego, attains peace.

हिन्दीजो मनुष्य सारी कामनाओं को छोड़कर लालसा से रहित होकर विचरता है, जिसमें न ममता है न अहंकार, वही शांति पाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When your grown children start running the home you ran for decades, it stings as long as it still feels like 'mine'. Keep helping, but loosen that grip; that is where the peace is.

Krishna gathers his portrait into one sentence: the person who gives up all desires and moves about without longing, without 'mine' and without ego, attains peace.

The three negatives are niḥspṛha, without longing; nirmama, with no sense of 'mine'; and nirahaṃkāra, without the ego that says 'I am the doer'. Yet such a person still carati, moves about, living and acting in the world. Read today, it places restlessness less in what we have than in how possessively we hold it.
