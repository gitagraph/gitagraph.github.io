Bhagavad Gita 2.10

Sanjaya

तमुवाच हृषीकेशः प्रहसन्निव भारत ।
सेनयोरुभयोर्मध्ये विषीदन्तमिदं वचः ॥ २.१० ॥

tamuvāca hṛṣīkeśaḥ prahasanniva bhārata ।
senayorubhayormadhye viṣīdantamidaṃ vacaḥ ॥

Then, O Bharata, between the two armies, Hrishikesha, as if smiling, spoke these words to the despairing Arjuna.

हिन्दीहे भारत, तब दोनों सेनाओं के बीच शोक में डूबे अर्जुन से हृषीकेश ने मानो मुस्कुराते हुए यह बात कही।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When a friend calls you in pieces, your calm matters more than your panic. A steady, warm presence, even a gentle smile, tells them the situation can be seen more clearly.

Sanjaya sets the scene for the teaching itself: Krishna speaks madhye, in the middle, between the two armies, to a man sinking in despair.

The striking detail is prahasann iva, 'as if smiling' or 'almost laughing': not mockery, but the ease of someone who sees the situation more clearly than the one in distress. The whole Gita is spoken in that space between two armies, so it is a teaching given in the middle of a crisis, not at a distance from one.
