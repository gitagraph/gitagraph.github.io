Bhagavad Gita 2.14

Krishna

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः ।
आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥ २.१४ ॥

mātrāsparśāstu kaunteya śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkhadāḥ ।
āgamāpāyino'nityāstāṃstitikṣasva bhārata ॥

Contacts of the senses with their objects, son of Kunti, give rise to cold and heat, pleasure and pain. They come and go and do not last; bear them patiently, Bharata.

हिन्दीहे कुंतीपुत्र, इंद्रियों का विषयों से संपर्क ही सर्दी-गर्मी और सुख-दुख देता है। ये आते-जाते रहते हैं और टिकते नहीं; हे भारत, इन्हें धैर्य से सहो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A harsh word from your boss, a bad day, a good one: each passes like the weather. Feel it fully, but don't let one day decide how you see your whole life.

Turning from death to daily life, Krishna names the source of pleasure and pain: mātrā-sparśa, the contact of the senses with their objects.

Cold and heat, comfort and discomfort all arise from such contact, and all are āgamāpāyin, 'coming and going'. His instruction is titikṣā, patient endurance. This is not a call to feel nothing, but to recognise that passing sensations, pleasant or painful, do pass, and need not decide what we do.
