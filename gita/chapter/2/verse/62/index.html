Bhagavad Gita 2.62

Krishna

ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते ।
सङ्गात्संजायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥ २.६२ ॥

dhyāyato viṣayānpuṃsaḥ saṅgasteṣūpajāyate ।
saṅgātsaṃjāyate kāmaḥ kāmātkrodho'bhijāyate ॥

When a person dwells on sense objects, attachment to them arises; from attachment comes desire, and from desire comes anger.

हिन्दीविषयों का चिंतन करने वाले मनुष्य की उनमें आसक्ति पैदा होती है; आसक्ति से कामना जन्म लेती है और कामना से क्रोध पैदा होता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Scroll past a friend's holiday photos again and again and you start wanting, then resenting. The chain begins with what you keep dwelling on, so that is where to break it.

Krishna traces how dwelling on things in the mind leads, step by step, to attachment, desire and finally anger, a chain that continues into the next verse.

It starts with dhyāyataḥ, simply dwelling on objects; that breeds saṅga, attachment; attachment ripens into kāma, desire; and desire, often when it is thwarted, flares into krodha, anger. For a reader the useful point is where the chain breaks most easily: at its first link, in what we let the mind keep returning to.
