Bhagavad Gita 2.50

Krishna

बुद्धियुक्तो जहातीह उभे सुकृतदुष्कृते ।
तस्माद्योगाय युज्यस्व योगः कर्मसु कौशलम् ॥ २.५० ॥

buddhiyukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛtaduṣkṛte ।
tasmādyogāya yujyasva yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam ॥

One joined with this understanding leaves behind both good and bad deeds here. So devote yourself to yoga; yoga is skill in action.

हिन्दीइस बुद्धि से युक्त मनुष्य यहीं पुण्य और पाप दोनों से छूट जाता है। इसलिए योग में लग जाओ; कर्मों में कुशलता ही योग है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Whatever your trade, tailoring, teaching or writing code, you work with more skill when you're absorbed in the job rather than anxious about credit. That is skill in action.

Krishna says that one joined with understanding leaves behind both good and bad deeds here, and gives yoga its best-known description: karmasu kauśalam, 'skill in action'.

In context the skill is acting in a way that does not bind, so that such a person is freed from both sukṛta and duṣkṛta, the merit and the fault that actions are thought to store up. Read simply, yoga is the art of acting well without getting caught in what one does.
