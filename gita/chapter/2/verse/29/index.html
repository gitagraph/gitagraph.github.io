Bhagavad Gita 2.29

Krishna

आश्चर्यवत्पश्यति कश्चिदेनमाश्चर्यवद्वदति तथैव चान्यः ।
आश्चर्यवच्चैनमन्यः शृणोति श्रुत्वाप्येनं वेद न चैव कश्चित् ॥ २.२९ ॥

āścaryavatpaśyati kaścidenamāścaryavadvadati tathaiva cānyaḥ ।
āścaryavaccainamanyaḥ śṛṇoti śrutvāpyenaṃ veda na caiva kaścit ॥

One person sees this self as a marvel, another speaks of it as a marvel, another hears of it as a marvel — and even after hearing of it, no one truly knows it.

हिन्दीकोई इस आत्मा को आश्चर्य की तरह देखता है, कोई आश्चर्य की तरह इसका वर्णन करता है, कोई आश्चर्य की तरह इसके बारे में सुनता है — और सुनकर भी कोई इसे ठीक से नहीं जान पाता।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When someone describes a moment of deep peace, it can sound like a marvel or like nonsense. Either way, hearing about it is not knowing it; that comes only from your own quiet practice.

Krishna admits that the self is āścaryavat, 'a wonder': one person sees it as a marvel, another speaks or hears of it as a marvel, and even after hearing, no one truly knows it.

In plain terms, he concedes that hearing the teaching is not the same as knowing it; this kind of understanding does not arrive just because the words have been heard.
