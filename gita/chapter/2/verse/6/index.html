Bhagavad Gita 2.6

Arjuna

न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषाम- स्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ २.६ ॥

na caitadvidmaḥ kataranno garīyo yadvā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ ।
yāneva hatvā na jijīviṣāma- ste'vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ ॥

Nor do we know which is better for us — that we conquer them or that they conquer us. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and if we killed them we would not even wish to live.

हिन्दीहम यह भी नहीं जानते कि हमारे लिए क्या अच्छा है — हम उन्हें जीतें या वे हमें जीतें। जिन्हें मारकर हम जीना भी नहीं चाहेंगे, वही धृतराष्ट्र के पुत्र हमारे सामने खड़े हैं।

Arjuna admits he cannot tell whether winning or losing would be better, since victory would mean killing the very kin without whom he would not want to live.

The word garīyas, 'weightier, better', shows him weighing the two and finding neither heavier; the Dhārtarāṣṭras are Dhritarashtra's sons (from the Mahabharata: the cousins he grew up with). This is honest confusion rather than cowardice, and it prepares his request for guidance in the next verse.
