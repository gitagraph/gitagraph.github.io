Bhagavad Gita 6.2

Krishna

यं संन्यासमिति प्राहुर्योगं तं विद्धि पाण्डव ।
न ह्यसंन्यस्तसंकल्पो योगी भवति कश्चन ॥ ६.२ ॥

yaṃ saṃnyāsamiti prāhuryogaṃ taṃ viddhi pāṇḍava ।
na hyasaṃnyastasaṃkalpo yogī bhavati kaścana ॥

Know, Pandava, that what people call renunciation is in fact yoga, for no one becomes a yogi without first giving up self-willed intentions.

हिन्दीहे पांडव, जिसे लोग संन्यास कहते हैं, उसी को योग समझो; क्योंकि अपने संकल्पों को त्यागे बिना कोई भी योगी नहीं बनता।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

If you plan every detail of a holiday and then sulk when it rains, the plan has turned into a demand. Loosening your grip on how things must go is where calm begins.

Krishna tells Arjuna that what people call renunciation, saṃnyāsa, is really the same as yoga.

The bridge between them is saṃkalpa, the mind's self-willed planning and intending: no one becomes a yogi without giving that up. So the two paths meet in one inner step, not in leaving the world or staying in it, but in loosening the habit of constantly scheming for outcomes.
