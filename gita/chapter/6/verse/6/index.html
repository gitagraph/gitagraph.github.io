Bhagavad Gita 6.6

Krishna

बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जितः ।
अनात्मनस्तु शत्रुत्वे वर्तेतात्मैव शत्रुवत् ॥ ६.६ ॥

bandhurātmātmanastasya yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ ।
anātmanastu śatrutve vartetātmaiva śatruvat ॥

The self is a friend to the one who has conquered himself by himself; but for one who has not mastered himself, that very self behaves with hostility, like an enemy.

हिन्दीजिसने अपने द्वारा अपने आप को जीत लिया है, उसके लिए वह स्वयं ही अपना मित्र है; पर जिसने स्वयं को नहीं जीता, उसके लिए उसका अपना आप ही शत्रु की तरह वैर करता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

The voice that keeps saying 'tomorrow' about your side project is the same self that could get you started. Master it in small daily ways, and it turns from enemy into friend.

Krishna explains when the self is a friend and when an enemy: a friend to one who has mastered himself, hostile to one who has not.

The key word is jita, 'conquered' or 'mastered', set against anātman, one who has not mastered himself. In everyday terms, the same energies, habits and moods that serve us when they are steadied turn against us when nothing governs them; the difference lies in self-mastery, not in what we were given.
