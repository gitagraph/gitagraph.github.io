Bhagavad Gita 6.46

Krishna

तपस्विभ्योऽधिको योगी ज्ञानिभ्योऽपि मतोऽधिकः ।
कर्मिभ्यश्चाधिको योगी तस्माद्योगी भवार्जुन ॥ ६.४६ ॥

tapasvibhyo'dhiko yogī jñānibhyo'pi mato'dhikaḥ ।
karmibhyaścādhiko yogī tasmādyogī bhavārjuna ॥

The yogi is greater than ascetics, considered greater even than scholars, and greater than those devoted to ritual works; therefore, Arjuna, be a yogi.

हिन्दीयोगी तपस्वियों से श्रेष्ठ है, ज्ञानियों से भी श्रेष्ठ माना गया है और कर्मकांडियों से भी श्रेष्ठ है; इसलिए हे अर्जुन, तुम योगी बनो।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Knowing a lot or keeping strict rules is admirable, but steadily joining your mind to what matters most is greater. Aim to be the friend who lives it, not just the one who explains it.

Drawing the chapter toward its close, Krishna ranks the yogi above ascetics, scholars and those devoted to ritual works, and urges Arjuna to be a yogi.

The key word is adhika, 'greater'. Tapasvin, jñānin and karmin here suggest austerity, learning and rites each pursued on its own; the yogi of this chapter joins discipline, inner stillness and right action, and so becomes the model held up to Arjuna.
