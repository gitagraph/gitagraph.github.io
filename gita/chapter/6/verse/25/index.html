Bhagavad Gita 6.25

Krishna

शनैः शनैरुपरमेद्बुद्ध्या धृतिगृहीतया ।
आत्मसंस्थं मनः कृत्वा न किंचिदपि चिन्तयेत् ॥ ६.२५ ॥

śanaiḥ śanairuparamedbuddhyā dhṛtigṛhītayā ।
ātmasaṃsthaṃ manaḥ kṛtvā na kiṃcidapi cintayet ॥

↑ The sentence begins in 6.24

...one should gradually come to rest, with the intellect held firm by steadiness; fixing the mind in the Self, one should think of nothing else at all.

हिन्दी…धैर्य से थामी हुई बुद्धि के द्वारा धीरे-धीरे शांत होता जाए; मन को आत्मा में स्थिर करके और कुछ भी चिंतन न करे।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Lying awake at night with a racing mind, don't try to force it silent. Let it settle slowly, little by little, with patient steadiness, the way muddy water clears when left alone.

Completing the method begun in 6.24, Krishna says the meditator should come to rest gradually, with the intellect held firm by dhṛti, steadiness or patient resolve.

The repeated śanaiḥ śanaiḥ, 'slowly, slowly', sets the pace: stillness is approached, not forced. Once the mind is settled in the Self, one should think of nothing else at all. For a reader, it is a reminder that calm comes in stages, not in a single effort.
