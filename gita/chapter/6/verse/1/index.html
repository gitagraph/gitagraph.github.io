Bhagavad Gita 6.1

Krishna

अनाश्रितः कर्मफलं कार्यं कर्म करोति यः ।
स संन्यासी च योगी च न निरग्निर्न चाक्रियः ॥ ६.१ ॥

anāśritaḥ karmaphalaṃ kāryaṃ karma karoti yaḥ ।
sa saṃnyāsī ca yogī ca na niragnirna cākriyaḥ ॥

One who does the work that ought to be done without leaning on its fruits is both a renunciant and a yogi; not the one who has merely put out the sacred fire, nor the one who simply stops acting.

हिन्दीजो कर्मफल का सहारा लिए बिना करने योग्य कर्म करता है, वही संन्यासी है और वही योगी भी; केवल अग्नि त्याग देने वाला या कर्म छोड़ बैठने वाला नहीं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Retirement isn't a reason to stop doing anything useful. Teaching a grandchild or helping run the housing society, without looking for credit, is closer to real renunciation than doing nothing.

Opening chapter 6, Krishna redefines the renunciant: the true saṃnyāsī, and the true yogi too, is one who does the work that needs doing without leaning on its fruits.

He sets this against the niragni, one who has given up tending the sacred fires (traditionally a mark of formal renunciation), and the akriya, one who has simply stopped acting. Outward signs of renouncing are not the test; letting go of results inwardly is.
