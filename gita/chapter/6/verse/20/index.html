Bhagavad Gita 6.20

Krishna

यत्रोपरमते चित्तं निरुद्धं योगसेवया ।
यत्र चैवात्मनात्मानं पश्यन्नात्मनि तुष्यति ॥ ६.२० ॥

yatroparamate cittaṃ niruddhaṃ yogasevayā ।
yatra caivātmanātmānaṃ paśyannātmani tuṣyati ॥

That state where the mind, held in check by the practice of yoga, comes to rest, and where, seeing the Self through the self, one is content in the Self...

It continues in 6.21 →

हिन्दीवह अवस्था जहाँ योग के अभ्यास से रोका गया चित्त शांत हो जाता है, और जहाँ अपने द्वारा आत्मा को देखता हुआ मनुष्य आत्मा में ही संतुष्ट रहता है…

Krishna begins describing the state that meditation leads to: the mind, held in check by practice, comes to rest, and one is content in the Self, seeing the Self through the self.

This opens a single long sentence that runs to 6.23, where the state is finally named yoga. The key word is uparamate, 'comes to rest', as when an activity ceases of its own accord. Contentment here comes from within, not from anything gained outside.
