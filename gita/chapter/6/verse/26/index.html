Bhagavad Gita 6.26

Krishna

यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम् ।
ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत् ॥ ६.२६ ॥

yato yato niścarati manaścañcalamasthiram ।
tatastato niyamyaitadātmanyeva vaśaṃ nayet ॥

Whenever the restless, unsteady mind wanders off, from there one should draw it back and bring it under control in the Self alone.

हिन्दीयह चंचल और अस्थिर मन जहाँ-जहाँ भटककर जाए, वहाँ-वहाँ से इसे रोककर बार-बार आत्मा में ही वश में लाए।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

While your child tells you about their day, your mind slips off to tomorrow's work. Notice it and bring it back to them; each return is the whole practice.

In his instruction on meditation, Krishna says that whenever the restless mind wanders off, one should draw it back from there and settle it in the Self.

The key word is niyamya, 'having drawn it back'; the doubled yato yato … tatas tatas, 'wherever … from there', admits that this will happen again and again. Put simply, wandering is expected rather than a failure: the practice is the quiet, repeated return, each time from wherever the mind has gone.
