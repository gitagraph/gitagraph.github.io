Bhagavad Gita 6.27

Krishna

प्रशान्तमनसं ह्येनं योगिनं सुखमुत्तमम् ।
उपैति शान्तरजसं ब्रह्मभूतमकल्मषम् ॥ ६.२७ ॥

praśāntamanasaṃ hyenaṃ yoginaṃ sukhamuttamam ।
upaiti śāntarajasaṃ brahmabhūtamakalmaṣam ॥

Supreme happiness comes to this yogi whose mind is fully calm, whose restless passion has subsided, who is free of taint and has become one with Brahman.

हिन्दीजिसका मन पूर्णतः शांत है, जिसका रजोगुण शांत हो गया है, जो निष्पाप है और ब्रह्मरूप हो गया है, ऐसे योगी को उत्तम सुख प्राप्त होता है।

After advising that the wandering mind be brought back again and again (6.26), Krishna describes what comes to the yogi whose mind has fully calmed: the highest happiness.

The key term is brahmabhūta, 'one who has become Brahman'. Some traditions read this as realising identity with Brahman, others as attaining a nature like Brahman's, pure and untroubled. Either way, the calm described is the fruit of patience, not a feeling to be chased.
