Bhagavad Gita 6.38

Arjuna

कच्चिन्नोभयविभ्रष्टश्छिन्नाभ्रमिव नश्यति ।
अप्रतिष्ठो महाबाहो विमूढो ब्रह्मणः पथि ॥ ६.३८ ॥

kaccinnobhayavibhraṣṭaśchinnābhramiva naśyati ।
apratiṣṭho mahābāho vimūḍho brahmaṇaḥ pathi ॥

Mighty-armed one, having fallen from both, without a footing and bewildered on the path to Brahman, does he not perish like a scattered cloud?

हिन्दीहे महाबाहु, दोनों ओर से भटका हुआ, बिना आधार का और ब्रह्म के मार्ग में मोहित वह, क्या छितराए हुए बादल की तरह नष्ट तो नहीं हो जाता?

Arjuna sharpens his worry with an image: does such a person, fallen from both, perish like a cloud torn from the rest and scattered?

The key word is ubhaya-vibhraṣṭa, 'fallen from both'. It is commonly taken as having lost both worldly rewards and the goal of yoga, though commentators fill 'both' in differently. The fear is familiar: having given up one path for another, ending with neither.
