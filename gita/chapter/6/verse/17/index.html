Bhagavad Gita 6.17

Krishna

युक्ताहारविहारस्य युक्तचेष्टस्य कर्मसु ।
युक्तस्वप्नावबोधस्य योगो भवति दुःखहा ॥ ६.१७ ॥

yuktāhāravihārasya yuktaceṣṭasya karmasu ।
yuktasvapnāvabodhasya yogo bhavati duḥkhahā ॥

For one who is measured in eating and recreation, measured in effort at work, and measured in sleep and waking, yoga becomes the remover of sorrow.

हिन्दीजिसका आहार-विहार संतुलित है, कर्मों में जिसकी चेष्टा संतुलित है और जिसका सोना-जागना संतुलित है, उसके लिए योग दुःखों का नाश करने वाला बनता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

If you work late every night and call it dedication, notice that the verse asks for measured effort too. Rest and leisure in proportion are part of the practice, not a break from it.

Krishna turns the warning of the previous verse into a positive rule: yoga ends sorrow for the person who is measured in eating, leisure, effort at work, sleep and waking.

The repeated word is yukta, here meaning 'fitting, well-proportioned', from the same root as yoga itself. So balance in ordinary life is already part of the discipline, not a preparation for it; the practice begins with how one eats, works and rests.
