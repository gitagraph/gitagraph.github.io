Bhagavad Gita 6.35

Krishna

असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम् ।
अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते ॥ ६.३५ ॥

asaṃśayaṃ mahābāho mano durnigrahaṃ calam ।
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate ॥

There is no doubt, mighty-armed one, that the mind is unsteady and hard to rein in. Yet, son of Kunti, it can be held firm by persistent practice and by dispassion.

हिन्दीहे महाबाहु, निःसंदेह मन चंचल है और कठिनाई से वश में होता है; परंतु हे कुंतीपुत्र, अभ्यास और वैराग्य से यह पकड़ में आ जाता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When your mind keeps drifting to your phone while you work, don't give up after one try. Bring it back gently, again and again; that is the practice. And cut down on what keeps pulling it away; that is dispassion.

Krishna agrees that the mind is restless and hard to rein in, but says it can be steadied through abhyāsa, practice, and vairāgya, dispassion.

Arjuna has just compared holding the mind to holding the wind; abhyāsa is steady repeated effort, vairāgya a loosening of the pull that things have on us. Plainly put, a calm mind is not a starting point but is trained gradually, by returning to the practice again and again and by wanting less from what distracts.
