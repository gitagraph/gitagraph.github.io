Bhagavad Gita 6.32

Krishna

आत्मौपम्येन सर्वत्र समं पश्यति योऽर्जुन ।
सुखं वा यदि वा दुःखं स योगी परमो मतः ॥ ६.३२ ॥

ātmaupamyena sarvatra samaṃ paśyati yo'rjuna ।
sukhaṃ vā yadi vā duḥkhaṃ sa yogī paramo mataḥ ॥

Arjuna, the one who, by comparing others to himself, sees pleasure or pain everywhere as the same is regarded as the highest yogi.

हिन्दीहे अर्जुन, जो अपनी उपमा से सब जगह सुख हो या दुःख, समान रूप से देखता है, वह परम योगी माना गया है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Before passing on gossip about a colleague, ask how it would feel if the story were about you. Weighing another's hurt as your own is what this verse calls the highest yoga.

Closing his account of the yogi's vision, Krishna names the highest yogi: the one who, comparing others to himself, sees their pleasure and pain as he would his own.

The key word is ātmaupamya, 'taking oneself as the measure'. After the inward language of meditation, the account turns outward into empathy: inner stillness shows in how one meets the joy and pain of others.
