Bhagavad Gita 6.22

Krishna

यं लब्ध्वा चापरं लाभं मन्यते नाधिकं ततः ।
यस्मिन्स्थितो न दुःखेन गुरुणापि विचाल्यते ॥ ६.२२ ॥

yaṃ labdhvā cāparaṃ lābhaṃ manyate nādhikaṃ tataḥ ।
yasminsthito na duḥkhena guruṇāpi vicālyate ॥

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Grief after losing someone you love is natural and will come. The verse speaks of an inner steadiness, built slowly, that heavy sorrow can shake but not uproot.

Krishna gives two more marks of the state that meditation leads to: once it is gained, no other gain seems greater, and settled in it, one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow.

The key word is vicālyate, 'is moved, is shaken'. The verse does not say sorrow stops coming; it says sorrow no longer uproots the person. For a reader, this distinguishes steadiness from simply avoiding pain.
