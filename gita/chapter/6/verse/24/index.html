Bhagavad Gita 6.24

Krishna

संकल्पप्रभवान्कामांस्त्यक्त्वा सर्वानशेषतः ।
मनसैवेन्द्रियग्रामं विनियम्य समन्ततः ॥ ६.२४ ॥

saṃkalpaprabhavānkāmāṃstyaktvā sarvānaśeṣataḥ ।
manasaivendriyagrāmaṃ viniyamya samantataḥ ॥

Giving up entirely all desires that spring from mental intention, and reining in the whole crowd of the senses from every side by the mind alone...

हिन्दीसंकल्प से उत्पन्न होने वाली सारी कामनाओं को पूरी तरह त्यागकर, और मन से ही इंद्रियों के समूह को चारों ओर से वश में करके…

Krishna begins a two-verse method (6.24–25) for quieting the mind: give up entirely every desire that springs from saṃkalpa, the mind's habit of planning and intending, and rein in the senses from every side using the mind itself.

Having described where meditation leads (6.20–23), he now returns to how it is done. The senses are steadied from inside, by the same mind that usually chases what they bring.
