Bhagavad Gita 6.21

Krishna

सुखमात्यन्तिकं यत्तद् बुद्धिग्राह्यमतीन्द्रियम् ।
वेत्ति यत्र न चैवायं स्थितश्चलति तत्त्वतः ॥ ६.२१ ॥

sukhamātyantikaṃ yattad buddhigrāhyamatīndriyam ।
vetti yatra na caivāyaṃ sthitaścalati tattvataḥ ॥

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

The deepest happiness isn't in the taste of a meal or the thrill of a purchase. It can be understood but not grabbed, and once found, it keeps you from straying.

Continuing the description begun in 6.20, Krishna says that in this state one knows a boundless happiness that the intellect can grasp but the senses cannot reach.

The key word is atīndriya, 'beyond the senses': this happiness does not arrive through sight, taste or touch. Once settled there, one no longer drifts from what is real. The verse points to a joy that does not need anything to arrive from outside.
