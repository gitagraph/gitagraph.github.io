Bhagavad Gita 7.20

Krishna

कामैस्तैस्तैर्हृतज्ञानाः प्रपद्यन्तेऽन्यदेवताः ।
तं तं नियममास्थाय प्रकृत्या नियताः स्वया ॥ ७.२० ॥

kāmaistaistairhṛtajñānāḥ prapadyante'nyadevatāḥ ।
taṃ taṃ niyamamāsthāya prakṛtyā niyatāḥ svayā ॥

Those whose understanding has been carried off by this or that desire turn to other gods, following one observance or another, driven by their own nature.

हिन्दीजिनका ज्ञान तरह-तरह की कामनाओं ने हर लिया है, वे अपने स्वभाव से प्रेरित होकर अलग-अलग नियमों का पालन करते हुए दूसरे देवताओं की शरण लेते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you badly want something, you trust whoever promises it fastest: a course that guarantees a job, a scheme that doubles your money. Notice when the wanting, not your judgement, is choosing.

Krishna now turns to people whose understanding has been carried off by this or that desire, and who therefore turn to other gods, each following some observance, driven by their own nature.

The key phrase is hṛtajñāna, 'knowledge carried off', echoing 7.15; there it was māyā, here it is desire that does the carrying. The verse is descriptive: what a person wants, and the nature they bring with them (prakṛti), shapes where they look for help.
