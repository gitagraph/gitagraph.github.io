Bhagavad Gita 7.24

Krishna

अव्यक्तं व्यक्तिमापन्नं मन्यन्ते मामबुद्धयः ।
परं भावमजानन्तो ममाव्ययमनुत्तमम् ॥ ७.२४ ॥

avyaktaṃ vyaktimāpannaṃ manyante māmabuddhayaḥ ।
paraṃ bhāvamajānanto mamāvyayamanuttamam ॥

The undiscerning think of me, the unmanifest, as having taken on a manifest form, not knowing my higher being, which is imperishable and unsurpassed.

हिन्दीनासमझ लोग मुझ अव्यक्त को व्यक्त रूप में आया हुआ मानते हैं, क्योंकि वे मेरे अविनाशी और सर्वोत्तम परम भाव को नहीं जानते।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

It is easy to size up a new son-in-law or daughter-in-law by their clothes or accent. The verse warns against stopping at appearances; take time to know the person underneath.

Krishna says that the undiscerning take him, who is unmanifest, to be someone who has merely come into a visible form, because they do not know his higher being, imperishable and unsurpassed.

The pair is avyakta, 'unmanifest', and vyakti, 'manifestation'. The mistake described is judging by appearances alone: seeing the figure in front of them and stopping there, without grasping the larger reality the chapter has been describing (7.7, 7.12).
