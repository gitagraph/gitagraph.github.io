Bhagavad Gita 7.28

Krishna

येषां त्वन्तगतं पापं जनानां पुण्यकर्मणाम् ।
ते द्वन्द्वमोहनिर्मुक्ता भजन्ते मां दृढव्रताः ॥ ७.२८ ॥

yeṣāṃ tvantagataṃ pāpaṃ janānāṃ puṇyakarmaṇām ।
te dvandvamohanirmuktā bhajante māṃ dṛḍhavratāḥ ॥

But people of virtuous deeds whose wrongdoing has come to an end are freed from the delusion of opposites and worship me with firm resolve.

हिन्दीपरंतु जिन पुण्यकर्मी लोगों के पाप समाप्त हो गए हैं, वे द्वंद्व के मोह से छूटकर दृढ़ व्रत के साथ मुझे भजते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When you finally stop lurching between love and resentment towards an old rival, the mind gets steady enough to commit to what matters. Clearing the swing comes first.

Krishna describes those who break free: people of good deeds whose wrongdoing has come to an end are released from the delusion of opposites and worship him with firm resolve.

The key word is dṛḍhavrata, 'firm in vow', for a commitment that no longer wavers. The verse presents freedom from the pull of liking and disliking (7.27) as what makes steady devotion possible, and it frames that freedom as the fruit of how a person has lived.
