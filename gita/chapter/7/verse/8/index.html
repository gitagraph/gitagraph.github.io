Bhagavad Gita 7.8

Krishna

रसोऽहमप्सु कौन्तेय प्रभास्मि शशिसूर्ययोः ।
प्रणवः सर्ववेदेषु शब्दः खे पौरुषं नृषु ॥ ७.८ ॥

raso'hamapsu kaunteya prabhāsmi śaśisūryayoḥ ।
praṇavaḥ sarvavedeṣu śabdaḥ khe pauruṣaṃ nṛṣu ॥

Son of Kunti, I am the taste in water, the light in the moon and the sun, the syllable Om in all the Vedas, the sound in space, and the vigour in human beings.

हिन्दीहे कुंतीपुत्र, मैं जल में रस हूँ, चंद्रमा और सूर्य में प्रकाश हूँ, सब वेदों में ओंकार हूँ, आकाश में शब्द हूँ और मनुष्यों में पुरुषार्थ हूँ।

In your life

A glass of cold water after a sweaty commute, moonlight over a rooftop: the verse asks you to notice what is essential in plain things. Pause on one of them today instead of rushing past.

Krishna begins naming where he can be recognised in ordinary things: the taste of water, the light of moon and sun, Om in the Vedas, sound in space, and vigour in human beings.

Each thing is paired with its defining quality — water is known by its taste, space (kha) by sound — and praṇava is the sacred syllable Om. The divine is pointed to not somewhere far off but in the very quality that makes each thing what it is.
