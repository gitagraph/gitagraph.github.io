Bhagavad Gita 12.13

Krishna

अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च ।
निर्ममो निरहंकारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी ॥ १२.१३ ॥

adveṣṭā sarvabhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca ।
nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ samaduḥkhasukhaḥ kṣamī ॥

One who bears ill will toward no being, who is friendly and compassionate, free of 'mine' and of ego, the same in pain and pleasure, and forgiving…

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When an old friend says something hurtful about you in a group chat, forgiving doesn't mean pretending it didn't hurt. It means not carrying ill will, so the grudge doesn't become yours to hold for years.

Krishna begins his portrait of the devotee dear to him: one without ill will toward any being, friendly and compassionate, free of 'mine' and of ego, even in pain and pleasure, and forgiving.

Two key words are nirmama, free of 'mine', and nirahaṃkāra, free of 'I-making', the ego; answering Arjuna's question about the best worshipper, the sentence runs on into the next verse. In everyday terms, the list is about how a person treats others and holds their own life rather than about rituals.
