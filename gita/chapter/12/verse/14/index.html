Bhagavad Gita 12.14

Krishna

संतुष्टः सततं योगी यतात्मा दृढनिश्चयः ।
मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः ॥ १२.१४ ॥

saṃtuṣṭaḥ satataṃ yogī yatātmā dṛḍhaniścayaḥ ।
mayyarpitamanobuddhiryo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ ॥

↑ The sentence begins in 12.13

…ever content, a yogi who is self-controlled and firm in resolve, whose mind and understanding are offered to me — that devotee of mine is dear to me.

हिन्दी…जो सदा संतुष्ट रहता है, संयमी योगी है, दृढ़ निश्चय वाला है, और जिसने अपना मन व बुद्धि मुझे अर्पित कर दी है — ऐसा मेरा भक्त मुझे प्रिय है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Content with an ordinary salary, firm in your decisions, your heart and mind given to what matters: a quiet life like that is no small thing. It is exactly what this verse holds dear.

Krishna completes the opening sentence of his portrait: the devotee who is ever content, self-controlled and firm in resolve, with mind and understanding offered to him, is dear to him.

This is the refrain that recurs through the verses that follow, and the key phrase is mayy arpita-mano-buddhiḥ: both feeling and judgement turned in one direction. Plainly, the qualities sit side by side on purpose, contentment with firm resolve, so the steadiness described is settled but not passive.
