Bhagavad Gita 12.3

Krishna

ये त्वक्षरमनिर्देश्यमव्यक्तं पर्युपासते ।
सर्वत्रगमचिन्त्यं च कूटस्थमचलं ध्रुवम् ॥ १२.३ ॥

ye tvakṣaramanirdeśyamavyaktaṃ paryupāsate ।
sarvatragamacintyaṃ ca kūṭasthamacalaṃ dhruvam ॥

But those who worship the imperishable — that which cannot be defined, is unmanifest, reaches everywhere, is beyond thought, unchanging at the core, unmoving and constant…

It continues in 12.4 →

हिन्दीपरंतु जो उस अविनाशी की उपासना करते हैं — जो बताया नहीं जा सकता, अव्यक्त है, सर्वत्र व्याप्त है, सोच से परे है, कूटस्थ, अचल और स्थिर है…

Krishna turns to those who worship the imperishable, formless reality and describes what they meditate on, in a sentence that finishes in 12.4.

Each word strips away a quality one could grasp: anirdeśya, 'that cannot be pointed out', acintya, 'beyond thought', and kūṭastha, traditionally pictured as an anvil that stays unchanged while everything on it is shaped. Such an object gives the mind almost nothing to hold, which prepares the point of 12.5 that this path is harder.
