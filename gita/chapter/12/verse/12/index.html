Bhagavad Gita 12.12

Krishna

श्रेयो हि ज्ञानमभ्यासाज्ज्ञानाद्ध्यानं विशिष्यते ।
ध्यानात्कर्मफलत्यागस्त्यागाच्छान्तिरनन्तरम् ॥ १२.१२ ॥

śreyo hi jñānamabhyāsājjñānāddhyānaṃ viśiṣyate ।
dhyānātkarmaphalatyāgastyāgācchāntiranantaram ॥

Knowledge is better than practice; meditation surpasses knowledge; giving up the fruits of action surpasses meditation, and from that giving up, peace follows at once.

हिन्दीअभ्यास से ज्ञान श्रेष्ठ है, ज्ञान से ध्यान बढ़कर है, ध्यान से कर्मफल का त्याग बढ़कर है, और इस त्याग से तुरंत शांति मिलती है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

You can read a great deal and meditate daily and still lie awake over whether the house sale will go through. It is letting go of the outcome that brings peace, and it brings it at once.

Krishna ranks four disciplines and places giving up the fruits of action above them all, because peace follows from it at once.

The order is surprising: practice (abhyāsa), then knowledge (jñāna), then meditation (dhyāna), and highest, tyāga, letting go of results. Commentators explain this ranking in different ways. In plain terms, the verse says inner quiet depends less on which discipline one follows than on no longer clinging to how things turn out.
