Chapter 12 · 20 verses

Devotion

भक्तियोग

Is it better to worship the personal God or the formless imperishable? Both reach him, but devotion to the personal is easier for embodied beings. A ladder of practice follows, then the qualities of the devotee dear to Krishna: free of hatred, friendly, compassionate, equal in honour and dishonour.

साकार ईश्वर की उपासना श्रेष्ठ है या निराकार अक्षर की? दोनों उन तक पहुँचते हैं, पर देहधारियों के लिए साकार की भक्ति सरल है। फिर साधना की एक सीढ़ी, और कृष्ण को प्रिय भक्त के गुण: द्वेषरहित, मैत्रीपूर्ण, करुणामय, मान और अपमान में समान।

  1. 12.1

    Of those devotees who, ever joined in yoga, worship you in this way, and those who worship the imperishable, unmanifest reality, which have the better grasp of yoga?

  2. 12.2

    Those who fix their minds on me and worship me, always united with me and filled with the highest faith, I consider the most perfectly joined in yoga.

  3. 12.3

    But those who worship the imperishable — that which cannot be defined, is unmanifest, reaches everywhere, is beyond thought, unchanging at the core, unmoving and constant…

  4. 12.4

    …keeping the whole host of senses in check, even-minded toward everything, and devoted to the welfare of all beings — they too reach me alone.

  5. 12.5

    The struggle is far greater for those whose minds cling to the unmanifest, for the path to the unmanifest is hard for embodied beings to travel.

  6. 12.6

    But those who lay down all their actions in me, hold me as their highest goal, and worship me, meditating on me with undivided yoga…

  7. 12.7

    …for them, whose minds have entered into me, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and rebirth, Partha.

  8. 12.8

    Set your mind on me alone and let your understanding settle in me; after that you will live in me, without any doubt.

  9. 12.9

    If you cannot hold your mind steadily on me, then seek to reach me through the yoga of repeated practice, Dhananjaya.

  10. 12.10

    If you are unable even to practise, make work done for me your highest aim; by doing actions for my sake you will also reach perfection.

  11. 12.11

    And if you cannot do even this, then, taking refuge in my yoga and with self-control, give up the fruits of all your actions.

  12. 12.12

    Knowledge is better than practice; meditation surpasses knowledge; giving up the fruits of action surpasses meditation, and from that giving up, peace follows at once.

  13. 12.13

    One who bears ill will toward no being, who is friendly and compassionate, free of 'mine' and of ego, the same in pain and pleasure, and forgiving…

  14. 12.14

    …ever content, a yogi who is self-controlled and firm in resolve, whose mind and understanding are offered to me — that devotee of mine is dear to me.

  15. 12.15

    One who does not disturb the world and is not disturbed by the world, who is free from elation, resentment, fear and agitation — such a person is dear to me.

  16. 12.16

    One who expects nothing, is pure, capable, unconcerned, free from anxiety, and has given up every self-driven undertaking — that devotee of mine is dear to me.

  17. 12.17

    One who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor craves, and who has let go of both good and bad outcomes — that person, full of devotion, is dear to me.

  18. 12.18

    One who is the same toward enemy and friend, in honour and dishonour, the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, and free from attachment…

  19. 12.19

    …who takes blame and praise alike, is quiet, content with whatever comes, has no fixed home, and is steady in mind — that devoted person is dear to me.

  20. 12.20

    But those who, full of faith and holding me as supreme, devotedly follow this immortal teaching of dharma just as I have given it — those devotees are exceedingly dear to me.
