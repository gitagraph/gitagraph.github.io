Chapter 8 · The Imperishable Brahman

Chapter 8 · 28 verses

The Imperishable Brahman

अक्षरब्रह्मयोग

Arjuna asks what Brahman, adhyatma and karma are. Whatever one remembers at the moment of death, one goes to. So remember Krishna at all times and fight. The paths of light and of darkness after death.

अर्जुन पूछते हैं कि ब्रह्म, अध्यात्म और कर्म क्या हैं। मृत्यु के क्षण में मनुष्य जिसका स्मरण करता है, उसी को प्राप्त होता है। इसलिए हर समय कृष्ण का स्मरण करो और युद्ध करो। मृत्यु के बाद के प्रकाश और अंधकार के मार्ग।

  1. 8.1

    Purushottama, what is that Brahman? What is the inner self? What is action? What is called the physical realm, and what is said to be the divine realm?

  2. 8.2

    Madhusudana, who is the lord of sacrifice here in this body, and how? And how are you to be known at the time of death by those who have mastered themselves?

  3. 8.3

    Brahman is the imperishable, the supreme. Its presence as one's own being is called the inner self. The creative outpouring that brings beings into existence is called action.

  4. 8.4

    The physical realm is perishable existence; the divine realm is the cosmic Person; and the lord of sacrifice, best of the embodied, is I myself, here in this body.

  5. 8.5

    Whoever leaves the body at the time of death remembering me alone attains my being; of this there is no doubt.

  6. 8.6

    Whatever state of being a person remembers when finally giving up the body, son of Kunti, to that very state he goes, having always been shaped by it.

  7. 8.7

    So at all times remember me, and fight. With mind and intellect offered to me, you will surely come to me.

  8. 8.8

    With a mind trained by the yoga of practice and straying nowhere else, contemplating constantly, Partha, one reaches the supreme divine Person.

  9. 8.9

    One who meditates on the seer, the ancient one, the ruler, subtler than the subtlest, the sustainer of all, of inconceivable form, radiant as the sun and beyond all darkness...

  10. 8.10

    ...at the time of departure, with an unwavering mind, filled with devotion and by the strength of yoga, drawing the life-breath fully between the eyebrows — he reaches that supreme divine Person.

  11. 8.11

    That which knowers of the Veda call imperishable, which ascetics free of passion enter, and in search of which people live a life of celibacy — that goal I will describe to you briefly.

  12. 8.12

    Closing all the gates of the body, confining the mind in the heart, placing his life-breath at the crown of the head, and holding firm in yogic concentration...

  13. 8.13

    ...uttering the single syllable Om, which is Brahman, and remembering me, whoever departs, leaving the body, reaches the supreme goal.

  14. 8.14

    Partha, for the yogi who remembers me constantly, day after day, with a mind turned to nothing else, ever united — for him I am easy to reach.

  15. 8.15

    Having come to me, these great souls, who have reached the highest perfection, do not return to rebirth, that impermanent home of sorrow.

  16. 8.16

    Arjuna, all worlds, up to the realm of Brahma, are subject to return; but having come to me, son of Kunti, there is no further birth.

  17. 8.17

    Those who know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages, and that his night likewise ends after a thousand ages, truly understand day and night.

  18. 8.18

    At the coming of that day, all manifest things emerge from the unmanifest; at the coming of night they dissolve back into that very thing called the unmanifest.

  19. 8.19

    This same multitude of beings, coming into existence again and again, dissolves helplessly when night falls, Partha, and springs forth again when day dawns.

  20. 8.20

    But beyond that unmanifest there is another unmanifest, eternal existence, which does not perish when all beings perish.

  21. 8.21

    This unmanifest is called the imperishable, and it is said to be the supreme goal. Those who reach it do not return; that is my highest abode.

  22. 8.22

    That supreme Person, Partha, within whom all beings dwell and by whom all this is pervaded, can be reached through undivided devotion.

  23. 8.23

    Now, best of the Bharatas, I will tell you the times at which yogis who depart either do not return or do return.

  24. 8.24

    Fire, light, day, the bright half of the month, the six months of the sun's northern course — departing by this path, those who know Brahman go to Brahman.

  25. 8.25

    Smoke, night, the dark half of the month, the six months of the sun's southern course — taking this path, the yogi reaches the light of the moon and returns.

  26. 8.26

    These two paths of the world, the bright and the dark, are held to be eternal. By one a person goes and does not return; by the other he comes back again.

  27. 8.27

    Knowing these two paths, Partha, no yogi is ever misled. Therefore, Arjuna, be steadfast in yoga at all times.

  28. 8.28

    Whatever reward of merit is promised for study of the Vedas, for sacrifices, austerities and gifts — the yogi who knows this goes beyond all of it and reaches the supreme, primal abode.
