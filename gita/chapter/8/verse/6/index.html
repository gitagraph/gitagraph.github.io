Chapter 8 · The Imperishable BrahmanVerse 6 of 28

Bhagavad Gita 8.6

Krishna

यं यं वापि स्मरन्भावं त्यजत्यन्ते कलेवरम् ।
तं तमेवैति कौन्तेय सदा तद्भावभावितः ॥ ८.६ ॥

yaṃ yaṃ vāpi smaranbhāvaṃ tyajatyante kalevaram ।
taṃ tamevaiti kaunteya sadā tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ ॥

Whatever state of being a person remembers when finally giving up the body, son of Kunti, to that very state he goes, having always been shaped by it.

हिन्दीहे कुंतीपुत्र, मनुष्य अंत में जिस-जिस भाव को स्मरण करते हुए शरीर छोड़ता है, वह उसी-उसी भाव को प्राप्त होता है, क्योंकि वह सदा उसी भाव में रंगा रहा होता है।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

In a crisis, the mind reaches for whatever it has rehearsed every day, worry or trust. What you dwell on through ordinary weeks is what you'll have when things go wrong.

Krishna widens the point of 8.5 into a general rule: whatever state of being a person remembers when finally leaving the body, to that state they go.

The key phrase is sadā tad-bhāva-bhāvitaḥ, 'always steeped in that state': the last thought is not picked at random but grows out of what the mind has dwelt on for years. Plainly, the verse is less about one final moment than about the habits of attention that a whole life builds up.
