Bhagavad Gita 8.18

Krishna

अव्यक्ताद्व्यक्तयः सर्वाः प्रभवन्त्यहरागमे ।
रात्र्यागमे प्रलीयन्ते तत्रैवाव्यक्तसंज्ञके ॥ ८.१८ ॥

avyaktādvyaktayaḥ sarvāḥ prabhavantyaharāgame ।
rātryāgame pralīyante tatraivāvyaktasaṃjñake ॥

At the coming of that day, all manifest things emerge from the unmanifest; at the coming of night they dissolve back into that very thing called the unmanifest.

हिन्दीउस दिन के आरंभ होने पर सभी व्यक्त पदार्थ अव्यक्त से प्रकट होते हैं, और रात्रि के आने पर उसी अव्यक्त कहे जाने वाले में फिर विलीन हो जाते हैं।

Krishna describes what happens across Brahma's day and night: when that day begins, all manifest things emerge from the avyakta, the unmanifest, and when night comes they dissolve back into it.

Avyakta means what is not yet visible or distinct, rather like a seed before it sprouts. The picture is of a universe that neither begins from nothing nor ends in nothing, but by turns unfolds from a hidden source and folds back into it.
