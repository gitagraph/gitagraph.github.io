Bhagavad Gita 8.17

Krishna

सहस्रयुगपर्यन्तमहर्यद्ब्रह्मणो विदुः ।
रात्रिं युगसहस्रान्तां तेऽहोरात्रविदो जनाः ॥ ८.१७ ॥

sahasrayugaparyantamaharyadbrahmaṇo viduḥ ।
rātriṃ yugasahasrāntāṃ te'horātravido janāḥ ॥

Those who know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages, and that his night likewise ends after a thousand ages, truly understand day and night.

हिन्दीजो लोग जानते हैं कि ब्रह्मा का एक दिन हजार युगों का होता है और उसकी रात भी हजार युगों में पूरी होती है, वे दिन और रात के तत्त्व को जानते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

Looking at the night sky or at a thousand-year-old temple, your bad week shrinks to its real size. A long view of time is a good cure for short-sighted worry.

Krishna sets the cycle of worlds on a cosmic clock: a single day of Brahma lasts a thousand yugas, ages, and his night as long again.

Those who grasp this are aho-rātra-vidaḥ, 'knowers of day and night' in the full sense. Traditionally, each yuga here is a great age made of four lesser ages, putting a day of Brahma at billions of human years. The scale makes the point: even the creator's time is measured and passes.
