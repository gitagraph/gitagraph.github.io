Bhagavad Gita 8.20

Krishna

परस्तस्मात्तु भावोऽन्योऽव्यक्तोऽव्यक्तात्सनातनः ।
यः स सर्वेषु भूतेषु नश्यत्सु न विनश्यति ॥ ८.२० ॥

parastasmāttu bhāvo'nyo'vyakto'vyaktātsanātanaḥ ।
yaḥ sa sarveṣu bhūteṣu naśyatsu na vinaśyati ॥

But beyond that unmanifest there is another unmanifest, eternal existence, which does not perish when all beings perish.

हिन्दीपरंतु उस अव्यक्त से भी परे एक दूसरा सनातन अव्यक्त भाव है, जो सब प्राणियों के नष्ट होने पर भी नष्ट नहीं होता।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

When everything you built seems to be crumbling, a move, a closure, an ending, remember there is something in you that doesn't crumble with it.

Krishna now points beyond the cycle: past the unmanifest from which worlds arise there is another unmanifest existence, sanātana, eternal, which does not perish when all beings perish.

The verse uses avyakta in two senses: first the hidden source into which worlds fold back each night, then a deeper reality beyond even that. The distinction matters because the source of the cycle still belongs to it; only the second is untouched by its endings.
