Chapter 4 · Knowledge and Renunciation of Action

Chapter 4 · 42 verses

Knowledge and Renunciation of Action

ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोग

Krishna reveals that this teaching is ancient, passed from Vivasvan to Manu to Ikshvaku, and that he takes birth age after age whenever dharma declines. Inaction can be found in action. The fire of knowledge burns all karma to ash.

कृष्ण बताते हैं कि यह शिक्षा प्राचीन है, जो विवस्वान से मनु और मनु से इक्ष्वाकु तक पहुँची, और जब-जब धर्म की हानि होती है, वे युग-युग में जन्म लेते हैं। कर्म में अकर्म देखा जा सकता है। ज्ञान की अग्नि सभी कर्मों को भस्म कर देती है।

  1. 4.1

    I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvan; Vivasvan passed it to Manu, and Manu told it to Ikshvaku.

  2. 4.2

    Handed down in this way from one to the next, it was known to the royal sages. But over a long stretch of time, scorcher of foes, this yoga was lost here.

  3. 4.3

    That same ancient yoga I have told you today, because you are my devotee and my friend; this is indeed the highest secret.

  4. 4.4

    Your birth is recent, and Vivasvan's birth was long ago. How am I to understand that you taught it at the beginning?

  5. 4.5

    Many births of mine have passed, Arjuna, and many of yours too. I know them all, but you do not, scorcher of foes.

  6. 4.6

    Though I am unborn and my being never perishes, and though I am the Lord of all beings, I take birth by governing my own nature through my own power of maya.

  7. 4.7

    Whenever righteousness declines, Bharata, and unrighteousness rises, then I bring myself forth.

  8. 4.8

    To protect the good, to destroy those who do evil, and to establish righteousness firmly, I come into being age after age.

  9. 4.9

    One who truly understands my divine birth and action in this way is not born again on leaving the body, but comes to me, Arjuna.

  10. 4.10

    Free from craving, fear and anger, absorbed in me and taking refuge in me, many, purified by the discipline of knowledge, have reached my state of being.

  11. 4.11

    In whatever way people come to me, in that very way I receive them. Everywhere, Partha, people follow my path.

  12. 4.12

    Wanting success in their work, people here worship the gods, for in the human world success that comes from action arrives quickly.

  13. 4.13

    The fourfold order of society was created by me according to the division of qualities and actions. Though I am its maker, know me as the unchanging non-doer.

  14. 4.14

    Actions do not stain me, nor do I long for their fruit. One who knows me in this way is not bound by actions.

  15. 4.15

    Knowing this, the seekers of freedom of old also acted. So you too should act, as the ancients did long before.

  16. 4.16

    What is action, and what is inaction? Even the wise are confused about this. So I will explain action to you, and knowing it you will be freed from what is harmful.

  17. 4.17

    One must understand what action is, understand what wrong action is, and understand what inaction is; the way of action is deep.

  18. 4.18

    One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is wise among people; that person is steady in yoga and does every action fully.

  19. 4.19

    One whose every undertaking is free of desire and selfish intention, whose actions are burned up in the fire of knowledge, is called learned by the wise.

  20. 4.20

    Letting go of attachment to the fruit of action, always content and depending on nothing, such a person does nothing at all even while fully engaged in action.

  21. 4.21

    Without craving, with mind and body held in check, giving up all possessiveness, doing only the work the body needs, one incurs no sin.

  22. 4.22

    Content with whatever comes by chance, beyond the pairs of opposites, free of envy, and even-minded in success and failure, one is not bound even when acting.

  23. 4.23

    For one who is unattached and free, whose mind is settled in knowledge, and who acts for the sake of sacrifice, all action melts away completely.

  24. 4.24

    The offering is Brahman, the oblation is Brahman, poured by Brahman into the fire that is Brahman. One who sees Brahman in all action reaches Brahman alone.

  25. 4.25

    Some yogis perform sacrifice to the gods alone; others offer sacrifice itself as an oblation into the fire of Brahman.

  26. 4.26

    Some offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and the other objects into the fires of the senses.

  27. 4.27

    Others offer all the workings of the senses and of the life-breath into the fire of self-control, kindled by knowledge.

  28. 4.28

    Some offer wealth as sacrifice, some austerity, some the practice of yoga; and other striving people of firm vows offer study of scripture and knowledge.

  29. 4.29

    Others, devoted to breath control, offer the in-breath into the out-breath and the out-breath into the in-breath, restraining the flow of both.

  30. 4.30

    Others, eating in measure, offer the life-breaths into the life-breaths. All of these know what sacrifice is, and through sacrifice their impurities are worn away.

  31. 4.31

    Those who eat the nectar left over from sacrifice reach the eternal Brahman. This world is not for one who makes no sacrifice, best of the Kurus; how then the other?

  32. 4.32

    In this way many kinds of sacrifice are laid out in the words of the Veda. Know that all of them are born of action; knowing this, you will be set free.

  33. 4.33

    Scorcher of foes, the sacrifice of knowledge is better than any sacrifice of material things; for all action, Partha, finds its completion in knowledge.

  34. 4.34

    Learn it by bowing down, by sincere questioning, and by service. The wise who have seen the truth will teach you that knowledge.

  35. 4.35

    Knowing that, Pandava, you will not fall into such confusion again, and through it you will see all beings without exception in yourself, and then in me.

  36. 4.36

    Even if you were the worst of all sinners, you would cross over every wrong by the boat of knowledge alone.

  37. 4.37

    Arjuna, as a blazing fire turns firewood to ash, so the fire of knowledge turns all actions to ash.

  38. 4.38

    Truly, nothing in this world purifies like knowledge. One who is perfected in yoga finds it in oneself, in time.

  39. 4.39

    One who has faith, who is devoted to it and has mastered the senses, gains knowledge; and having gained it, soon reaches supreme peace.

  40. 4.40

    But one who is ignorant, without faith and full of doubt is ruined. For the doubter there is neither this world nor the next, nor any happiness.

  41. 4.41

    Actions do not bind one who has given up action through yoga, whose doubts are cut away by knowledge, and who is established in the Self, Dhananjaya.

  42. 4.42

    So, Bharata, cut through this doubt in your heart, born of ignorance, with the sword of knowledge of the Self. Take refuge in yoga, and stand up.
