Bhagavad Gita 4.31

Krishna

यज्ञशिष्टामृतभुजो यान्ति ब्रह्म सनातनम् ।
नायं लोकोऽस्त्ययज्ञस्य कुतोऽन्यः कुरुसत्तम ॥ ४.३१ ॥

yajñaśiṣṭāmṛtabhujo yānti brahma sanātanam ।
nāyaṃ loko'styayajñasya kuto'nyaḥ kurusattama ॥

Those who eat the nectar left over from sacrifice reach the eternal Brahman. This world is not for one who makes no sacrifice, best of the Kurus; how then the other?

हिन्दीयज्ञ से बचे अमृत रूपी अन्न को खाने वाले सनातन ब्रह्म को प्राप्त होते हैं। हे कुरुश्रेष्ठ, जो यज्ञ नहीं करता उसके लिए यह लोक भी नहीं है, तो परलोक कैसे होगा?

In your life

A life that only takes, from family, friends and the city, starts to feel thin even when it is full of things. Giving something back is what makes the rest worth enjoying.

Krishna says that those who live on what remains after sacrifice reach the eternal Brahman, while one who offers nothing has no place even in this world, let alone another.

The image is amṛta, nectar: what is left once one has given is what truly nourishes. It echoes 3.13 on eating the remnants of sacrifice. In everyday terms, a life of pure taking does not work even here and now.
