Bhagavad Gita 4.35

Krishna

यज्ज्ञात्वा न पुनर्मोहमेवं यास्यसि पाण्डव ।
येन भूतान्यशेषेण द्रक्ष्यस्यात्मन्यथो मयि ॥ ४.३५ ॥

yajjñātvā na punarmohamevaṃ yāsyasi pāṇḍava ।
yena bhūtānyaśeṣeṇa drakṣyasyātmanyatho mayi ॥

Knowing that, Pandava, you will not fall into such confusion again, and through it you will see all beings without exception in yourself, and then in me.

हिन्दीहे पांडव, उसे जानकर तुम फिर इस प्रकार मोह में नहीं पड़ोगे, और उसी से तुम सब प्राणियों को पूरी तरह पहले अपने में और फिर मुझमें देखोगे।

In your life

Once you truly see that the rude stranger and you want the same things, safety, respect, rest, the old confusion about 'us and them' doesn't return so easily.

Krishna promises Arjuna that once he has this knowledge he will not fall into such confusion again, and will see all beings in himself and then in Krishna.

Moha, 'delusion', recalls the bewilderment that made Arjuna set down his bow at the end of chapter 1. The sequence matters: first seeing others as continuous with oneself, then seeing all of it within Krishna, the one who is speaking. Traditions explain how 'yourself' and 'me' relate here in different ways.
