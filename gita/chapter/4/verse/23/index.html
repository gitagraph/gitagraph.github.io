Chapter 4 · Knowledge and Renunciation of ActionVerse 23 of 42

Bhagavad Gita 4.23

Krishna

गतसङ्गस्य मुक्तस्य ज्ञानावस्थितचेतसः ।
यज्ञायाचरतः कर्म समग्रं प्रविलीयते ॥ ४.२३ ॥

gatasaṅgasya muktasya jñānāvasthitacetasaḥ ।
yajñāyācarataḥ karma samagraṃ pravilīyate ॥

For one who is unattached and free, whose mind is settled in knowledge, and who acts for the sake of sacrifice, all action melts away completely.

हिन्दीजिसकी आसक्ति मिट गई है, जो मुक्त है, जिसका चित्त ज्ञान में स्थित है और जो यज्ञ के लिए कर्म करता है, उसके सारे कर्म पूरी तरह विलीन हो जाते हैं।

In your life · a reflection by Gitagraph

A teacher who prepares each lesson for the students' sake, not for an inspection report, finds the day's work leaves no residue at night. It is done, and it is let go.

Krishna says that the action of someone free of attachment, whose mind is settled in knowledge and who acts as a yajña, an offering, melts away entirely.

The verb pravilīyate means to dissolve completely, leaving nothing behind that binds. It also prepares what follows, where Krishna widens the meaning of sacrifice (4.24–30). In plain terms, work done as a contribution rather than a grab does not pile up as a burden on the doer.
