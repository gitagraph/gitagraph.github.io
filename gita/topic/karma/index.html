कर्म

Action

Action and its binding consequence. No one can remain without acting, even for a moment (3.5).

कर्म और उसका बंधनकारी परिणाम। कोई क्षण भर भी बिना कर्म के नहीं रह सकता (3.5)।

44 verses

  1. 2.47

    Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to inaction.

  2. 3.1

    Janardana, if you hold that understanding is higher than action, then why, Keshava, do you push me into this terrible deed?

  3. 3.4

    A person does not reach freedom from action merely by not starting any work, nor does one attain perfection by renunciation alone.

  4. 3.5

    No one can stay without acting even for a moment; every being is driven to act, whether it wants to or not, by the qualities (gunas) born of nature (Prakriti).

  5. 3.8

    Do the work that is yours to do, for action is better than inaction; without acting you could not even keep your body going.

  6. 3.9

    This world is bound by action, except action done as sacrifice. So, son of Kunti, act for that purpose, free of attachment.

  7. 3.14

    Beings arise from food, food comes from rain, rain comes from sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of action.

  8. 3.16

    Partha, whoever in this world does not keep turning the wheel set in motion this way, living in sin and taking delight only in the senses, lives in vain.

  9. 3.18

    Such a one gains nothing here by acting and loses nothing by not acting, and depends on no being for any purpose.

  10. 3.22

    Partha, there is nothing in the three worlds that I must do, nothing I lack that I need to gain; and yet I go on acting.

  11. 3.23

    For if I ever stopped acting tirelessly, Partha, people everywhere would follow my path.

  12. 3.27

    All actions are carried out entirely by the qualities of nature, but one whose mind is clouded by ego thinks, "I am the doer."

  13. 3.31

    Those who always follow this teaching of mine with faith and without finding fault are also released from the bonds of action.

  14. 4.12

    Wanting success in their work, people here worship the gods, for in the human world success that comes from action arrives quickly.

  15. 4.13

    The fourfold order of society was created by me according to the division of qualities and actions. Though I am its maker, know me as the unchanging non-doer.

  16. 4.14

    Actions do not stain me, nor do I long for their fruit. One who knows me in this way is not bound by actions.

  17. 4.16

    What is action, and what is inaction? Even the wise are confused about this. So I will explain action to you, and knowing it you will be freed from what is harmful.

  18. 4.17

    One must understand what action is, understand what wrong action is, and understand what inaction is; the way of action is deep.

  19. 4.18

    One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is wise among people; that person is steady in yoga and does every action fully.

  20. 4.32

    In this way many kinds of sacrifice are laid out in the words of the Veda. Know that all of them are born of action; knowing this, you will be set free.

  21. 4.37

    Arjuna, as a blazing fire turns firewood to ash, so the fire of knowledge turns all actions to ash.

  22. 5.8

    One who is steady and knows the truth thinks, "I do nothing at all," even while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping and breathing…

  23. 5.9

    …while speaking, letting go, taking hold, opening and closing the eyes, holding firmly that it is only the senses moving among their objects.

  24. 5.14

    The Lord does not create agency for the world, nor actions, nor the link between action and its fruit; it is nature that carries all this on.

  25. 7.29

    Those who take refuge in me and strive for release from old age and death come to know that Brahman, the whole of the inner self, and all action.

  26. 8.1

    Purushottama, what is that Brahman? What is the inner self? What is action? What is called the physical realm, and what is said to be the divine realm?

  27. 8.3

    Brahman is the imperishable, the supreme. Its presence as one's own being is called the inner self. The creative outpouring that brings beings into existence is called action.

  28. 9.9

    Those actions do not bind me, Dhananjaya, for I sit among them as one who is indifferent, unattached to those deeds.

  29. 15.2

    Its branches reach both downward and upward, fed and swelled by the gunas, with sense-objects as their young shoots. Below, in the world of humans, its roots stretch on and on, binding people through their actions.

  30. 16.24

    So let scripture be your authority in deciding what should and should not be done. Knowing what the scriptural injunctions say, you should do your work here.

  31. 18.3

    Some thinkers hold that action itself should be abandoned because it is tainted with fault; others say that acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity must never be abandoned.

  32. 18.5

    Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up; they ought to be performed, for sacrifice, giving and austerity purify even the wise.

  33. 18.11

    No embodied being can give up actions completely. But one who gives up the fruits of action is truly called a relinquisher.

  34. 18.12

    The fruit of action is threefold after death for those who have not relinquished: unwanted, wanted and mixed. For those who have renounced, there is none at all.

  35. 18.13

    Mighty-armed one, learn from me these five causes for the accomplishment of every action, as set out in the Sankhya teaching that brings action to its end.

  36. 18.14

    They are the seat of action (the body), the doer, the various instruments (the senses), the many distinct kinds of effort, and fifth among them, the divine factor (daiva).

  37. 18.15

    Whatever action a person begins with body, speech or mind, whether right or wrong, these five are its causes.

  38. 18.16

    That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees the pure Self alone as the doer, that dull-minded person does not see truly.

  39. 18.17

    One who has no sense of being the doer and whose understanding is not stained, even if he kills these people, does not kill and is not bound.

  40. 18.18

    Knowledge, the object known and the knower are the threefold impulse to action; the instrument, the act and the doer are the threefold basis of action.

  41. 18.23

    An action that is prescribed, done without attachment, without liking or dislike, by one who does not seek its fruit, is called sattvic.

  42. 18.41

    The duties of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, scorcher of foes, are divided according to the gunas that arise from their own nature.

  43. 18.48

    Kaunteya, one should not abandon the work that comes naturally to one, even if it has flaws; for every undertaking is wrapped in flaws, as fire is wrapped in smoke.

  44. 18.60

    Kaunteya, bound by your own work born of your nature, you will do, even against your will, what you now refuse to do out of delusion.
