Yajna

यज्ञ

Sacrifice, offering

Action done as an offering. Work done for yajna does not bind; all else does (3.9).

अर्पण के रूप में किया गया कर्म। यज्ञ के लिए किया गया कर्म नहीं बाँधता; बाकी सब बाँधता है (3.9)।

38 verses

  1. 3.9

    This world is bound by action, except action done as sacrifice. So, son of Kunti, act for that purpose, free of attachment.

  2. 3.10

    In the beginning, the Lord of creatures brought forth beings together with sacrifice and said: "Through this you will multiply; let this be the cow that yields all you wish for."

  3. 3.11

    "Nourish the gods with this, and let the gods nourish you. Sustaining one another, you will reach the highest good."

  4. 3.12

    "Pleased by sacrifice, the gods will give you the enjoyments you desire." One who enjoys their gifts without offering anything back to them is nothing but a thief.

  5. 3.13

    The good, who eat what remains after sacrifice, are released from all faults; but the wicked who cook only for themselves eat their own sin.

  6. 3.14

    Beings arise from food, food comes from rain, rain comes from sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of action.

  7. 3.15

    Know that action springs from the Veda, and the Veda springs from the Imperishable. So the all-pervading Brahman is always present in sacrifice.

  8. 3.16

    Partha, whoever in this world does not keep turning the wheel set in motion this way, living in sin and taking delight only in the senses, lives in vain.

  9. 4.23

    For one who is unattached and free, whose mind is settled in knowledge, and who acts for the sake of sacrifice, all action melts away completely.

  10. 4.24

    The offering is Brahman, the oblation is Brahman, poured by Brahman into the fire that is Brahman. One who sees Brahman in all action reaches Brahman alone.

  11. 4.25

    Some yogis perform sacrifice to the gods alone; others offer sacrifice itself as an oblation into the fire of Brahman.

  12. 4.26

    Some offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and the other objects into the fires of the senses.

  13. 4.27

    Others offer all the workings of the senses and of the life-breath into the fire of the yoga of self-control, kindled by knowledge.

  14. 4.28

    Some offer wealth as sacrifice, some austerity, some the practice of yoga; and other striving people of firm vows offer study of scripture and knowledge.

  15. 4.29

    Others, devoted to breath control, offer the in-breath into the out-breath and the out-breath into the in-breath, restraining the flow of both.

  16. 4.30

    Others, eating in measure, offer the life-breaths into the life-breaths. All of these know what sacrifice is, and through sacrifice their impurities are worn away.

  17. 4.31

    Those who eat the nectar left over from sacrifice reach the eternal Brahman. This world is not for one who makes no sacrifice, best of the Kurus; how then the other?

  18. 4.32

    In this way many kinds of sacrifice are laid out in the words of the Veda. Know that all of them are born of action; knowing this, you will be set free.

  19. 4.33

    Scorcher of foes, the sacrifice of knowledge is better than any sacrifice of material things; for all action, Partha, finds its completion in knowledge.

  20. 5.29

    Knowing me as the one who receives all sacrifice and austerity, the great Lord of all the worlds and the friend of every being, one attains peace.

  21. 7.30

    Those who know me together with the physical realm, the divine realm and the lord of sacrifice — they, with minds steadfast, know me even at the moment of departure.

  22. 8.2

    Madhusudana, who is the lord of sacrifice here in this body, and how? And how are you to be known at the time of death by those who have mastered themselves?

  23. 8.4

    The physical realm is perishable existence; the divine realm is the cosmic Person; and the lord of sacrifice, best of the embodied, is I myself, here in this body.

  24. 8.28

    Whatever reward of merit is promised for study of the Vedas, for sacrifices, austerities and gifts — the yogi who knows this goes beyond all of it and reaches the supreme, primal abode.

  25. 9.15

    Others worship me by offering the sacrifice of knowledge, seeing me as one, as distinct, and in many ways as the one whose faces are everywhere.

  26. 9.16

    I am the ritual and I am the sacrifice; I am the offering to the ancestors and the healing herb; I am the sacred chant, I am the clarified butter, I am the fire and I am the act of offering.

  27. 9.20

    Those versed in the three Vedas, who drink the soma and are cleansed of sin, worship me through sacrifices and pray for a path to heaven. Reaching the holy world of the king of the gods through their merit, they enjoy the divine pleasures of the gods in heaven.

  28. 9.23

    Even those who are devoted to other gods and worship them with faith, Kaunteya, are in fact worshipping me alone, though not in the proper way.

  29. 9.24

    For I am the enjoyer and the lord of every sacrifice. But they do not know me as I truly am, and so they fall back.

  30. 16.17

    Self-admiring, stubborn, drunk on wealth and status, they perform sacrifices in name only, out of show and without following the prescribed rules.

  31. 17.11

    Sacrifice offered according to scriptural rule by those who seek no reward, with the mind settled on the thought that it simply ought to be done, is sattvic.

  32. 17.12

    But sacrifice performed with an eye on the reward, or merely for show, know that to be rajasic, best of the Bharatas.

  33. 17.13

    Sacrifice that ignores the rules, distributes no food, lacks mantras, gives no fee to the priests and is devoid of faith is called tamasic.

  34. 17.24

    That is why those who speak of Brahman always begin their acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity, performed as the scriptures direct, by uttering 'Om'.

  35. 17.27

    Steadfastness in sacrifice, austerity and charity is also called 'Sat', and any action done for that purpose is likewise named 'Sat'.

  36. 18.3

    Some thinkers hold that action itself should be abandoned because it is tainted with fault; others say that acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity must never be abandoned.

  37. 18.5

    Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up; they ought to be performed, for sacrifice, giving and austerity purify even the wise.

  38. 18.70

    And whoever studies this sacred dialogue of ours, by him I will have been worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge; that is my view.
