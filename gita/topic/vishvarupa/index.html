विश्वरूप

The universal form

Krishna's cosmic form, with countless faces and arms, containing all worlds (Ch 11).

कृष्ण का विराट रूप, असंख्य मुखों और भुजाओं वाला, जिसमें सारे लोक समाए हैं (अध्याय 11)।

38 verses

  1. 11.3

    It is just as you have described yourself, Supreme Lord. Yet I long to see your divine, sovereign form, Supreme Person.

  2. 11.4

    If you think it is possible for me to see it, Lord, then, master of yoga, show me your imperishable Self.

  3. 11.5

    Behold, Partha, my forms by the hundreds and thousands, of many kinds, divine, of many colours and shapes.

  4. 11.6

    Behold the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Ashvins and the Maruts as well. Behold many wonders never seen before, Bharata.

  5. 11.7

    Here, today, Gudakesha, see the whole universe, moving and still, gathered in one place within my body, and whatever else you wish to see.

  6. 11.8

    But you cannot see me with these eyes of yours. I give you divine sight: behold my sovereign power.

  7. 11.9

    Having spoken thus, O King, Hari, the great lord of yoga, then revealed to Partha his supreme, sovereign form.

  8. 11.10

    It had countless mouths and eyes and countless wondrous sights, countless divine ornaments, and countless divine weapons held aloft.

  9. 11.11

    He wore divine garlands and garments and was anointed with divine fragrances. That god was all wonder, boundless, with faces turned in every direction.

  10. 11.12

    If the light of a thousand suns were to blaze up in the sky all at once, that might resemble the radiance of that great being.

  11. 11.13

    There, in the body of the god of gods, the Pandava then saw the entire universe, divided in many ways, gathered together in one place.

  12. 11.14

    Then Dhananjaya, overcome with wonder, his hair standing on end, bowed his head to the god and, with joined palms, spoke.

  13. 11.15

    In your body, O God, I see all the gods and the hosts of every kind of being: Brahma, the lord, seated on the lotus, all the sages and the divine serpents.

  14. 11.16

    I see you everywhere, endless in form, with countless arms, bellies, mouths and eyes. Lord of the universe, whose form is the universe, I see no end of you, no middle and no beginning.

  15. 11.17

    I see you wearing a crown, bearing a mace and a discus, a mass of light shining on every side, hard to look at, blazing all round like burning fire and the sun, beyond all measure.

  16. 11.19

    I see you without beginning, middle or end, of infinite power, with countless arms, the sun and moon your eyes, your mouth a blazing fire, scorching this whole universe with your radiance.

  17. 11.20

    The space between heaven and earth, and all the directions, are filled by you alone. Seeing this wondrous and terrible form of yours, great soul, the three worlds tremble.

  18. 11.21

    Hosts of gods are entering into you; some, afraid, praise you with joined palms. Hosts of great sages and perfected ones cry 'May all be well!' and praise you with abundant hymns.

  19. 11.22

    The Rudras, Adityas, Vasus and Sadhyas, the Vishvadevas, the two Ashvins, the Maruts and the ancestors, and hosts of Gandharvas, Yakshas, demons and perfected ones, all gaze at you in amazement.

  20. 11.23

    Seeing your immense form, mighty-armed one, with its many mouths and eyes, many arms, thighs and feet, many bellies, and terrifying with many tusks, the worlds are shaken, and so am I.

  21. 11.24

    Seeing you touching the sky, blazing, of many colours, with mouths gaping and huge eyes aflame, my heart shakes within me, Vishnu, and I find neither courage nor calm.

  22. 11.25

    Seeing your mouths, terrible with tusks and like the fire at the end of time, I lose all sense of direction and find no peace. Be gracious, lord of gods, refuge of the world.

  23. 11.26

    And all these sons of Dhritarashtra, along with the hosts of kings, Bhishma, Drona and that charioteer's son Karna too, together with the chief warriors on our side as well,

  24. 11.27

    are rushing into your fearful mouths, terrible with tusks. Some are seen caught between your teeth, their heads crushed to powder.

  25. 11.28

    As the many currents of rivers race headlong toward the sea, so these heroes of the human world are entering your flaming mouths.

  26. 11.29

    As moths rush with ever greater speed into a blazing fire to their destruction, so these people rush with ever greater speed into your mouths to their destruction.

  27. 11.30

    Devouring the worlds on every side with your flaming mouths, you lick your lips. Your fierce rays fill the whole universe with their brilliance and scorch it, Vishnu.

  28. 11.31

    Tell me who you are, in this fierce form. I bow to you, best of gods; be gracious. I wish to know you, the primal one, for I do not understand what you are doing.

  29. 11.32

    I am Time, grown mighty, the destroyer of worlds, now set to sweep these people away. Even without you, none of the warriors drawn up in the opposing ranks will survive.

  30. 11.36

    It is fitting, Hrishikesha, that the world rejoices and delights in praising you; that terrified demons flee in all directions, and all the hosts of perfected ones bow to you.

  31. 11.45

    I am thrilled to have seen what was never seen before, yet my mind is shaken with fear. Show me your former form, O God; be gracious, lord of gods, refuge of the world.

  32. 11.46

    I wish to see you just as before, wearing your crown, holding the mace and the discus. Take on that four-armed form again, O thousand-armed one, whose body is the universe.

  33. 11.47

    Pleased with you, Arjuna, through my own power I have shown you this supreme form, radiant, universal, infinite and primal, which no one but you has ever seen before.

  34. 11.48

    Not by the Vedas, not by sacrifices or study, not by gifts, not by rituals and not by severe austerities can I be seen in this form in the world of men by anyone but you, great hero of the Kurus.

  35. 11.49

    Do not be afraid and do not be bewildered at seeing this terrible form of mine. Free from fear and glad at heart, look once more upon that familiar form of mine.

  36. 11.52

    This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to see. Even the gods always long for a sight of it.

  37. 11.53

    Not through the Vedas, not through austerity, not through charity and not through sacrifice can I be seen in the way you have seen me.

  38. 18.77

    And as I recall again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again.
