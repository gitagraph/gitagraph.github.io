Cycle of rebirth

As one discards old clothes and puts on new, the Self discards worn-out bodies (2.22).

जैसे मनुष्य पुराने वस्त्र छोड़कर नए पहनता है, वैसे ही आत्मा जीर्ण शरीर छोड़कर नया धारण करती है (2.22)।

25 verses

  1. 2.13

    Just as the embodied self passes through childhood, youth and old age in this body, so it passes on to another body. The steady person is not confused by this.

  2. 2.22

    As a person discards worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self discards worn-out bodies and enters new ones.

  3. 2.27

    For death is certain for whoever is born, and birth is certain for whoever dies. So you should not grieve over what cannot be avoided.

  4. 2.28

    Beings are unmanifest in their beginning, manifest in their middle, and unmanifest again at their end, Bharata. What is there to lament in that?

  5. 6.40

    Partha, there is no ruin for him either in this world or the next; for, my friend, no one who does good ever comes to a bad end.

  6. 6.41

    One who has fallen from yoga reaches the worlds of the virtuous, lives there for many long years, and is then born into the home of pure and prosperous people.

  7. 6.42

    Or he is born into a family of wise yogis itself; but such a birth is very hard to come by in this world.

  8. 6.43

    There he regains the understanding he had developed in his former body, and from that point, joy of the Kurus, he strives again, even harder, for perfection.

  9. 8.6

    Whatever state of being a person remembers when finally giving up the body, son of Kunti, to that very state he goes, having always been shaped by it.

  10. 8.15

    Having come to me, these great souls, who have reached the highest perfection, do not return to rebirth, that impermanent home of sorrow.

  11. 8.16

    Arjuna, all worlds, up to the realm of Brahma, are subject to return; but having come to me, son of Kunti, there is no further birth.

  12. 8.19

    This same multitude of beings, coming into existence again and again, dissolves helplessly when night falls, Partha, and springs forth again when day dawns.

  13. 8.25

    Smoke, night, the dark half of the month, the six months of the sun's southern course — taking this path, the yogi reaches the light of the moon and returns.

  14. 8.26

    These two paths of the world, the bright and the dark, are held to be eternal. By one a person goes and does not return; by the other he comes back again.

  15. 9.3

    People who have no faith in this teaching, Parantapa, do not reach me; they turn back onto the road of death and rebirth.

  16. 9.21

    Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they return to the world of mortals once their merit is spent. In this way, following the ritual dharma of the three Vedas and craving pleasures, they keep going and coming.

  17. 12.7

    …for them, whose minds have entered into me, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and rebirth, Partha.

  18. 13.21

    Dwelling in Prakriti, Purusha experiences the gunas born of Prakriti; its attachment to the gunas is the cause of its births in good and evil wombs.

  19. 14.2

    Those who take refuge in this knowledge come to share my nature; they are not born even at the creation of the world, nor shaken at its dissolution.

  20. 14.15

    Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; dying in tamas, one is born in deluded wombs.

  21. 15.1

    They speak of an imperishable ashvattha (sacred fig) tree whose roots point upward and whose branches spread downward. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns, and whoever understands this tree truly knows the Veda.

  22. 15.2

    Its branches reach both downward and upward, fed and swelled by the gunas, with sense-objects as their young shoots. Below, in the world of humans, its roots stretch on and on, binding people through their actions.

  23. 15.8

    Whenever this lord of the body takes up a body or leaves one, it carries these along with it, the way the wind carries scents away from their source.

  24. 16.19

    These hateful, cruel, inauspicious people, the lowest of humans, I hurl again and again into demonic wombs within the cycles of birth and death.

  25. 16.20

    Having entered demonic wombs, deluded birth after birth, Kaunteya, they never reach me and sink from there to an even lower state.
