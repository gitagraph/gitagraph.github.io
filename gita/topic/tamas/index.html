तमस्

Inertia · darkness

Born of ignorance; binds through heedlessness, laziness and sleep (14.8).

अज्ञान से उत्पन्न; प्रमाद, आलस्य और निद्रा से बाँधता है (14.8)।

26 verses

  1. 7.12

    Whatever states of being there are — sattvic, rajasic or tamasic — know that they come from me alone. Yet I am not in them; they are in me.

  2. 14.5

    Sattva, rajas and tamas — these gunas born of Prakriti bind the imperishable embodied self to the body, mighty-armed one.

  3. 14.8

    Know tamas to be born of ignorance, deluding all embodied beings; it binds them, Bharata, through carelessness, sloth and sleep.

  4. 14.9

    Sattva attaches one to happiness and rajas to action, Bharata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches one to carelessness.

  5. 14.10

    Sattva prevails by overpowering rajas and tamas, Bharata; rajas prevails by overpowering sattva and tamas; and tamas likewise by overpowering sattva and rajas.

  6. 14.13

    Darkness of mind, inertia, carelessness and delusion — these arise when tamas has grown strong, joy of the Kurus.

  7. 14.15

    Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; dying in tamas, one is born in deluded wombs.

  8. 14.16

    The fruit of good action, it is said, is sattvic and pure; the fruit of rajas is pain, and the fruit of tamas is ignorance.

  9. 14.17

    From sattva comes knowledge, and from rajas greed; from tamas come carelessness and delusion, and ignorance as well.

  10. 14.18

    Those established in sattva rise upward; the rajasic stay in the middle; the tamasic, sunk in the lowest of the gunas, go downward.

  11. 17.2

    The faith of embodied beings is of three kinds, born of each one's own nature: sattvic, rajasic and tamasic. Hear about it.

  12. 17.4

    Sattvic people worship the gods; rajasic people worship yakshas and rakshasas; and tamasic people worship ghosts and hosts of spirits.

  13. 17.7

    Even the food each person likes is of three kinds, and so are sacrifice, austerity and charity. Hear how they differ.

  14. 17.10

    Food that is stale, tasteless, putrid, kept overnight, left over by others, or impure is dear to tamasic people.

  15. 17.13

    Sacrifice that ignores the rules, distributes no food, lacks mantras, gives no fee to the priests and is devoid of faith is called tamasic.

  16. 17.19

    Austerity undertaken out of a foolish obstinacy, through torment of oneself, or in order to ruin another, is declared tamasic.

  17. 17.22

    A gift given at the wrong place and time, to unworthy people, without respect and with contempt, is declared tamasic.

  18. 18.7

    It is not right to renounce an action that is one's duty. Giving it up out of delusion is called tamasic relinquishment.

  19. 18.19

    Knowledge, action and the doer are each said to be of three kinds, differing according to the gunas, in the analysis of the gunas. Hear about them as they are.

  20. 18.22

    And the knowledge that clings to one single thing as if it were the whole, without reason, without grasp of the truth, and narrow, is declared tamasic.

  21. 18.25

    An action undertaken out of delusion, without regard for its consequences, loss, harm to others, or one's own capacity, is called tamasic.

  22. 18.28

    A doer who is undisciplined, crude, stubborn, deceitful, malicious, lazy, gloomy and given to delay, is called tamasic.

  23. 18.29

    Now hear, Dhananjaya, the threefold division of understanding and of resolve according to the gunas, which I will explain fully and one by one.

  24. 18.32

    The understanding that, wrapped in darkness, takes adharma for dharma and sees everything upside down, that understanding, Partha, is tamasic.

  25. 18.35

    The resolve by which a dull-witted person refuses to let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency and arrogance, that resolve, Partha, is tamasic.

  26. 18.39

    The happiness that deludes the self both at the beginning and in its outcome, arising from sleep, sloth and carelessness, is declared tamasic.
