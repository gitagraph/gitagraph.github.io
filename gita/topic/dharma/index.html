धर्म

Right order, duty

The order that upholds the world and one's rightful duty within it. The Gita opens on 'the field of dharma'.

वह व्यवस्था जो संसार को धारण करती है, और उसमें मनुष्य का उचित कर्तव्य। गीता 'धर्मक्षेत्र' से आरंभ होती है।

22 verses

  1. 1.1

    On the field of dharma, at Kurukshetra, my sons and the sons of Pandu gathered there eager to fight. What did they do, Sanjaya?

  2. 1.40

    When a family is destroyed, its age-old family traditions perish; and when dharma is lost, lawlessness overwhelms the whole family.

  3. 1.41

    When lawlessness prevails, Krishna, the women of the family go astray; and when the women go astray, Varshneya, the social order is confused.

  4. 1.42

    This confusion leads both the family and its destroyers to hell, for their ancestors fall, deprived of the offerings of rice and water.

  5. 1.43

    Through these wrongs of the family-destroyers, which cause confusion of the social order, the enduring duties of caste and family are swept away.

  6. 1.44

    We have heard, Janardana, that people whose family traditions have been destroyed dwell in hell for an indefinite time.

  7. 2.7

    My nature is overcome by the flaw of faint-heartedness, and my mind is confused about dharma. I ask you: tell me clearly what is truly good for me. I am your disciple; I have come to you for refuge — guide me.

  8. 2.31

    Considering your own duty, too, you should not waver. For a kshatriya there is nothing better than a righteous war.

  9. 2.33

    But if you will not fight this righteous battle, then, having abandoned your duty and your honour, you will incur sin.

  10. 4.7

    Whenever righteousness declines, Bharata, and unrighteousness rises, then I bring myself forth.

  11. 4.8

    To protect the good, to destroy those who do evil, and to establish righteousness firmly again, I come into being age after age.

  12. 7.11

    In the strong I am strength free of craving and attachment; and in beings, best of the Bharatas, I am desire that does not go against dharma.

  13. 9.31

    He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace. Declare it boldly, Kaunteya: my devotee never perishes.

  14. 11.18

    You are the imperishable, the supreme one to be known; you are the final resting place of this universe. You are the undying guardian of eternal dharma; you are, I believe, the everlasting Person.

  15. 12.20

    But those who, full of faith and holding me as supreme, devotedly follow this immortal teaching of dharma just as I have given it — those devotees are exceedingly dear to me.

  16. 16.23

    One who sets aside the guidance of scripture and acts on the impulse of desire attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest goal.

  17. 16.24

    So let scripture be your authority in deciding what should and should not be done. Knowing what the scriptural injunctions say, you should do your work here.

  18. 18.31

    The understanding by which one judges dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done, in a distorted way, that understanding, Partha, is rajasic.

  19. 18.32

    The understanding that, wrapped in darkness, takes adharma for dharma and sees everything upside down, that understanding, Partha, is tamasic.

  20. 18.47

    Better one's own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Doing the work set by one's own nature, one incurs no sin.

  21. 18.66

    Letting go of all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.

  22. 18.78

    Wherever there is Krishna, the lord of yoga, and wherever there is Partha, the archer, there, I am sure, are fortune, victory, prosperity and steady good conduct.
