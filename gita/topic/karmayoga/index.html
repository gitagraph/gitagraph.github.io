Karma Yoga

कर्मयोग

Path of action

Doing one's duty fully while giving up attachment to its results, offering the work rather than owning it.

अपना कर्तव्य पूरी तरह निभाना और उसके फल की आसक्ति छोड़ देना; कर्म को अपना मानने के बजाय उसे अर्पित करना।

34 verses

  1. 2.39

    This understanding has been explained to you in terms of Sankhya; now hear it in terms of yoga. Joined with this understanding, Partha, you will throw off the bondage of action.

  2. 2.40

    On this path no effort is lost and no reverse occurs. Even a little of this practice protects one from great fear.

  3. 2.48

    Established in yoga, do your work, Dhananjaya, giving up attachment and staying even-minded in success and failure. This evenness is called yoga.

  4. 2.49

    Action done for its result is far inferior to the yoga of understanding, Dhananjaya. Seek refuge in that understanding; pitiable are those whose motive is the fruit.

  5. 2.50

    One joined with this understanding leaves behind both good and bad deeds here. So devote yourself to yoga; yoga is skill in action.

  6. 3.3

    Sinless one, long ago I taught that in this world there are two ways of steady practice: the path of knowledge for those given to reflection, and the path of action for those given to yoga.

  7. 3.7

    But one who reins in the senses with the mind, Arjuna, and without attachment takes up the yoga of action through the organs of action, stands far above.

  8. 3.19

    So always do the work that must be done, without attachment; by acting unattached a person reaches the Supreme.

  9. 3.20

    Janaka and others reached perfection through action alone. And looking to the welfare of the world too, you should act.

  10. 3.26

    The wise should not unsettle the minds of the ignorant who are attached to action. Acting with discipline themselves, they should lead others to take joy in all their work.

  11. 4.1

    I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvan; Vivasvan passed it to Manu, and Manu told it to Ikshvaku.

  12. 4.15

    Knowing this, the seekers of freedom of old also acted. So you too should act, as the ancients did long before.

  13. 4.18

    One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is wise among people; that person is steady in yoga and does every action fully.

  14. 4.41

    Actions do not bind one who has given up action through yoga, whose doubts are cut away by knowledge, and who is established in the Self, Dhananjaya.

  15. 4.42

    So, Bharata, cut through this doubt in your heart, born of ignorance, with the sword of knowledge of the Self. Take refuge in yoga, and stand up.

  16. 5.1

    Krishna, you praise the renunciation of action, and then again the yoga of action. Tell me with certainty which one of the two is better.

  17. 5.2

    Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good, but of the two, the yoga of action is better than renouncing action.

  18. 5.4

    Only the naive, not the wise, speak of the path of knowledge and the path of yoga as different. One who is truly settled in either one gains the fruit of both.

  19. 5.5

    The state reached by the followers of knowledge is reached by the followers of yoga as well. One who sees knowledge and yoga as one truly sees.

  20. 5.6

    But renunciation, mighty-armed one, is hard to attain without yoga; a sage who is steady in yoga reaches Brahman before long.

  21. 5.7

    Steady in yoga, pure in heart, master of mind and senses, whose self has become the self of all beings, such a person is not stained even while acting.

  22. 5.11

    Giving up attachment, yogis act with the body, the mind, the intellect, and even the senses alone, for the purification of the self.

  23. 6.1

    One who does the work that ought to be done without leaning on its fruits is both a renunciant and a yogi; not the one who has merely put out the sacred fire, nor the one who simply stops acting.

  24. 6.2

    Know, Pandava, that what people call renunciation is in fact yoga, for no one becomes a yogi without first giving up self-willed intentions.

  25. 6.3

    For the sage who wants to climb to yoga, action is said to be the means; for that same person once established in yoga, calm is said to be the means.

  26. 9.27

    Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in the fire, whatever you give and whatever discipline you practise, Kaunteya, do it as an offering to me.

  27. 11.33

    So rise up and win glory. Defeat your enemies and enjoy a flourishing kingdom. They have already been slain by me; be merely the instrument, Savyasachin.

  28. 11.55

    One who works for me, holds me as the highest goal, is devoted to me, is free from attachment and bears no ill will toward any being: that person comes to me, Pandava.

  29. 12.10

    If you are unable even to practise, make work done for me your highest aim; by doing actions for my sake you will also reach perfection.

  30. 13.24

    Some see the Self within themselves by their own self through meditation; others by the path of Sankhya; and still others by the yoga of action.

  31. 18.45

    A person devoted to his own work attains perfection. Hear how one who is intent on his own work reaches that perfection.

  32. 18.46

    By worshipping, through his own work, the one from whom all beings come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a person attains perfection.

  33. 18.56

    Though always performing all actions, one who takes refuge in me reaches, by my grace, the eternal, imperishable abode.

  34. 18.57

    Mentally offering all actions to me, holding me as your highest goal, and resting on the yoga of understanding, keep your mind fixed on me always.
