Rajas

रजस्

Passion · activity

Born of craving and attachment; binds through attachment to action (14.7).

तृष्णा और आसक्ति से उत्पन्न; कर्म की आसक्ति से बाँधता है (14.7)।

28 verses

  1. 3.37

    It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of passion; it devours without end and is the great evil. Know it as the enemy here.

  2. 6.27

    Supreme happiness comes to this yogi whose mind is fully calm, whose restless passion has subsided, who is free of taint and has become one with Brahman.

  3. 7.12

    Whatever states of being there are — sattvic, rajasic or tamasic — know that they come from me alone. Yet I am not in them; they are in me.

  4. 14.5

    Sattva, rajas and tamas — these gunas born of Prakriti bind the imperishable embodied self to the body, mighty-armed one.

  5. 14.7

    Know rajas to be of the nature of passion, springing from craving and attachment; it binds the embodied self, Kaunteya, through attachment to action.

  6. 14.9

    Sattva attaches one to happiness and rajas to action, Bharata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches one to carelessness.

  7. 14.10

    Sattva prevails by overpowering rajas and tamas, Bharata; rajas prevails by overpowering sattva and tamas; and tamas likewise by overpowering sattva and rajas.

  8. 14.12

    Greed, busy activity, the launching of ventures, restlessness and longing — these arise when rajas has grown strong, best of the Bharatas.

  9. 14.15

    Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; dying in tamas, one is born in deluded wombs.

  10. 14.16

    The fruit of good action, it is said, is sattvic and pure; the fruit of rajas is pain, and the fruit of tamas is ignorance.

  11. 14.17

    From sattva comes knowledge, and from rajas greed; from tamas come carelessness and delusion, and ignorance as well.

  12. 14.18

    Those established in sattva rise upward; the rajasic stay in the middle; the tamasic, sunk in the lowest of the gunas, go downward.

  13. 17.1

    Krishna, what about those who set aside the rules of scripture yet worship with faith? What is their standing: sattva, rajas or tamas?

  14. 17.7

    Even the food each person likes is of three kinds, and so are sacrifice, austerity and charity. Hear how they differ.

  15. 17.9

    Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, overly hot, pungent, dry and burning are favoured by rajasic people; they bring pain, grief and illness.

  16. 17.12

    But sacrifice performed with an eye on the reward, or merely for show, know that to be rajasic, best of the Bharatas.

  17. 17.18

    Austerity performed to gain respect, honour and reverence, and done with hypocrisy, is called rajasic here; it is unsteady and does not last.

  18. 17.21

    But a gift given to get something back, or with a reward in view, or given grudgingly, is held to be rajasic.

  19. 18.8

    Whoever gives up an action because it is painful, or out of fear of bodily hardship, makes a rajasic relinquishment and does not gain the reward of relinquishment.

  20. 18.19

    Knowledge, action and the doer are each said to be of three kinds, differing according to the gunas, in the analysis of the gunas. Hear about them as they are.

  21. 18.21

    But the knowledge that sees in all beings separate entities of various kinds, each distinct from the others, know that knowledge to be rajasic.

  22. 18.24

    But an action done by one craving desires, or with ego, involving a great deal of strain, is called rajasic.

  23. 18.27

    A doer who is driven by passion, eager for the fruits of action, greedy, harmful by nature, impure, and swept by joy and grief, is called rajasic.

  24. 18.29

    Now hear, Dhananjaya, the threefold division of understanding and of resolve according to the gunas, which I will explain fully and one by one.

  25. 18.31

    The understanding by which one judges dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done, in a distorted way, that understanding, Partha, is rajasic.

  26. 18.34

    But the resolve by which one holds on to dharma, pleasure and wealth, Arjuna, craving their fruits out of attachment, that resolve, Partha, is rajasic.

  27. 18.38

    The happiness that comes from the contact of the senses with their objects, nectar-like at first but like poison in the end, is remembered as rajasic.

  28. 18.40

    There is no being on earth, nor among the gods in heaven, that is free of these three gunas born of Prakriti.
