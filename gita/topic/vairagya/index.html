वैराग्य

Detachment

Dispassion toward sense objects; with practice, the way to hold the mind (6.35).

इंद्रिय-विषयों के प्रति विरक्ति; अभ्यास के साथ मन को वश में करने का मार्ग (6.35)।

10 verses

  1. 2.52

    When your understanding crosses beyond the thicket of delusion, you will become indifferent to what you have heard and what is yet to be heard.

  2. 2.59

    Sense objects fall away from one who abstains from them, but the taste for them remains; even that taste fades once the Supreme is seen.

  3. 5.22

    The pleasures born of contact with objects are only sources of sorrow; they have a beginning and an end, son of Kunti, and the wise take no delight in them.

  4. 6.10

    A yogi should continually bring his mind to stillness, staying in a secluded place, alone, with mind and body restrained, free of cravings and without possessions.

  5. 6.35

    There is no doubt, mighty-armed one, that the mind is unsteady and hard to rein in. Yet, son of Kunti, it can be held firm by persistent practice and by dispassion.

  6. 12.16

    One who expects nothing, is pure, capable, unconcerned, free from anxiety, and has given up every self-driven undertaking — that devotee of mine is dear to me.

  7. 13.8

    …dispassion toward the objects of the senses, absence of ego, and seeing clearly the suffering and flaw in birth, death, old age and disease…

  8. 15.3

    Its true form cannot be grasped here, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor what it rests on. Cutting down this firmly rooted ashvattha with the strong axe of non-attachment…

  9. 18.51

    Endowed with a purified understanding, firmly restraining himself with resolve, setting aside sound and the other sense objects, and casting off attraction and aversion…

  10. 18.52

    …living in solitude, eating lightly, with speech, body and mind controlled, always devoted to the yoga of meditation, and taking refuge in dispassion…
