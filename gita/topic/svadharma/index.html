स्वधर्म

One's own duty

The duty that belongs to one's own nature and station. Better done imperfectly than another's done well (3.35, 18.47).

अपनी प्रकृति और स्थिति के अनुरूप कर्तव्य। दूसरे का धर्म भली-भाँति करने से इसे अपूर्ण रूप से करना श्रेष्ठ है (3.35, 18.47)।

20 verses

  1. 2.31

    Considering your own duty, too, you should not waver. For a kshatriya there is nothing better than a righteous war.

  2. 2.32

    Fortunate are the kshatriyas, Partha, who meet such a war, come of its own accord — an open door to heaven.

  3. 2.33

    But if you will not fight this righteous battle, then, having abandoned your duty and your honour, you will incur sin.

  4. 2.37

    Either you are killed and attain heaven, or you win and enjoy the earth. So rise, son of Kunti, resolved to fight.

  5. 3.8

    Do the work that is yours to do, for action is better than inaction; without acting you could not even keep your body going.

  6. 3.35

    Better one's own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Better to die in one's own dharma; another's dharma brings danger.

  7. 8.7

    So at all times remember me, and fight. With mind and intellect offered to me, you will surely come to me.

  8. 11.33

    So rise up and win glory. Defeat your enemies and enjoy a flourishing kingdom. They have already been slain by me; be merely the instrument, Savyasachin.

  9. 11.34

    Drona and Bhishma, Jayadratha and Karna, and the other heroic warriors too, already slain by me, you must kill. Do not be troubled. Fight: you will conquer your rivals in battle.

  10. 18.7

    It is not right to renounce an action that is one's duty. Giving it up out of delusion is called tamasic relinquishment.

  11. 18.41

    The duties of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, scorcher of foes, are divided according to the gunas that arise from their own nature.

  12. 18.42

    Calm, self-restraint, austerity, purity, forbearance, straightforwardness, knowledge, realised wisdom and faith in the sacred are the natural work of a brahmin.

  13. 18.43

    Valour, vigour, steadiness, skill, not fleeing in battle, generosity and the bearing of a leader are the natural work of a kshatriya.

  14. 18.44

    Farming, tending cattle and trade are the natural work of a vaishya; work that consists of service is the natural work of a shudra.

  15. 18.45

    A person devoted to their own work attains perfection. Hear how one who is intent on their own work reaches that perfection.

  16. 18.46

    By worshipping, through his own work, the one from whom all beings come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a person attains perfection.

  17. 18.47

    Better one's own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Doing the work set by one's own nature, one incurs no sin.

  18. 18.48

    Kaunteya, one should not abandon the work that comes naturally to one, even if it has flaws; for every undertaking is wrapped in flaws, as fire is wrapped in smoke.

  19. 18.59

    If, relying on ego, you think 'I will not fight', that resolve of yours is futile; your own nature will compel you.

  20. 18.60

    Kaunteya, bound by your own work born of your nature, you will do, even against your will, what you now refuse to do out of delusion.
