अभ्यास

Practice

Repeated effort to bring the wandering mind back (6.26, 6.35).

भटकते मन को बार-बार लौटा लाने का प्रयत्न (6.26, 6.35)।

11 verses

  1. 6.23

    ...know that this is what is called yoga: the unlinking from all contact with sorrow. It should be practised with resolve and an undiscouraged heart.

  2. 6.26

    Whenever the restless, unsteady mind wanders off, from there one should draw it back and bring it under control in the Self alone.

  3. 6.35

    There is no doubt, mighty-armed one, that the mind is unsteady and hard to rein in. Yet, son of Kunti, it can be held firm by persistent practice and by dispassion.

  4. 6.36

    My view is that yoga is hard to attain for one who has not mastered himself; but one who has mastered himself and keeps striving can reach it by the right means.

  5. 6.43

    There he regains the understanding he had developed in his former body, and from that point, joy of the Kurus, he strives again, even harder, for perfection.

  6. 6.44

    By that earlier practice he is carried forward even without willing it; even one who merely seeks to know about yoga goes beyond the ritual word of the Vedas.

  7. 6.45

    But the yogi who strives with diligence, cleansed of faults and brought to perfection over many births, then reaches the highest goal.

  8. 8.8

    With a mind trained by the yoga of practice and straying nowhere else, contemplating constantly, Partha, one reaches the supreme divine Person.

  9. 12.9

    If you cannot hold your mind steadily on me, then seek to reach me through the yoga of repeated practice, Dhananjaya.

  10. 15.11

    Yogis who strive see it dwelling within themselves. But those whose minds are unrefined and lack understanding do not see it, even when they make the effort.

  11. 18.36

    Now hear from me, best of the Bharatas, about the three kinds of happiness: the happiness in which one finds delight through practice and arrives at the end of sorrow…
