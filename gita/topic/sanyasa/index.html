Sannyasa

संन्यास

Renunciation

Giving up actions driven by desire (18.2). Its inner form, not its outer show, is what counts.

कामना से प्रेरित कर्मों का त्याग (18.2)। इसका भीतरी रूप मायने रखता है, बाहरी दिखावा नहीं।

14 verses

  1. 3.4

    A person does not reach freedom from action merely by not starting any work, nor does one attain perfection by renunciation alone.

  2. 5.1

    Krishna, you praise the renunciation of action, and then again the yoga of action. Tell me with certainty which one of the two is better.

  3. 5.2

    Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good, but of the two, the yoga of action is better than renouncing action.

  4. 5.3

    Know that person as a true renunciant at all times who neither hates nor craves; free of the pairs of opposites, mighty-armed one, they are easily released from bondage.

  5. 5.6

    But renunciation, mighty-armed one, is hard to attain without yoga; a sage who is steady in yoga reaches Brahman before long.

  6. 5.13

    Having given up all actions with the mind, the self-controlled embodied one dwells at ease in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor causing anything to be done.

  7. 6.1

    One who does the work that ought to be done without leaning on its fruits is both a renunciant and a yogi; not the one who has merely put out the sacred fire, nor the one who simply stops acting.

  8. 6.2

    Know, Pandava, that what people call renunciation is in fact yoga, for no one becomes a yogi without first giving up self-willed intentions.

  9. 6.4

    When a person no longer clings to the objects of the senses or to actions, and has let go of every self-willed intention, then he is called one who has mounted to yoga.

  10. 9.28

    In this way you will be freed from the bonds of action and their good and bad results. With your mind set firm in the yoga of renunciation, you will be liberated and come to me.

  11. 18.1

    Mighty-armed one, Hrishikesha, slayer of the demon Keshi, I want to understand the true nature of renunciation (sannyasa) and of relinquishment (tyaga), each one separately.

  12. 18.2

    The wise understand sannyasa as setting aside actions driven by desire, while the discerning describe tyaga as giving up the fruits of every action.

  13. 18.12

    The fruit of action is threefold after death for those who have not relinquished: unwanted, wanted and mixed. For those who have renounced, there is none at all.

  14. 18.49

    One whose understanding is unattached everywhere, who has mastered himself and is free of craving, attains through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action.
