Life questions

When you're facing one of these, start with what the Gita says about it.

  • Anxious or worriedThe Gita treats worry as the mind being thrown about by things that come and go. It asks you to bear them steadily and not let them decide your peace.
  • Unsure of my duty or what to doThe whole Gita begins here: Arjuna does not know what he should do. Krishna's answer is to do the work that is truly yours, even imperfectly, and to think it through before you act.
  • Grieving a lossKrishna's first teaching is spoken to someone overwhelmed by grief. He speaks of a self that is not born and does not die, and of death as part of every life.
  • Angry or resentfulThe Gita traces anger back to wanting: brooding on something breeds attachment, attachment breeds desire, and blocked desire turns into anger. It asks you to notice that chain early.
  • Afraid of failure, attached to resultsYour part is the work, not the outcome. The Gita asks you to act fully, stay even in success and failure, and let go of the result.
  • Restless mind, can't focusArjuna himself says the mind is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna agrees, and says it is steadied by practice and letting go, bringing it back each time it wanders.
  • Feeling low or without purposeThe Gita says you can lift yourself, that no sincere effort is wasted, and that no one who does good comes to a bad end.
  • Who am I?The Gita separates the body, which it calls the field, from the one who knows it. That knower is described as unborn, beyond harm, and part of something larger.
  • Afraid of deathThe Upanishads put this fear in the mouth of a boy who walks into Death's house and asks. Their answer is that what you most deeply are was never born and does not die.
  • How should I live?Speak the truth, give, hold yourself back, be kind, enjoy without grabbing: the Upanishads give short, practical answers alongside the deep ones.
