The deluded one

The deluded one

मूढः

mūḍha, ajña

Contrasted again and again with the wise one, especially in chapter 18: the fool tries hard, grasps at stillness and calls it freedom.

बार-बार ज्ञानी से तुलना, विशेषकर अठारहवें अध्याय में: मूढ़ बहुत प्रयास करता है, शांति को पकड़ता है और उसे मुक्ति कहता है।

19 verses

  1. 4.1

    Ah, the wise one who knows the Self plays the game of life's enjoyments, and bears no likeness at all to the deluded, who are hauled along like beasts of burden by the world.

  2. 18.18

    The wise one, the reverse of ordinary people, though he lives just as they do, sees in himself neither samadhi nor distraction nor any taint.

  3. 18.32

    Hearing the truth as it really is, a dull person becomes bewildered; while some who are not deluded draw inward and seem like fools.

  4. 18.33

    The deluded labour hard at concentration and at stilling the mind. The wise, resting in their own state as if asleep, see nothing that needs to be done.

  5. 18.34

    Neither by effort nor by giving up effort does the deluded one find peace. The wise one is at peace simply by being certain of the truth.

  6. 18.36

    The deluded one does not reach liberation through the action of repeated practice. The blessed one, through understanding alone, is free and remains unchanged.

  7. 18.37

    The deluded one does not reach Brahman, because he wants to become it. The wise one, without even wanting it, already shares the nature of the supreme Brahman.

  8. 18.38

    Without real ground and restless to grasp, the deluded keep feeding samsara. The wise have cut this root of all misery at its root.

  9. 18.39

    The deluded one finds no peace, because he tries to make himself peaceful. The wise one, having settled the truth, is at peace in mind always.

  10. 18.40

    How can one who leans on what is seen ever see the Self? The wise do not see this or that; they see the imperishable Self.

  11. 18.41

    Where is stillness for the deluded one, who forces his mind? For the wise one who delights in himself, it is always natural.

  12. 18.45

    Seeing the tigers of sense-objects, the frightened run for refuge and quickly crawl into a cave, hoping to achieve control and concentration of mind.

  13. 18.52

    The wise one's way of being shines, unrestrained and unplanned though it is; not so the forced calm of the deluded one whose mind is full of craving.

  14. 18.58

    The deluded one is anxious and restless everywhere, even when doing nothing. The capable one stays untroubled, even while doing what has to be done.

  15. 18.61

    For the deluded one, even withdrawing from action turns into activity. For the wise one, even activity bears the fruit of withdrawal.

  16. 18.62

    The deluded one often shows distaste for possessions. But for one whose attachment even to the body has fallen away, where is liking and where is distaste?

  17. 18.63

    The deluded one's outlook always clings to contemplating or not contemplating. The settled one, though he contemplates what comes, has an outlook that is no outlook at all.

  18. 18.75

    If a dull-minded person gives up practices such as stilling the mind, he falls at once into daydreams and idle chatter.

  19. 18.76

    The dull one, even after hearing that truth, does not give up his delusion. Outwardly he strains to be free of thought, but inwardly he hungers for objects.
