Ego, I and mine

Ego, I and mine

अहंकारः

ahaṃkāra, mamatā

The sense of a separate 'I' with things that are 'mine'. 'Where there is I, there is bondage.'

अलग 'मैं' और 'मेरा' होने का भाव। 'जहाँ मैं है, वहाँ बंधन है।'

16 verses

  1. 1.8

    You have been bitten by the great black snake of the ego that says, 'I am the doer.' Drink the nectar of the conviction 'I am not the doer,' and be happy.

  2. 1.14

    For a long time, my child, you have been bound by the noose of identifying with the body. Cut it with the sword of the knowledge 'I am awareness,' and be happy.

  3. 3.5

    How strange that the sense of 'mine' still lingers in a sage who knows the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self.

  4. 8.4

    When there is no 'I', there is liberation; when there is 'I', there is bondage. Seeing this, lightly and without effort, neither take hold of anything nor push anything away.

  5. 15.6

    Knowing the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self, be free of 'I' and 'mine', and be happy.

  6. 15.13

    In you, the one, the imperishable, the peaceful, the stainless sky of consciousness, where is birth, where is action, where is even the ego?

  7. 15.15

    Give up dividing things into 'this I am' and 'this I am not'. Knowing for certain that all is the Self, let go of intentions and be happy.

  8. 16.10

    One who takes pride even in liberation and still claims the body as his own is neither a knower nor a yogi; he simply has his share of suffering.

  9. 17.19

    Without 'mine', without 'I', certain that nothing is, with every hope melted away within, he does nothing even while acting.

  10. 18.9

    Notions like 'this I am' and 'this I am not' fade away for the yogi who, certain that all is the Self, has fallen silent.

  11. 18.29

    One who carries ego within is acting even when he does nothing. For the wise one free of ego, nothing is done and nothing is left undone.

  12. 18.73

    In a samsara that reaches only as far as the intellect, mere illusion plays itself out. The wise one, free of 'mine', free of ego and free of desire, shines.

  13. 18.74

    For the sage who sees the Self as imperishable and free of sorrow, where is knowledge, where is the world, where the thought 'I am the body' or 'this is mine'?

  14. 18.88

    The wise one, free of 'mine', shines; to him a clod of earth, a stone and gold are all the same. The knot of his heart is cut through, and restlessness and dullness are shaken off.

  15. 18.95

    The knower, though he has thoughts, is free of thought; though he has senses, is beyond the senses; though he has intellect, is beyond intellect; though he has an ego, is without ego.

  16. 20.3

    Where is knowledge, where is ignorance, where is 'I', where is 'this', where is 'mine'? Where is bondage, where is liberation? How can my own nature have any form?
