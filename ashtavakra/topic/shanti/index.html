Stillness and rest

Stillness and rest

शान्तिः

śānti, viśrānti

The calm that is there when nothing is pursued. Chapter 18 is named for it.

वह शांति जो तब होती है जब किसी चीज़ का पीछा नहीं होता। अठारहवाँ अध्याय इसी के नाम पर है।

    If you set the body apart and rest in consciousness, you will this very moment be happy, at peace, and free from bondage.

  2. 1.17

    Be free of expectation, unchanging, full, cool at heart, of unfathomable understanding and undisturbed, with your only inclination toward pure consciousness.

  3. 2.1

    Oh! I am stainless, at peace, pure awareness, beyond nature. All this time I have simply been fooled by delusion.

  4. 2.24

    In me, the boundless ocean, when the wind of the mind dies down, the ship of the world is wrecked, to the misfortune of its merchant, the individual soul.

  6. 7.3

  7. 7.4

  8. 9.3

    Having seen for certain that all this is fleeting, spoiled by the three kinds of suffering, hollow, contemptible and fit to be left, one becomes calm.

  9. 9.5

  10. 10.7

    Enough of wealth, of pleasure, and even of good deeds. In the wilderness of the world, the mind never found rest through any of them.

  11. 10.8

    For how many lives have you not toiled with body, mind and speech at painful, exhausting work? At least now, let it come to rest.

  12. 11.1

  13. 11.2

  14. 11.5

    One who knows for certain that suffering in this world comes from anxious thinking and nothing else is freed of it — happy, at peace, his wants fallen away everywhere.

  15. 11.7

  16. 11.8

    One who knows for certain that this universe, with all its many wonders, is nothing at all becomes free of latent desires; pure shining awareness, he rests in peace as if nothing were there.

  17. 12.1

    First I lost patience with the work of the body, then with long talk, and then with thinking itself; and so I remain just as I am.

  18. 12.2

    Since I take no delight in sound and the other objects, and since the Self is never something seen, my heart is free of scattering and rests one-pointed; and so I remain just as I am.

  19. 12.4

    Having nothing to reject or accept, and so no joy or sorrow, today, O brahmin, I remain just as I am.

  20. 13.1

    The ease that comes from owning nothing is hard to find even for one who wears only a loincloth. So, letting go of both giving up and taking, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  21. 14.1

  22. 15.13

  23. 15.17

    One who knows for certain that this world is mere illusion and nothing at all is freed of latent desires; he is sheer radiance and comes to rest as though he were nothing.

  24. 15.19

    You who are made of consciousness, do not stir up your mind with wanting and doubting. Grow calm and rest happily in your own Self, which is bliss itself.

  25. 16.1

  26. 16.3

    Everyone is miserable because of effort, yet no one understands this. Through this one teaching alone, the fortunate one reaches release.

  27. 16.11

    Even if Shiva, Vishnu or lotus-born Brahma were your teacher, you would not rest in yourself until you forget everything.

  28. 17.11

    The free one is seen everywhere at ease, everywhere pure in heart; released from all latent desires, he shines wherever he is.

    Salutations to that peaceful radiance, whose nature is bliss alone, with the dawning of whose knowledge delusion at once becomes like a dream.

  30. 18.3

  31. 18.10

  32. 18.14

    Where is delusion, where is the world, where is meditation on it, where is liberation, for the great soul who rests at the far edge of all intentions?

  33. 18.22

    For one who has gone beyond samsara there is no elation and no dejection anywhere. His mind always cool, he shines as though he had no body.

  34. 18.27

    Worn out by endless reasoning, the wise one has come to rest. He no longer imagines, knows, hears or sees.

  35. 18.30

    The mind of the free one shines: not agitated and not gratified, not acting, without stirring, without hopes, its doubts gone.

  36. 18.34

  37. 18.39

    The deluded one finds no peace, because he tries to make himself peaceful. The wise one, having settled the truth, is at peace in mind always.

  38. 18.42

    One person holds that things exist, another holds that nothing exists. Rare is the one who holds to neither; he alone is at ease.

  39. 18.50

  40. 18.58

  41. 18.59

  42. 18.60

    By his very nature he feels no distress, though he deals with the world like anyone else. Calm as a vast lake, his troubles gone, he shines.

  43. 18.70

    Certain that all this is a product of illusion and that nothing truly is, the pure one, whose light cannot be made an object, comes to rest by his very nature.

  44. 18.81

    He neither prays for gain nor grieves over loss. The cool mind of the wise one is filled as if with nectar.

  45. 18.86

    Let the body fall or rise; the great soul does not care, for resting on the ground of his own nature he has forgotten all of samsara.

  46. 18.93

    What is felt within by one content in resting in the Self, free of hope and free of pain — how could it be told, and to whom?

  47. 18.100

    The one whose mind is at peace runs neither toward the crowd nor toward the forest. However things are, wherever he is, he remains the same.

  48. 19.3

    Where is the past, where is the future, where even the present? Where is space, and where is eternity, for me who abides in my own glory?

  49. 19.8

    Enough of talk about the three aims of life, enough of talk about yoga, enough even of talk about knowledge, for me who rests in the Self.

  50. 20.12

    Where is activity, where is withdrawal, where is liberation, where is bondage, for me who am always unchanging, undivided and at rest in myself?

  51. 20.14

    Where is existence, where is non-existence, where is oneness, where is twoness? What more is there to say? Nothing at all arises for me.
