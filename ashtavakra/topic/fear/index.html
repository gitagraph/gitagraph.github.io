Fear and sorrow

Fear and sorrow

भयशोकौ

bhaya, śoka, duḥkha

Fear, grief and suffering, which belong to the one who thinks they are separate.

भय, शोक और दुख, जो उसके हैं जो अपने को अलग मानता है।

26 verses

  1. 1.9

    With the fire of the certainty 'I am the one, pure awareness,' burn down the dense forest of ignorance. Free from sorrow, be happy.

  2. 2.16

    Oh! Duality is the root of suffering, and there is no other cure for it than this: all that is seen is false, and I am one, pure, the very essence of consciousness.

  3. 2.20

    The body, heaven and hell, bondage and liberation, and fear too: all this is mere imagining. What have I, who am consciousness, to do with any of it?

  4. 3.8

    How strange that one who is detached from this world and the next, who can tell the eternal from the passing, and who longs for liberation, should be afraid of liberation itself.

  5. 3.11

    Seeing this world as mere illusion, with all curiosity gone, how could one of steady mind fear even death when it is near?

  6. 4.6

    Rare is the one who knows the Self as one without a second, lord of the world. He does whatever he knows to do, and has no fear from anywhere.

  7. 9.3

    Having seen for certain that all this is fleeting, spoiled by the three kinds of suffering, hollow, contemptible and fit to be left, one becomes calm.

  8. 10.8

    For how many lives have you not toiled with body, mind and speech at painful, exhausting work? At least now, let it come to rest.

  9. 11.3

    One who knows for certain that misfortune and good fortune come in their time by destiny alone stays content, his senses at rest; he neither longs nor grieves.

  10. 11.5

    One who knows for certain that suffering in this world comes from anxious thinking and nothing else is freed of it — happy, at peace, his wants fallen away everywhere.

  11. 15.9

    The body, wrapped in the qualities (gunas), stays, comes and goes. The Self neither goes nor comes. Why grieve over it?

  12. 16.3

    Everyone is miserable because of effort, yet no one understands this. Through this one teaching alone, the fortunate one reaches release.

  13. 16.9

    The passionate person wants to leave worldly life to escape sorrow. But one free of passion is free of sorrow and is not troubled even while in the world.

  14. 16.10

    One who takes pride even in liberation and still claims the body as his own is neither a knower nor a yogi; he simply has his share of suffering.

  15. 17.14

    Whether he sees a woman full of love or death drawing near, the great-souled one stays steady in mind and at ease. He is truly free.

  16. 18.3

    When one's inner being is scorched by the blazing sun of the pain of duties, where is happiness without a downpour of the nectar of calm?

  17. 18.6

    Simply by ending delusion and simply by grasping their own nature, those whose sight is unveiled live radiant and free of sorrow.

  18. 18.38

    Without real ground and restless to grasp, the deluded keep feeding samsara. The wise have cut this root of all misery at its root.

  19. 18.45

    Seeing the tigers of sense-objects, the frightened run for refuge and quickly crawl into a cave, hoping to achieve control and concentration of mind.

  20. 18.78

    For the wise one who is always unchanging and without fear, where is darkness, where light, where renunciation? There is nothing at all.

  21. 18.79

    For the yogi whose nature cannot be put into words, who has no fixed nature at all, where is steadiness, where discernment, where even fearlessness?

  22. 18.99

    He is not pleased when praised, nor angered when blamed. He neither dreads death nor rejoices in life.

  23. 19.5

    Where is dreaming, where is deep sleep, where is waking? Where is the fourth state, or even fear, for me who abides in my own glory?

  24. 19.7

    Where is death, where is life, where are the worlds, and where are worldly affairs? Where is dissolution, and where is samadhi, for me who abides in my own glory?

  25. 20.9

    Where is distraction, where is one-pointedness, where is dullness, where is delusion? Where is joy, and where is sorrow, for me who am always actionless?

  26. 20.10

    Where is everyday life, and where is ultimate truth? Where is happiness, and where is sorrow, for me who am always beyond thought?
