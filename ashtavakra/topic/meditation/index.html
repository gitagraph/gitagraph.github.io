Meditation and samadhi

समाधिः

dhyāna, samādhi

Unusually, the text treats the effort to meditate as a subtle bondage: 'your bondage is that you practise samadhi.'

असामान्य रूप से, ग्रंथ ध्यान के प्रयास को सूक्ष्म बंधन मानता है: 'तुम्हारा बंधन यही है कि तुम समाधि का अभ्यास करते हो।'

31 verses

  1. 1.15

    You are unattached, actionless, self-luminous and without stain. Your bondage is just this: that you keep practising samadhi.

  2. 12.3

    Scattering comes from false superimposition and the like, and so one takes up practice for samadhi. Seeing this pattern, I remain just as I am.

  3. 12.5

    Stages of life or none at all, meditation, renouncing what the mind clings to — seeing that through these only my own mental fancies arise, I remain just as I am.

  4. 12.7

    Even when thinking of the unthinkable, one still takes on the form of thought. So, giving up even that contemplation, I remain just as I am.

  5. 15.20

    Give up meditation altogether; hold nothing in your heart. You are the Self, already free. What will you achieve by pondering?

  6. 16.2

    Enjoy pleasures, do your work, or practise samadhi, knowing one; still, your mind will long above all for the state where every hope has fallen away.

  7. 17.1

    One who is content, with senses clear, and always delights in being alone, has won the fruit of knowledge and the fruit of yoga practice as well.

  8. 17.18

    With an empty mind, as if established in complete aloneness (kaivalya), he does not even know the notions of focused and unfocused, good and bad.

  9. 18.7

    Knowing that everything is mere imagination and that the Self is free and eternal, why would the wise one go on practising like a child?

  10. 18.10

    For the yogi who has become wholly calm there is no distraction and no focus, no heightened knowing and no dullness, no pleasure and no pain.

  11. 18.14

    Where is delusion, where is the world, where is meditation on it, where is liberation, for the great soul who rests at the far edge of all intentions?

  12. 18.16

    One who has seen the supreme Brahman may meditate, 'I am Brahman.' But what is there to think about for one beyond thought, who sees no second?

  13. 18.17

    One who has noticed his own distraction tries to restrain it. But the noble one is not distracted; with nothing to achieve, what should he do?

  14. 18.18

    The wise one, the reverse of ordinary people, though he lives just as they do, sees in himself neither samadhi nor distraction nor any taint.

  15. 18.28

    Since he is neither in samadhi nor distracted, he is neither a seeker of liberation nor its opposite. Seeing with certainty that all this is imagined, the great-souled one simply remains as Brahman.

  16. 18.31

    The free one's mind does not set out to meditate or to act; yet, without any reason, it meditates and it acts.

  17. 18.33

    The deluded labour hard at concentration and at stilling the mind. The wise, resting in their own state as if asleep, see nothing that needs to be done.

  18. 18.35

    The Self is pure, awake, beloved, complete, beyond the world's spread and free of all ill. People absorbed in practice do not know it.

  19. 18.36

    The deluded one does not reach liberation through the action of repeated practice. The blessed one, through understanding alone, is free and remains unchanged.

  20. 18.41

    Where is stillness for the deluded one, who forces his mind? For the wise one who delights in himself, it is always natural.

  21. 18.43

    People of poor understanding meditate on the Self as pure and non-dual, but in their confusion they never actually know it, and remain unfulfilled all their lives.

  22. 18.45

    Seeing the tigers of sense-objects, the frightened run for refuge and quickly crawl into a cave, hoping to achieve control and concentration of mind.

  23. 18.47

    Free of doubt and with a settled mind, he takes up no methods for liberation. Seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, he lives at ease.

  24. 18.63

    The deluded one's outlook always clings to contemplating or not contemplating. The settled one, though he contemplates what comes, has an outlook that is no outlook at all.

  25. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  26. 18.75

    If a dull-minded person gives up practices such as stilling the mind, he falls at once into daydreams and idle chatter.

  27. 18.97

    The blessed one is not distracted in distraction, not absorbed in samadhi, not dull in dullness, and not learned in learning.

  28. 19.7

    Where is death, where is life, where are the worlds, and where are worldly affairs? Where is dissolution, and where is samadhi, for me who abides in my own glory?

  29. 19.8

    Enough of talk about the three aims of life, enough of talk about yoga, enough even of talk about knowledge, for me who rests in the Self.

  30. 20.7

    Where is creation, where is destruction, where is the goal, where is the means? Where is the seeker, and where is attainment, for me in my own non-dual nature?

  31. 20.9

    Where is distraction, where is one-pointedness, where is dullness, where is delusion? Where is joy, and where is sorrow, for me who am always actionless?
