Dispassion

Dispassion

वैराग्यम्

vairāgya, tyāga, nirveda

Losing the taste for the world's objects. Janaka asks about it in the first verse; later the text drops even giving up as a task.

संसार की वस्तुओं में रस का छूट जाना। जनक पहले श्लोक में इसके बारे में पूछते हैं; बाद में ग्रंथ त्याग को भी कोई काम नहीं मानता।

36 verses

  1. 1.1

    How does one gain knowledge? How will liberation come? And how is dispassion reached? Tell me this, master.

  2. 1.2

    If you want liberation, child, drop the objects of the senses as you would poison. Take up patience, honesty, kindness, contentment and truth as you would nectar.

  3. 2.3

    Oh! Now that I have let go of the world along with the body, by some fine skill the supreme Self is seen.

  4. 2.21

    Oh! Even in a crowd of people I see no duality. It has become like a wilderness to me, so what could I take delight in?

  5. 3.8

    How strange that one who is detached from this world and the next, who can tell the eternal from the passing, and who longs for liberation, should be afraid of liberation itself.

  6. 5.1

    You are tied to nothing at all. Being pure, what would you renounce? Letting this bundle of body and mind fall apart, just so, let yourself dissolve.

  7. 7.4

    The Self is not in things, nor are things in that boundless, spotless one. So, unattached, wanting nothing and at peace, in this alone I abide.

  8. 9.1

    Things done and not done, and the pairs of opposites — when have they ever ended, and for whom? Knowing this, be dispassionate here, given to letting go and bound by no vows.

  9. 9.2

    Child, only some rare blessed one, by watching how people strive, has lost the wish to live, the hunger to enjoy, and the urge to know.

  10. 9.3

    Having seen for certain that all this is fleeting, spoiled by the three kinds of suffering, hollow, contemptible and fit to be left, one becomes calm.

  11. 9.5

    Seeing how the great sages, the saints and the yogis all hold different views, what person would not grow weary of it all and become calm?

  12. 9.6

    Is he not a true guru who has fully known the nature of consciousness, and who, through dispassion, evenness and right reasoning, carries one across the round of birth and death?

  13. 9.8

    Latent desires are themselves the world of becoming, so let go of them all. Dropping them is dropping the world; then remain, from now on, however you happen to be.

  14. 10.1

    Put away desire, which is your enemy, and wealth, which is full of trouble, and even duty, which is the root of both. Be indifferent to all of them.

  15. 10.2

    See friends, lands, money, houses, wives, inheritances and all such riches as a dream or a magic show, lasting three days or five.

  16. 10.3

    Wherever there is craving, know that the world of becoming is right there. Taking firm hold of mature dispassion, be free of craving and be happy.

  17. 10.6

    Kingdoms, sons, wives, bodies and pleasures have been lost to you birth after birth, even though you clung to them.

  18. 11.2

    One who knows for certain that God is the maker of everything and there is no other here has all his hopes melted away within; at peace, he clings to nothing anywhere.

  19. 12.5

    Stages of life or none at all, meditation, renouncing what the mind clings to — seeing that through these only my own mental fancies arise, I remain just as I am.

  20. 13.1

    The ease that comes from owning nothing is hard to find even for one who wears only a loincloth. So, letting go of both giving up and taking, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  21. 13.7

    Having seen again and again how pleasure and the like come and go without any rule among things, I let go of good and bad, and live at ease, as it suits me.

  22. 15.2

    Liberation is losing the taste for sense objects; bondage is relishing them. That is all there is to know. Now do as you wish.

  23. 16.6

    The renunciant hates sense objects; the passionate person is greedy for them. But one who neither grasps nor lets go is neither renunciant nor passionate.

  24. 16.9

    The passionate person wants to leave worldly life to escape sorrow. But one free of passion is free of sorrow and is not troubled even while in the world.

  25. 17.4

    One who does not cling to pleasures already enjoyed and does not crave those not yet enjoyed is rare in this world.

  26. 17.9

    For one in whom the ocean of worldly life has dried up, the gaze is empty, actions have no purpose, the senses are idle; there is neither craving nor dispassion.

  27. 17.13

    He neither blames nor praises, is neither elated nor angry, neither gives nor takes. The free one has no taste for anything anywhere.

  28. 18.2

    A person may gain every kind of wealth and win abundant pleasures, yet no one can be happy without giving up everything.

  29. 18.23

    The wise one who delights in the Self, whose inner being is cool and clear, has no wish to give anything up and no sense of loss, anywhere.

  30. 18.46

    Seeing the lion who is free of craving, the elephants of sense-objects quietly flee; those that cannot, stay and serve him like flatterers.

  31. 18.62

    The deluded one often shows distaste for possessions. But for one whose attachment even to the body has fallen away, where is liking and where is distaste?

  32. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  33. 18.71

    For one whose nature is pure radiance and who sees no world of objects, where is rule, where dispassion, where renunciation, where even calm?

  34. 18.84

    Without attachment to son, wife and the rest, without desire for sense-objects, unconcerned even about his own body, free of hopes, the wise one shines.

  35. 18.87

    Owning nothing, moving as he pleases, beyond the pairs of opposites, his doubts cut away, unattached to all things, the wise one delights, alone.

  36. 20.11

    Where is illusion, where is worldly life, where is attachment, where is detachment? Where is the individual soul, and where is that Brahman, for me who am always pure?
