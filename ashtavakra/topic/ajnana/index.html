Ignorance

Ignorance

अज्ञानम्

ajñāna, moha, bhrānti

Not knowing what you are. It alone makes the world seem real and you seem bound.

यह न जानना कि तुम क्या हो। यही संसार को सच और तुम्हें बँधा हुआ दिखाता है।

24 verses

  1. 1.9

    With the fire of the certainty 'I am the one, pure awareness,' burn down the dense forest of ignorance. Free from sorrow, be happy.

  2. 1.12

    The Self is the witness: all-pervading, complete, one, free, consciousness itself, without action, unattached, without desire, at peace. Only through delusion does it seem caught up in worldly life.

  3. 1.13

    Let go of the illusion that you are a reflected self, and of every sense of outer and inner. Contemplate the Self as unchanging, non-dual awareness.

  4. 2.1

    Oh! I am stainless, at peace, pure awareness, beyond nature. All this time I have simply been fooled by delusion.

  5. 2.7

    The world appears through not knowing the Self and does not appear when the Self is known, just as a snake appears through not knowing the rope and vanishes once the rope is known.

  6. 2.9

    Oh! Through ignorance the imagined world appears in me, like silver in a shell, a snake in a rope, or water in the sun's rays.

  7. 2.15

    Knowledge, the known and the knower: this triad is not real. I am that stainless one in whom it appears through ignorance.

  8. 2.17

    I am pure awareness; through ignorance I imagined limitations on myself. Reflecting on this constantly, I abide beyond all thought.

  9. 3.2

    Ah, it is from not knowing the Self that one delights in the illusory objects of the senses, just as greed arises for the silver mistakenly seen in a shell.

  10. 10.5

    You are the one pure consciousness; the world is inert and unreal. Even ignorance is nothing at all. So what is left for you to want to know?

  11. 12.3

    Scattering comes from false superimposition and the like, and so one takes up practice for samadhi. Seeing this pattern, I remain just as I am.

  12. 12.6

    Doing actions springs from ignorance, and so does ceasing from them. Understanding this truth fully, I remain just as I am.

  13. 15.1

    A person of pure understanding reaches life's goal even from a passing word of instruction, while another may keep inquiring all his life and still stay confused about it.

  14. 15.8

    Have faith, child, have faith; do not be confused about this, my dear. You are the Self, knowledge itself, the Lord, beyond nature (prakriti).

  15. 15.16

    The world exists only through your own ignorance; in truth you alone are. Apart from you there is no one, bound to worldly life or free of it.

  16. 16.7

    As long as craving lives, the soil in which want of discernment grows, so long will the sense of rejecting and accepting remain, the sprout of the tree of worldly life.

  17. 17.20

    One whose mind has melted away reaches a state beyond words, free of the mind's display, delusion, dreaming and dullness.

  18. 18.6

    Simply by ending delusion and simply by grasping their own nature, those whose sight is unveiled live radiant and free of sorrow.

  19. 18.14

    Where is delusion, where is the world, where is meditation on it, where is liberation, for the great soul who rests at the far edge of all intentions?

  20. 18.32

    Hearing the truth as it really is, a dull person becomes bewildered; while some who are not deluded draw inward and seem like fools.

  21. 18.35

    The Self is pure, awake, beloved, complete, beyond the world's spread and free of all ill. People absorbed in practice do not know it.

  22. 18.43

    People of poor understanding meditate on the Self as pure and non-dual, but in their confusion they never actually know it, and remain unfulfilled all their lives.

  23. 18.76

    The dull one, even after hearing that truth, does not give up his delusion. Outwardly he strains to be free of thought, but inwardly he hungers for objects.

  24. 20.3

    Where is knowledge, where is ignorance, where is 'I', where is 'this', where is 'mine'? Where is bondage, where is liberation? How can my own nature have any form?
