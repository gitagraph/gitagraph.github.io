The world as appearance

The world as appearance

जगन्मिथ्यात्वम्

bhrama, kalpita, māyā

The world appears in the Self like a snake in a rope, silver in mother-of-pearl, water in a mirage, a magic show.

संसार आत्मा में वैसे दिखता है जैसे रस्सी में साँप, सीप में चाँदी, मृगतृष्णा में जल, जादू का खेल।

38 verses

  1. 1.10

    That in which this world appears, imagined like a snake on a rope, that awareness, bliss beyond all bliss, is what you are. Live happily.

  2. 1.12

    The Self is the witness: all-pervading, complete, one, free, consciousness itself, without action, unattached, without desire, at peace. Only through delusion does it seem caught up in worldly life.

  3. 1.18

    Know that whatever has form is unreal, and the formless is unmoving. Through this teaching of the truth, you will not be born into the world again.

  4. 2.6

    Just as sugar formed from cane juice is wholly pervaded by that juice, so the world imagined in me is pervaded by me through and through.

  5. 2.7

    The world appears through not knowing the Self and does not appear when the Self is known, just as a snake appears through not knowing the rope and vanishes once the rope is known.

  6. 2.9

    Oh! Through ignorance the imagined world appears in me, like silver in a shell, a snake in a rope, or water in the sun's rays.

  7. 2.15

    Knowledge, the known and the knower: this triad is not real. I am that stainless one in whom it appears through ignorance.

  8. 2.16

    Oh! Duality is the root of suffering, and there is no other cure for it than this: all that is seen is false, and I am one, pure, the very essence of consciousness.

  9. 2.18

    For me there is neither bondage nor liberation. The illusion has lost its ground and come to rest. Oh! The world rests in me, and yet in truth it does not rest in me.

  10. 2.19

    It is now certain that this world, body included, is nothing, and that the Self is pure consciousness alone. So on what could imagination now rest?

  11. 2.20

    The body, heaven and hell, bondage and liberation, and fear too: all this is mere imagining. What have I, who am consciousness, to do with any of it?

  12. 3.2

    Ah, it is from not knowing the Self that one delights in the illusory objects of the senses, just as greed arises for the silver mistakenly seen in a shell.

  13. 3.11

    Seeing this world as mere illusion, with all curiosity gone, how could one of steady mind fear even death when it is near?

  14. 3.13

    Knowing by his very nature that all that is seen is nothing, how could the one of steady mind see this as something to grasp and that as something to reject?

  15. 5.3

    Though it is right before your eyes, the universe has no real substance, and so it does not exist in you, the stainless one, any more than the snake in a rope. Just so, let yourself dissolve.

  16. 6.1

  17. 6.3

    I am like the mother-of-pearl; the imagined universe is like the silver seen in it. This is knowledge, and so there is nothing to give up, nothing to take hold of, nothing to dissolve.

  18. 7.3

    In me, the boundless ocean, the universe is only an imagining. Utterly still and without form, in this alone I abide.

  19. 7.5

    Ah, I am pure consciousness alone, and the world is like a magician's show. So how, and where, could the idea of rejecting or accepting anything arise for me?

  20. 10.2

    See friends, lands, money, houses, wives, inheritances and all such riches as a dream or a magic show, lasting three days or five.

  21. 10.5

    You are the one pure consciousness; the world is inert and unreal. Even ignorance is nothing at all. So what is left for you to want to know?

  22. 11.8

    One who knows for certain that this universe, with all its many wonders, is nothing at all becomes free of latent desires; pure shining awareness, he rests in peace as if nothing were there.

  23. 15.14

    Whatever you see, it is you alone appearing there. Do bracelets, armlets and anklets shine apart from the gold?

  24. 15.16

    The world exists only through your own ignorance; in truth you alone are. Apart from you there is no one, bound to worldly life or free of it.

  25. 15.17

    One who knows for certain that this world is mere illusion and nothing at all is freed of latent desires; he is sheer radiance and comes to rest as though he were nothing.

  26. 18.1

    Salutations to that peaceful radiance, whose nature is bliss alone, with the dawning of whose knowledge delusion at once becomes like a dream.

  27. 18.4

    This world is only an imagining; in truth it is nothing. But for those who see clearly into being and non-being, their own nature never ceases to be.

  28. 18.7

    Knowing that everything is mere imagination and that the Self is free and eternal, why would the wise one go on practising like a child?

  29. 18.8

    Let the one who has seen this world try to deny it. But what is there for one without latent desires to do? Even seeing, he does not see.

  31. 18.28

    Since he is neither in samadhi nor distracted, he is neither a seeker of liberation nor its opposite. Seeing with certainty that all this is imagined, the great-souled one simply remains as Brahman.

  32. 18.66

    For the wise one who is always free of mental constructs, like the sky, where is samsara, where is appearance, where is a goal, where a means?

  33. 18.69

    The dual world, from mahat onward, is just an unfolding of names. Once that is let go, what is left to do for one who is pure awareness?

  34. 18.70

    Certain that all this is a product of illusion and that nothing truly is, the pure one, whose light cannot be made an object, comes to rest by his very nature.

  35. 18.71

  36. 18.73

    In a samsara that reaches only as far as the intellect, mere illusion plays itself out. The wise one, free of 'mine', free of ego and free of desire, shines.

  37. 18.80

  38. 20.11

    Where is illusion, where is worldly life, where is attachment, where is detachment? Where is the individual soul, and where is that Brahman, for me who am always pure?
