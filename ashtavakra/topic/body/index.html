The body

The body

देहः

deha, kāya

Identifying with the body is the first knot. 'Separate yourself from the body and rest in awareness.'

शरीर से अपनी पहचान पहली गाँठ है। 'शरीर से अलग होकर चैतन्य में विश्राम करो।'

25 verses

  1. 1.4

    If you set the body apart and rest in consciousness, you will this very moment be happy, at peace, and free from bondage.

  2. 1.14

    For a long time, my child, you have been bound by the noose of identifying with the body. Cut it with the sword of the knowledge 'I am awareness,' and be happy.

  3. 1.19

    Just as a mirror is both inside and all around the image seen in it, so the Supreme Lord is within this body and all around it.

  4. 2.2

    As I alone light up this body, so I light up the world. So either the whole world is mine, or nothing at all is.

  5. 2.12

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! Though I have a body I am one; I neither go anywhere nor come from anywhere, but abide pervading the whole world.

  6. 2.13

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! No one here is as capable as I, who have held up the world for so long without touching it with the body.

  7. 2.19

    It is now certain that this world, body included, is nothing, and that the Self is pure consciousness alone. So on what could imagination now rest?

  8. 2.22

    I am not the body, nor is the body mine; I am not the individual soul, I am consciousness. My only bondage was this: the craving for life.

  9. 3.10

    The great-hearted one watches his own body act as if it were someone else's. How could praise or blame disturb him?

  10. 5.1

    You are tied to nothing at all. Being pure, what would you renounce? Letting this bundle of body and mind fall apart, just so, let yourself dissolve.

  11. 10.6

    Kingdoms, sons, wives, bodies and pleasures have been lost to you birth after birth, even though you clung to them.

  12. 11.6

    One who knows for certain, 'I am not the body, the body is not mine, I am awareness,' is as if he has reached absolute aloneness; he does not recall what was done or left undone.

  13. 13.2

    Somewhere the body gets weary, somewhere the tongue gets tired, somewhere the mind. Leaving all that behind, I rest happily in life's true aim.

  14. 13.4

    Yogis who identify with the body insist on action or on inaction. Free of both union and separation, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  15. 15.4

    You are not the body, nor is the body yours; you are neither the doer nor the enjoyer. You are consciousness itself, ever the witness, needing nothing. Go about happily.

  16. 15.9

    The body, wrapped in the qualities (gunas), stays, comes and goes. The Self neither goes nor comes. Why grieve over it?

  17. 15.10

    Let the body last until the end of the age, or let it go this very day. What gain or loss is there for you, who are pure consciousness?

  18. 16.10

    One who takes pride even in liberation and still claims the body as his own is neither a knower nor a yogi; he simply has his share of suffering.

  19. 18.22

    For one who has gone beyond samsara there is no elation and no dejection anywhere. His mind always cool, he shines as though he had no body.

  20. 18.25

    "This action was done by the body, not by me, who am pure by nature." One who keeps to this understanding does nothing, even while acting.

  21. 18.62

    The deluded one often shows distaste for possessions. But for one whose attachment even to the body has fallen away, where is liking and where is distaste?

  22. 18.74

    For the sage who sees the Self as imperishable and free of sorrow, where is knowledge, where is the world, where the thought 'I am the body' or 'this is mine'?

  23. 18.84

    Without attachment to son, wife and the rest, without desire for sense-objects, unconcerned even about his own body, free of hopes, the wise one shines.

  24. 18.86

    Let the body fall or rise; the great soul does not care, for resting on the ground of his own nature he has forgotten all of samsara.

  25. 20.1

    Where are the elements, where is the body, where are the senses, where is the mind? Where is emptiness, and where is despair, in my own stainless nature?
