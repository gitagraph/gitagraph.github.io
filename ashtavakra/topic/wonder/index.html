Wonder

आश्चर्यम्

aho, āścarya

Janaka's 'aho!': astonishment at seeing what he is. Ashtavakra also uses it, wondering that anyone could still cling.

जनक का 'अहो!': यह देखकर विस्मय कि वे क्या हैं। अष्टावक्र भी इसका प्रयोग करते हैं, आश्चर्य से कि कोई अब भी कैसे चिपक सकता है।

19 verses

  1. 2.1

    Oh! I am stainless, at peace, pure awareness, beyond nature. All this time I have simply been fooled by delusion.

  2. 2.3

    Oh! Now that I have let go of the world along with the body, by some fine skill the supreme Self is seen.

  3. 2.9

    Oh! Through ignorance the imagined world appears in me, like silver in a shell, a snake in a rope, or water in the sun's rays.

  4. 2.11

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself, who has no destruction and remains even when the whole world, from Brahma down to a blade of grass, is destroyed.

  5. 2.12

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! Though I have a body I am one; I neither go anywhere nor come from anywhere, but abide pervading the whole world.

  6. 2.13

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! No one here is as capable as I, who have held up the world for so long without touching it with the body.

  7. 2.14

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself, who has nothing at all, or else who has everything that speech and mind can reach.

  8. 2.18

    For me there is neither bondage nor liberation. The illusion has lost its ground and come to rest. Oh! The world rests in me, and yet in truth it does not rest in me.

  9. 2.21

    Oh! Even in a crowd of people I see no duality. It has become like a wilderness to me, so what could I take delight in?

  10. 2.23

    Oh! In me, the boundless ocean, when the wind of the mind rises, many-coloured waves of worlds spring up at once.

  11. 2.25

    How wonderful! In me, the boundless ocean, the waves that are living beings rise, collide, play and sink back, simply by their own nature.

  12. 3.5

    How strange that the sense of 'mine' still lingers in a sage who knows the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self.

  13. 3.6

    How strange that one who abides in the supreme non-dual and is set on liberation should still fall under the sway of desire, unsettled by the habits of amorous play.

  14. 3.7

    How strange that someone very feeble, nearing his final days, should still long for sensual pleasure, though he knows it to be the enemy of knowledge.

  15. 3.8

    How strange that one who is detached from this world and the next, who can tell the eternal from the passing, and who longs for liberation, should be afraid of liberation itself.

  16. 4.2

    The state that Indra and all the gods long for and pine after — amazing, the yogi established there feels no thrill of joy.

  17. 7.5

    Ah, I am pure consciousness alone, and the world is like a magician's show. So how, and where, could the idea of rejecting or accepting anything arise for me?

  18. 17.10

    He is neither awake nor asleep, neither opens his eyes nor closes them. Ah, what a supreme state the free mind abides in!

  19. 18.56

    Pleased, he is not pleased; pained, he does not suffer. Only those like him understand that wondrous state of his.
