अष्टावक्र

The sage, the teacher

A sage born, the story goes, with his body bent in eight places. Young in years, he teaches King Janaka not a method but a direct recognition: you are already free awareness. He speaks most of the text.

एक ऋषि जिनका शरीर, कथा के अनुसार, आठ जगह से टेढ़ा था। उम्र में छोटे, वे राजा जनक को कोई विधि नहीं, सीधी पहचान सिखाते हैं: तुम पहले से ही मुक्त चैतन्य हो। ग्रंथ का अधिकांश भाग उन्हीं का है।

    Happily he sits, happily he sleeps, happily he comes and goes, happily he speaks, happily he eats: the one whose mind is at peace, even in everyday life.

  182. 18.60

    By his very nature he feels no distress, though he deals with the world like anyone else. Calm as a vast lake, his troubles gone, he shines.

  183. 18.61

    For the deluded one, even withdrawing from action turns into activity. For the wise one, even activity bears the fruit of withdrawal.

  184. 18.62

    The deluded one often shows distaste for possessions. But for one whose attachment even to the body has fallen away, where is liking and where is distaste?

  185. 18.63

    The deluded one's outlook always clings to contemplating or not contemplating. The settled one, though he contemplates what comes, has an outlook that is no outlook at all.

  186. 18.64

    The sage who goes about all undertakings without desire, like a child, is pure; no action clings to him, even as it is being done.

  187. 18.65

    Blessed indeed is the knower of the Self who is the same toward all things; seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, his mind is free of thirst.

  188. 18.66

    For the wise one who is always free of mental constructs, like the sky, where is samsara, where is appearance, where is a goal, where a means?

  189. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  190. 18.68

    Why say more? The great-souled one who has known the truth wants neither enjoyment nor liberation, and finds no relish in anything, always and everywhere.

  191. 18.69

    The dual world, from mahat onward, is just an unfolding of names. Once that is let go, what is left to do for one who is pure awareness?

  192. 18.70

    Certain that all this is a product of illusion and that nothing truly is, the pure one, whose light cannot be made an object, comes to rest by his very nature.

  193. 18.71

    For one whose nature is pure radiance and who sees no world of objects, where is rule, where dispassion, where renunciation, where even calm?

  194. 18.72

    For one who shines as the infinite and does not see nature at all, where is bondage, where liberation, where joy, where sorrow?

  195. 18.73

    In a samsara that reaches only as far as the intellect, mere illusion plays itself out. The wise one, free of 'mine', free of ego and free of desire, shines.

  196. 18.74

    For the sage who sees the Self as imperishable and free of sorrow, where is knowledge, where is the world, where the thought 'I am the body' or 'this is mine'?

  197. 18.75

    If a dull-minded person gives up practices such as stilling the mind, he falls at once into daydreams and idle chatter.

  198. 18.76

    The dull one, even after hearing that truth, does not give up his delusion. Outwardly he strains to be free of thought, but inwardly he hungers for objects.

  199. 18.77

    One whose actions have fallen away through knowledge, though in the world's eyes he still acts, finds no occasion to do or to say anything at all.

  200. 18.78

    For the wise one who is always unchanging and without fear, where is darkness, where light, where renunciation? There is nothing at all.

  201. 18.79

    For the yogi whose nature cannot be put into words, who has no fixed nature at all, where is steadiness, where discernment, where even fearlessness?

  202. 18.80

    There is no heaven, no hell, not even liberation while living. In short, in the yogi's vision there is nothing at all.

  203. 18.81

    He neither prays for gain nor grieves over loss. The cool mind of the wise one is filled as if with nectar.

  204. 18.82

    Free of desire, he neither praises the peaceful nor blames the wicked. Content, the same in sorrow and joy, he sees nothing that must be done.

  205. 18.83

    The wise one does not hate samsara, nor does he long to see the Self. Free of joy and anger, he is neither dead nor alive.

  206. 18.84

    Without attachment to son, wife and the rest, without desire for sense-objects, unconcerned even about his own body, free of hopes, the wise one shines.

  207. 18.85

    The wise one is content everywhere, living on whatever comes his way, wandering freely from place to place, lying down wherever the sun sets.

  208. 18.86

    Let the body fall or rise; the great soul does not care, for resting on the ground of his own nature he has forgotten all of samsara.

  209. 18.87

    Owning nothing, moving as he pleases, beyond the pairs of opposites, his doubts cut away, unattached to all things, the wise one delights, alone.

  210. 18.88

    The wise one, free of 'mine', shines; to him a clod of earth, a stone and gold are all the same. The knot of his heart is cut through, and restlessness and dullness are shaken off.

  211. 18.89

    He fixes his attention on nothing in particular, and no craving remains in his heart. With whom could such a free and fully content soul be compared?

  212. 18.90

    Knowing, he does not know; seeing, he does not see; speaking, he does not speak. Who could this be but one free of all craving?

  213. 18.91

    Beggar or king, the one who is free of desire shines, the one whose sense of things as good or bad has fallen away.

  214. 18.92

    For the yogi who has become simple and without guile, whose purpose is fulfilled, where is license, where restraint, where even the settling of truth?

  215. 18.93

    What is felt within by one content in resting in the Self, free of hope and free of pain — how could it be told, and to whom?

  216. 18.94

    Asleep, he is not in deep sleep; in dream, he is not asleep; awake, he is not awake. The wise one is content at every step.

  217. 18.95

    The knower, though he has thoughts, is free of thought; though he has senses, is beyond the senses; though he has intellect, is beyond intellect; though he has an ego, is without ego.

  218. 18.96

    He is neither happy nor sad, neither detached nor attached, neither seeking liberation nor liberated; he is neither something nor nothing.

  219. 18.97

    The blessed one is not distracted in distraction, not absorbed in samadhi, not dull in dullness, and not learned in learning.

  220. 18.98

    The free one, at ease with things as they are, at peace having done what was to be done, the same everywhere, free of thirst, does not dwell on what he did or left undone.

  221. 18.99

    He is not pleased when praised, nor angered when blamed. He neither dreads death nor rejoices in life.

  222. 18.100

    The one whose mind is at peace runs neither toward the crowd nor toward the forest. However things are, wherever he is, he remains the same.
