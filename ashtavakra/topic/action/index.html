Action and inaction

Action and inaction

कर्म

karma, pravṛtti, nivṛtti

Both doing and refraining belong to the body and mind. The wise one neither clings to action nor makes a show of giving it up.

करना और न करना दोनों शरीर और मन के हैं। ज्ञानी न कर्म से चिपकता है, न उसे छोड़ने का दिखावा करता है।

    Things done and not done, and the pairs of opposites — when have they ever ended, and for whom? Knowing this, be dispassionate here, given to letting go and bound by no vows.

  9. 16.2

  10. 16.4

  12. 16.8

    Activity breeds attachment, and withdrawal breeds aversion. The wise person, free of opposites like a child, simply stays as he is.

  14. 17.12

    Seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, taking, speaking, walking, yet free of wanting and not wanting, the great-souled one is truly free.

  15. 17.19

    Without 'mine', without 'I', certain that nothing is, with every hope melted away within, he does nothing even while acting.

  17. 18.8

  18. 18.12

    For the yogi who is free while living, there is nothing at all left to do and no attachment in the heart; he simply lives as life comes.

  22. 18.26

  23. 18.29

  25. 18.33

  26. 18.48

    Just by hearing the truth, his mind becomes pure and untroubled. He sees no proper conduct, no improper conduct, and no indifference either.

  27. 18.49

  28. 18.57

  29. 18.58

  31. 18.64

  32. 18.69

  33. 18.77

    One whose actions have fallen away through knowledge, though in the world's eyes he still acts, finds no occasion to do or to say anything at all.

  34. 18.82

    Free of desire, he neither praises the peaceful nor blames the wicked. Content, the same in sorrow and joy, he sees nothing that must be done.

  35. 18.98

    The free one, at ease with things as they are, at peace having done what was to be done, the same everywhere, free of thirst, does not dwell on what he did or left undone.

    Where is creation, where is destruction, where is the goal, where is the means? Where is the seeker, and where is attainment, for me in my own non-dual nature?
