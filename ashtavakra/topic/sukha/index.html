Happiness and ease

Happiness and ease

सुखम्

sukha

Ashtavakra's refrain: 'be happy' (sukhī bhava), 'move about at ease'. Ease is the sign of knowledge, not its reward.

अष्टावक्र की टेक: 'सुखी हो जाओ', 'सुख से विचरो'। सुख ज्ञान का चिह्न है, उसका पुरस्कार नहीं।

33 verses

  1. 1.5

    You are not a Brahmin or any other caste, you belong to no stage of life, and you are not an object of the senses. Unattached and formless, you are the witness of all. Be happy.

  2. 1.8

    You have been bitten by the great black snake of the ego that says, 'I am the doer.' Drink the nectar of the conviction 'I am not the doer,' and be happy.

  3. 1.9

    With the fire of the certainty 'I am the one, pure awareness,' burn down the dense forest of ignorance. Free from sorrow, be happy.

  4. 1.10

    That in which this world appears, imagined like a snake on a rope, that awareness, bliss beyond all bliss, is what you are. Live happily.

  5. 10.3

    Wherever there is craving, know that the world of becoming is right there. Taking firm hold of mature dispassion, be free of craving and be happy.

  6. 11.5

    One who knows for certain that suffering in this world comes from anxious thinking and nothing else is freed of it — happy, at peace, his wants fallen away everywhere.

  7. 13.1

    The ease that comes from owning nothing is hard to find even for one who wears only a loincloth. So, letting go of both giving up and taking, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  8. 13.2

    Somewhere the body gets weary, somewhere the tongue gets tired, somewhere the mind. Leaving all that behind, I rest happily in life's true aim.

  9. 13.3

    Seeing truly that nothing is ever really done, I do whatever comes to be done when it comes, and live at ease, as it suits me.

  10. 13.4

    Yogis who identify with the body insist on action or on inaction. Free of both union and separation, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  11. 13.5

    I gain nothing and lose nothing by standing, walking or lying down; so whether I stand, walk or sleep, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  12. 13.6

    I lose nothing by sleeping and gain nothing by striving. So, letting go of both loss and elation, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  13. 13.7

    Having seen again and again how pleasure and the like come and go without any rule among things, I let go of good and bad, and live at ease, as it suits me.

  14. 15.5

    Attraction and aversion belong to the mind, and the mind is never yours. You are free of thought, pure awareness, unchanging. Go about happily.

  15. 15.6

    Knowing the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self, be free of 'I' and 'mine', and be happy.

  16. 15.15

    Give up dividing things into 'this I am' and 'this I am not'. Knowing for certain that all is the Self, let go of intentions and be happy.

  17. 16.4

    Happiness belongs to that master of idleness who finds even opening and closing his eyes a bother, and to no one else.

  18. 17.1

    One who is content, with senses clear, and always delights in being alone, has won the fruit of knowledge and the fruit of yoga practice as well.

  19. 17.3

    No sense objects ever delight one who rejoices in the Self, just as neem leaves do not please an elephant that loves sallaki leaves.

  20. 17.7

    He neither wishes for the world to dissolve nor resents its going on. So the blessed one lives happily on whatever livelihood comes.

  21. 17.8

    Fulfilled by this knowledge, his thinking mind dissolved, the accomplished one lives happily, seeing, hearing, touching, smelling and eating.

  22. 18.1

    Salutations to that peaceful radiance, whose nature is bliss alone, with the dawning of whose knowledge delusion at once becomes like a dream.

  23. 18.2

    A person may gain every kind of wealth and win abundant pleasures, yet no one can be happy without giving up everything.

  24. 18.3

    When one's inner being is scorched by the blazing sun of the pain of duties, where is happiness without a downpour of the nectar of calm?

  25. 18.20

    The wise one does not cling to either activity or withdrawal. Whatever comes to be done, he does it and stays at ease.

  26. 18.26

    He acts like one who does not profess this teaching, yet he is no simpleton. Free while living, happy and graced, he shines even as he moves through the world.

  27. 18.47

    Free of doubt and with a settled mind, he takes up no methods for liberation. Seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, he lives at ease.

  28. 18.50

    Through freedom one finds happiness; through freedom one gains the highest; through freedom one reaches peace; through freedom, the supreme state.

  29. 18.59

    Happily he sits, happily he sleeps, happily he comes and goes, happily he speaks, happily he eats: the one whose mind is at peace, even in everyday life.

  30. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  31. 18.85

    The wise one is content everywhere, living on whatever comes his way, wandering freely from place to place, lying down wherever the sun sets.

  32. 18.93

    What is felt within by one content in resting in the Self, free of hope and free of pain — how could it be told, and to whom?

  33. 20.10

    Where is everyday life, and where is ultimate truth? Where is happiness, and where is sorrow, for me who am always beyond thought?
