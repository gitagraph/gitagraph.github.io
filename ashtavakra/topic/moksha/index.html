Liberation

Liberation

मोक्षः

mokṣa, mukti

Not something to attain later. 'Think yourself free and you are free.' The text's last word is that even liberation is not a thing.

बाद में पाने की कोई चीज़ नहीं। 'अपने को मुक्त मानो, तो मुक्त हो।' ग्रंथ अंत में कहता है कि मुक्ति भी कोई वस्तु नहीं।

44 verses

  1. 1.1

    How does one gain knowledge? How will liberation come? And how is dispassion reached? Tell me this, master.

  2. 1.2

    If you want liberation, child, drop the objects of the senses as you would poison. Take up patience, honesty, kindness, contentment and truth as you would nectar.

  3. 1.3

    You are not earth, nor water, nor fire, nor air, nor space. To be free, know yourself as the Self, the witness of all these, whose nature is consciousness.

  4. 1.4

    If you set the body apart and rest in consciousness, you will this very moment be happy, at peace, and free from bondage.

  5. 1.6

    Right and wrong, pleasure and pain belong to the mind, not to you, all-pervading one. You are neither the doer nor the enjoyer; you have always been free.

  6. 1.7

    You are the one seer of everything, and you are always essentially free. Your only bondage is this: you see the seer as something other than yourself.

  7. 1.11

    One who thinks himself free is free, and one who thinks himself bound is bound. The saying holds true here: as you think, so you become.

  8. 1.18

    Know that whatever has form is unreal, and the formless is unmoving. Through this teaching of the truth, you will not be born into the world again.

  9. 2.18

    For me there is neither bondage nor liberation. The illusion has lost its ground and come to rest. Oh! The world rests in me, and yet in truth it does not rest in me.

  10. 2.20

    The body, heaven and hell, bondage and liberation, and fear too: all this is mere imagining. What have I, who am consciousness, to do with any of it?

  11. 3.8

    How strange that one who is detached from this world and the next, who can tell the eternal from the passing, and who longs for liberation, should be afraid of liberation itself.

  12. 5.1

    You are tied to nothing at all. Being pure, what would you renounce? Letting this bundle of body and mind fall apart, just so, let yourself dissolve.

  13. 8.2

    It is liberation when the mind does not want or grieve, does not let go or grab, is not pleased or angered.

  14. 8.3

    It is bondage when the mind clings to any of the things it sees. It is liberation when the mind clings to nothing it sees.

  15. 8.4

    When there is no 'I', there is liberation; when there is 'I', there is bondage. Seeing this, lightly and without effort, neither take hold of anything nor push anything away.

  16. 9.7

    See the changes of the elements as they truly are, as nothing but the elements. In that very moment you will be free of bondage and rest in your own nature.

  17. 10.4

    Bondage is made of craving alone, and its ending is called liberation. Simply by not clinging to the world, one finds contentment again and again.

  18. 11.6

    One who knows for certain, 'I am not the body, the body is not mine, I am awareness,' is as if he has reached absolute aloneness; he does not recall what was done or left undone.

  19. 14.3

    Now that I have known the witness, the supreme Self and the Lord, and have no hope left of bondage or freedom, I have no worry about liberation.

  20. 15.2

    Liberation is losing the taste for sense objects; bondage is relishing them. That is all there is to know. Now do as you wish.

  21. 15.18

    In the ocean of existence there was, is and will be only one. You have no bondage and no liberation. Your work is done; go about happily.

  22. 15.20

    Give up meditation altogether; hold nothing in your heart. You are the Self, already free. What will you achieve by pondering?

  23. 16.10

    One who takes pride even in liberation and still claims the body as his own is neither a knower nor a yogi; he simply has his share of suffering.

  24. 17.5

    In this world we see people hungry for enjoyment and people hungry for liberation, but rare indeed is the great-souled one who wants neither.

  25. 17.10

    He is neither awake nor asleep, neither opens his eyes nor closes them. Ah, what a supreme state the free mind abides in!

  26. 17.18

    With an empty mind, as if established in complete aloneness (kaivalya), he does not even know the notions of focused and unfocused, good and bad.

  27. 18.7

    Knowing that everything is mere imagination and that the Self is free and eternal, why would the wise one go on practising like a child?

  28. 18.14

    Where is delusion, where is the world, where is meditation on it, where is liberation, for the great soul who rests at the far edge of all intentions?

  29. 18.21

    Free of latent desires, leaning on nothing, moving as he pleases, his bonds undone, he is tossed by the wind of past impressions and drifts about like a dry leaf.

  30. 18.30

    The mind of the free one shines: not agitated and not gratified, not acting, without stirring, without hopes, its doubts gone.

  31. 18.36

    The deluded one does not reach liberation through the action of repeated practice. The blessed one, through understanding alone, is free and remains unchanged.

  32. 18.44

    The mind of one seeking liberation cannot stand without something to lean on. The mind of the free one is always without support and without desire.

  33. 18.47

    Free of doubt and with a settled mind, he takes up no methods for liberation. Seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, he lives at ease.

  34. 18.50

    Through freedom one finds happiness; through freedom one gains the highest; through freedom one reaches peace; through freedom, the supreme state.

  35. 18.53

    The wise, free of imaginings, unbound, their minds free, may enjoy great luxuries or retreat into mountain caves.

  36. 18.68

    Why say more? The great-souled one who has known the truth wants neither enjoyment nor liberation, and finds no relish in anything, always and everywhere.

  37. 18.72

    For one who shines as the infinite and does not see nature at all, where is bondage, where liberation, where joy, where sorrow?

  38. 18.80

    There is no heaven, no hell, not even liberation while living. In short, in the yogi's vision there is nothing at all.

  39. 18.89

    He fixes his attention on nothing in particular, and no craving remains in his heart. With whom could such a free and fully content soul be compared?

  40. 18.96

    He is neither happy nor sad, neither detached nor attached, neither seeking liberation nor liberated; he is neither something nor nothing.

  41. 18.98

    The free one, at ease with things as they are, at peace having done what was to be done, the same everywhere, free of thirst, does not dwell on what he did or left undone.

  42. 20.4

    Where are the past actions now bearing fruit, where is liberation while living, and where is that final freedom after the body falls, for me who am always without distinctions?

  43. 20.6

    Where is the world, where is the seeker of liberation, where is the yogi, where is the knower? Where is the bound, and where is the free, for me in my own non-dual nature?

  44. 20.12

    Where is activity, where is withdrawal, where is liberation, where is bondage, for me who am always unchanging, undivided and at rest in myself?
