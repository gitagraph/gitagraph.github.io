Wealth, pleasure, duty

Wealth, pleasure, duty

पुरुषार्थाः

artha, kāma, dharma

The ordinary aims of life, and enjoyment (bhoga). The text sees through them all, even religious duty.

जीवन के साधारण लक्ष्य, और भोग। ग्रंथ इन सबको आर-पार देखता है, धर्म को भी।

17 verses

  1. 3.1

    Having truly known the Self as one and indestructible, how can you, a wise knower of the Self, still take pleasure in earning wealth?

  2. 10.1

    Put away desire, which is your enemy, and wealth, which is full of trouble, and even duty, which is the root of both. Be indifferent to all of them.

  3. 10.2

    See friends, lands, money, houses, wives, inheritances and all such riches as a dream or a magic show, lasting three days or five.

  4. 10.6

    Kingdoms, sons, wives, bodies and pleasures have been lost to you birth after birth, even though you clung to them.

  5. 10.7

    Enough of wealth, of pleasure, and even of good deeds. In the wilderness of the world, the mind never found rest through any of them.

  6. 14.2

    Where now is wealth, where are friends, where are the thieves that are my senses' objects? Where is scripture, where is knowledge, now that my wanting has melted away?

  7. 16.2

    Enjoy pleasures, do your work, or practise samadhi, knowing one; still, your mind will long above all for the state where every hope has fallen away.

  8. 16.5

    When the mind is free of opposites like 'this is done, this is not done', it no longer cares for duty, wealth, pleasure or liberation.

  9. 17.6

    Only a rare, open-hearted person has no sense of rejecting or accepting duty, wealth, pleasure, liberation, life or death.

  10. 18.2

    A person may gain every kind of wealth and win abundant pleasures, yet no one can be happy without giving up everything.

  11. 18.12

    Where is duty, where is pleasure, where is wealth, where is discernment, for the yogi freed from opposites like 'this is done, this is not done'?

  12. 18.53

    The wise, free of imaginings, unbound, their minds free, may enjoy great luxuries or retreat into mountain caves.

  13. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  14. 18.68

    Why say more? The great-souled one who has known the truth wants neither enjoyment nor liberation, and finds no relish in anything, always and everywhere.

  15. 19.2

    Where is duty, where is pleasure, where is wealth, where is discernment? Where is duality, and where is non-duality, for me who abides in my own glory?

  16. 19.8

    Enough of talk about the three aims of life, enough of talk about yoga, enough even of talk about knowledge, for me who rests in the Self.

  17. 20.13

    Where is instruction, where is scripture, where is the disciple, where is the teacher? Where is any goal of life, for me who am free of all limits and auspicious?
