Knowledge

Knowledge

ज्ञानम्

jñāna, tattvajñāna

Knowing the Self directly, which the text treats as instant and sufficient, not as a long study.

आत्मा को सीधे जानना, जिसे ग्रंथ तुरंत और पर्याप्त मानता है, लंबा अध्ययन नहीं।

38 verses

  1. 1.1

    How does one gain knowledge? How will liberation come? And how is dispassion reached? Tell me this, master.

  2. 1.14

    For a long time, my child, you have been bound by the noose of identifying with the body. Cut it with the sword of the knowledge 'I am awareness,' and be happy.

  3. 2.5

    Just as cloth, when examined, turns out to be nothing but thread, so this world, when examined, is nothing but the Self.

  4. 2.7

    The world appears through not knowing the Self and does not appear when the Self is known, just as a snake appears through not knowing the rope and vanishes once the rope is known.

  5. 2.15

    Knowledge, the known and the knower: this triad is not real. I am that stainless one in whom it appears through ignorance.

  6. 3.1

    Having truly known the Self as one and indestructible, how can you, a wise knower of the Self, still take pleasure in earning wealth?

  7. 3.7

    How strange that someone very feeble, nearing his final days, should still long for sensual pleasure, though he knows it to be the enemy of knowledge.

  8. 3.12

    The great soul whose mind wants nothing, even in disappointment, and who is content with knowledge of the Self: with whom could he be compared?

  9. 4.3

    For one who knows That, merit and sin never touch the inner self, just as smoke, though it seems to, never truly clings to the sky.

  10. 6.1

    I am boundless like space; the world of nature is like a pot. This is knowledge, and so there is nothing to give up, nothing to take hold of, nothing to dissolve.

  11. 6.2

    I am like the great ocean; this spread of the world is like its waves. This is knowledge, and so there is nothing to give up, nothing to take hold of, nothing to dissolve.

  12. 6.3

    I am like the mother-of-pearl; the imagined universe is like the silver seen in it. This is knowledge, and so there is nothing to give up, nothing to take hold of, nothing to dissolve.

  13. 6.4

    I am in all beings, and all beings are in me. This is knowledge, and so there is nothing to give up, nothing to take hold of, nothing to dissolve.

  14. 9.7

    See the changes of the elements as they truly are, as nothing but the elements. In that very moment you will be free of bondage and rest in your own nature.

  15. 11.1

    One who knows for certain that existence, non-existence and change all arise from the nature of things becomes free of change and distress, and easily finds peace.

  16. 12.6

    Doing actions springs from ignorance, and so does ceasing from them. Understanding this truth fully, I remain just as I am.

  17. 15.1

    A person of pure understanding reaches life's goal even from a passing word of instruction, while another may keep inquiring all his life and still stay confused about it.

  18. 15.3

    This knowledge of truth turns an eloquent, clever, hard-striving person into one who is silent, still and idle. That is why those hungry for enjoyment avoid it.

  19. 15.8

    Have faith, child, have faith; do not be confused about this, my dear. You are the Self, knowledge itself, the Lord, beyond nature (prakṛti).

  20. 17.1

    One who is content, with senses clear, and always delights in being alone, has won the fruit of knowledge and the fruit of yoga practice as well.

  21. 17.2

    Ah, the knower of truth is never troubled in this world, for he alone fills this whole sphere of the universe.

  22. 17.8

    Fulfilled by this knowledge, his thinking mind dissolved, the accomplished one lives happily, seeing, hearing, touching, smelling and eating.

  23. 18.6

    Simply by ending delusion and simply by grasping their own nature, those whose sight is unveiled live radiant and free of sorrow.

  24. 18.32

    Hearing the truth as it really is, a dull person becomes bewildered; while some who are not deluded draw inward and seem like fools.

  25. 18.34

    Neither by effort nor by giving up effort does the deluded one find peace. The wise one is at peace simply by being certain of the truth.

  26. 18.36

    The deluded one does not reach liberation through the action of repeated practice. The blessed one, through understanding alone, is free and remains unchanged.

  27. 18.39

    The deluded one finds no peace, because he tries to make himself peaceful. The wise one, having settled the truth, is at peace in mind always.

  28. 18.48

    Just by hearing the truth, his mind becomes pure and untroubled. He sees no proper conduct, no improper conduct, and no indifference either.

  29. 18.65

    Blessed indeed is the knower of the Self who is the same toward all things; seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, his mind is free of thirst.

  30. 18.74

    For the sage who sees the Self as imperishable and free of sorrow, where is knowledge, where is the world, where the thought 'I am the body' or 'this is mine'?

  31. 18.77

    One whose actions have fallen away through knowledge, though in the world's eyes he still acts, finds no occasion to do or to say anything at all.

  32. 18.92

    For the yogi who has become simple and without guile, whose purpose is fulfilled, where is license, where restraint, where even the settling of truth?

  33. 19.1

    With the tongs of the knowledge of truth, I have pulled out from deep within my heart the thorn of countless opinions.

  34. 19.8

    Enough of talk about the three aims of life, enough of talk about yoga, enough even of talk about knowledge, for me who rests in the Self.

  35. 20.2

    Where is scripture, where is knowledge of the Self, where is a mind free of objects? Where is contentment, and where is freedom from craving, for me who am always beyond the pairs of opposites?

  36. 20.3

    Where is knowledge, where is ignorance, where is 'I', where is 'this', where is 'mine'? Where is bondage, where is liberation? How can my own nature have any form?

  37. 20.5

    Where is the doer, where is the enjoyer, where is actionless shining? Where is direct realisation, and where is its fruit, for me who am always without any nature of my own?

  38. 20.8

    Where is the knower, where is the means of knowing, where is the known, where is knowing itself? Where is something, and where is nothing, for me who am always pure?
