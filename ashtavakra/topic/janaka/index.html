जनक

King of Videha, the disciple

The philosopher-king of Videha, famous in the Upanishads for living in the world while free of it. He asks the opening questions, wakes up quickly, and then describes his own realisation in chapters of his own.

विदेह के दार्शनिक राजा, जो उपनिषदों में संसार में रहते हुए भी उससे मुक्त रहने के लिए प्रसिद्ध हैं। वे आरंभिक प्रश्न पूछते हैं, जल्दी जागते हैं, और फिर अपने अध्यायों में अपना अनुभव बताते हैं।

  1. 1.1

    How does one gain knowledge? How will liberation come? And how is dispassion reached? Tell me this, master.

  2. 1.2

    If you want liberation, child, drop the objects of the senses as you would poison. Take up patience, honesty, kindness, contentment and truth as you would nectar.

  3. 1.6

    Right and wrong, pleasure and pain belong to the mind, not to you, all-pervading one. You are neither the doer nor the enjoyer; you have always been free.

  4. 1.14

    For a long time, my child, you have been bound by the noose of identifying with the body. Cut it with the sword of the knowledge 'I am awareness,' and be happy.

  5. 2.1

    Oh! I am stainless, at peace, pure awareness, beyond nature. All this time I have simply been fooled by delusion.

  6. 2.2

    As I alone light up this body, so I light up the world. So either the whole world is mine, or nothing at all is.

  7. 2.3

    Oh! Now that I have let go of the world along with the body, by some fine skill the supreme Self is seen.

  8. 2.4

    Just as waves, foam and bubbles are not different from water, so the world that has come forth from the Self is not different from the Self.

  9. 2.5

    Just as cloth, when examined, turns out to be nothing but thread, so this world, when examined, is nothing but the Self.

  10. 2.6

    Just as sugar formed from cane juice is wholly pervaded by that juice, so the world imagined in me is pervaded by me through and through.

  11. 2.7

    The world appears through not knowing the Self and does not appear when the Self is known, just as a snake appears through not knowing the rope and vanishes once the rope is known.

  12. 2.8

    Light is my very nature; I am nothing other than that. When the world shines, it is only I who shine.

  13. 2.9

    Oh! Through ignorance the imagined world appears in me, like silver in a shell, a snake in a rope, or water in the sun's rays.

  14. 2.10

    The world that came out of me will dissolve back into me alone, like a pot into clay, a wave into water, a bracelet into gold.

  15. 2.11

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself, who has no destruction and remains even when the whole world, from Brahma down to a blade of grass, is destroyed.

  16. 2.12

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! Though I have a body I am one; I neither go anywhere nor come from anywhere, but abide pervading the whole world.

  17. 2.13

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself! No one here is as capable as I, who have held up the world for so long without touching it with the body.

  18. 2.14

    Oh, how wonderful I am! Salutations to myself, who has nothing at all, or else who has everything that speech and mind can reach.

  19. 2.15

    Knowledge, the known and the knower: this triad is not real. I am that stainless one in whom it appears through ignorance.

  20. 2.16

    Oh! Duality is the root of suffering, and there is no other cure for it than this: all that is seen is false, and I am one, pure, the very essence of consciousness.

  21. 2.17

    I am pure awareness; through ignorance I imagined limitations on myself. Reflecting on this constantly, I abide beyond all thought.

  22. 2.18

    For me there is neither bondage nor liberation. The illusion has lost its ground and come to rest. Oh! The world rests in me, and yet in truth it does not rest in me.

  23. 2.19

    It is now certain that this world, body included, is nothing, and that the Self is pure consciousness alone. So on what could imagination now rest?

  24. 2.20

    The body, heaven and hell, bondage and liberation, and fear too: all this is mere imagining. What have I, who am consciousness, to do with any of it?

  25. 2.21

    Oh! Even in a crowd of people I see no duality. It has become like a wilderness to me, so what could I take delight in?

  26. 2.22

    I am not the body, nor is the body mine; I am not the individual soul, I am consciousness. My only bondage was this: the craving for life.

  27. 2.23

    Oh! In me, the boundless ocean, when the wind of the mind rises, many-coloured waves of worlds spring up at once.

  28. 2.24

    In me, the boundless ocean, when the wind of the mind dies down, the ship of the world is wrecked, to the misfortune of its merchant, the individual soul.

  29. 2.25

    How wonderful! In me, the boundless ocean, the waves that are living beings rise, collide, play and sink back, simply by their own nature.

  30. 3.1

    Having truly known the Self as one and indestructible, how can you, a wise knower of the Self, still take pleasure in earning wealth?

  31. 3.3

    Having known 'I am that in which this world shimmers like waves on the sea,' why do you run about like someone poor?

  32. 4.1

    Ah, the wise one who knows the Self plays the game of life's enjoyments, and bears no likeness at all to the deluded, who are hauled along like beasts of burden by the world.

  33. 4.2

    The state that Indra and all the gods long for and pine after — amazing, the yogi established there feels no thrill of joy.

  34. 4.3

    For one who knows That, merit and sin never touch the inner self, just as smoke, though it seems to, never truly clings to the sky.

  35. 4.4

    Who could restrain the great soul who has known this whole world as the Self alone, and who moves about as things happen to come?

  36. 4.5

    Among the four kinds of beings, from Brahma down to a blade of grass, only the one who truly knows has the power to drop both wanting and not-wanting.

  37. 4.6

    Rare is the one who knows the Self as one without a second, lord of the world. He does whatever he knows to do, and has no fear from anywhere.

  38. 7.1

    In me, the boundless ocean, the ship of the world drifts here and there, driven by its own wind. I am not troubled by it.

  39. 7.2

    In me, the boundless ocean, the waves of the world rise or sink by their own nature. Let them rise or vanish; I neither grow nor shrink.

  40. 7.3

    In me, the boundless ocean, the universe is only an imagining. Utterly still and without form, in this alone I abide.

  41. 7.4

    The Self is not in things, nor are things in that boundless, spotless one. So, unattached, wanting nothing and at peace, in this alone I abide.

  42. 7.5

    Ah, I am pure consciousness alone, and the world is like a magician's show. So how, and where, could the idea of rejecting or accepting anything arise for me?

  43. 9.2

    Child, only some rare blessed one, by watching how people strive, has lost the wish to live, the hunger to enjoy, and the urge to know.

  44. 12.1

    First I lost patience with the work of the body, then with long talk, and then with thinking itself; and so I remain just as I am.

  45. 12.2

    Since I take no delight in sound and the other objects, and since the Self is never something seen, my heart is free of scattering and rests one-pointed; and so I remain just as I am.

  46. 12.3

    Scattering comes from false superimposition and the like, and so one takes up practice for samadhi. Seeing this pattern, I remain just as I am.

  47. 12.4

    Having nothing to reject or accept, and so no joy or sorrow, today, O brahmin, I remain just as I am.

  48. 12.5

    Stages of life or none at all, meditation, renouncing what the mind clings to — seeing that through these only my own mental fancies arise, I remain just as I am.

  49. 12.6

    Doing actions springs from ignorance, and so does ceasing from them. Understanding this truth fully, I remain just as I am.

  50. 12.7

    Even when thinking of the unthinkable, one still takes on the form of thought. So, giving up even that contemplation, I remain just as I am.

  51. 12.8

    Whoever has done just this has fulfilled the purpose of life. Whoever is just so by nature has fulfilled the purpose of life.

  52. 13.1

    The ease that comes from owning nothing is hard to find even for one who wears only a loincloth. So, letting go of both giving up and taking, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  53. 13.2

    Somewhere the body gets weary, somewhere the tongue gets tired, somewhere the mind. Leaving all that behind, I rest happily in life's true aim.

  54. 13.3

    Seeing truly that nothing is ever really done, I do whatever comes to be done when it comes, and live at ease, as it suits me.

  55. 13.4

    Yogis who identify with the body insist on action or on inaction. Free of both union and separation, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  56. 13.5

    I gain nothing and lose nothing by standing, walking or lying down; so whether I stand, walk or sleep, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  57. 13.6

    I lose nothing by sleeping and gain nothing by striving. So, letting go of both loss and elation, I live at ease, as it suits me.

  58. 13.7

    Having seen again and again how pleasure and the like come and go without any rule among things, I let go of good and bad, and live at ease, as it suits me.

  59. 14.1

    One whose mind is by nature empty, who thinks of things only by oversight, who is as if awake even in sleep — his memories of worldly life have worn away.

  60. 14.2

    Where now is wealth, where are friends, where are the thieves that are my senses' objects? Where is scripture, where is knowledge, now that my wanting has melted away?

  61. 14.3

    Now that I have known the witness, the supreme Self and the Lord, and have no hope left of bondage or freedom, I have no worry about liberation.

  62. 14.4

    He is empty of thoughts within and moves freely without, seeming like someone confused; only those like him can know the states he passes through.

  63. 15.7

    That in which this world shimmers like waves on the sea, that is what you are, without doubt. You who are consciousness itself, let the fever go.

  64. 15.8

    Have faith, child, have faith; do not be confused about this, my dear. You are the Self, knowledge itself, the Lord, beyond nature (prakṛti).

  65. 15.12

    Child, you are pure consciousness; this world is not separate from you. So who could reject or accept anything, and how, and where?

  66. 15.19

    You who are made of consciousness, do not stir up your mind with wanting and doubting. Grow calm and rest happily in your own Self, which is bliss itself.

  67. 16.1

    You may recite or listen to many scriptures again and again, child, yet you will not rest in yourself until you forget everything.

  68. 16.2

    Enjoy pleasures, do your work, or practise samādhi, knowing one; still, your mind will long above all for the state where every hope has fallen away.
