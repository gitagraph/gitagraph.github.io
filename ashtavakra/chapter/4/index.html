Chapter 4 · 6 verses

The Knower at Play

धीरस्य लीला

Janaka replies that the one who knows the Self plays through life like a game. Such a person cannot be compared with those who carry the world like a load. Even the gods long for the state the knower rests in, and merit and sin do not touch them, as smoke does not stain the sky.

जनक उत्तर देते हैं कि आत्मा को जानने वाला जीवन को खेल की तरह जीता है। ऐसे व्यक्ति की तुलना उन लोगों से नहीं हो सकती जो संसार को बोझ की तरह ढोते हैं। देवता भी उस अवस्था को चाहते हैं जिसमें ज्ञानी विश्राम करता है, और पुण्य-पाप उसे वैसे ही नहीं छूते जैसे धुआँ आकाश को।

  1. 4.1

    Ah, the wise one who knows the Self plays the game of life's enjoyments, and bears no likeness at all to the deluded, who are hauled along like beasts of burden by the world.

  2. 4.2

    The state that Indra and all the gods long for and pine after — amazing, the yogi established there feels no thrill of joy.

  3. 4.3

    For one who knows That, merit and sin never touch the inner self, just as smoke, though it seems to, never truly clings to the sky.

  4. 4.4

    Who could restrain the great soul who has known this whole world as the Self alone, and who moves about as things happen to come?

  5. 4.5

    Among the four kinds of beings, from Brahma down to a blade of grass, only the one who truly knows has the power to drop both wanting and not-wanting.

  6. 4.6

    Rare is the one who knows the Self as one without a second, lord of the world. He does whatever he knows to do, and has no fear from anywhere.
