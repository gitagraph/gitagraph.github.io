Chapter 3 · 14 verses

The Test

आत्मज्ञस्य परीक्षा

Ashtavakra tests the new disciple. If you truly know the Self as one and imperishable, why are you still drawn to wealth, to pleasure, to the body of a lover? Why fear, why hope? Each verse holds up a gap between knowing and living.

अष्टावक्र नए शिष्य की परीक्षा लेते हैं। यदि तुम सचमुच आत्मा को एक और अविनाशी जानते हो, तो अब भी धन, भोग और प्रिय के शरीर की ओर क्यों खिंचते हो? भय क्यों, आशा क्यों? हर श्लोक जानने और जीने के बीच की दूरी दिखाता है।

  1. 3.1

    Having truly known the Self as one and indestructible, how can you, a wise knower of the Self, still take pleasure in earning wealth?

  2. 3.2

    Ah, it is from not knowing the Self that one delights in the illusory objects of the senses, just as greed arises for the silver mistakenly seen in a shell.

  3. 3.3

    Having known 'I am that in which this world shimmers like waves on the sea,' why do you run about like someone poor?

  4. 3.4

    Even after hearing that the Self is pure consciousness and supremely beautiful, a person who is deeply attached to sex falls into impurity.

  5. 3.5

    How strange that the sense of 'mine' still lingers in a sage who knows the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self.

  6. 3.6

    How strange that one who abides in the supreme non-dual and is set on liberation should still fall under the sway of desire, unsettled by the habits of amorous play.

  7. 3.7

    How strange that someone very feeble, nearing his final days, should still long for sensual pleasure, though he knows it to be the enemy of knowledge.

  8. 3.8

    How strange that one who is detached from this world and the next, who can tell the eternal from the passing, and who longs for liberation, should be afraid of liberation itself.

  9. 3.9

    But the wise one, whether being feasted or tormented, always sees only the Self, and is neither pleased nor angered.

  10. 3.10

    The great-hearted one watches his own body act as if it were someone else's. How could praise or blame disturb him?

  11. 3.11

    Seeing this world as mere illusion, with all curiosity gone, how could one of steady mind fear even death when it is near?

  12. 3.12

    The great soul whose mind wants nothing, even in disappointment, and who is content with knowledge of the Self: with whom could he be compared?

  13. 3.13

    Knowing by his very nature that all that is seen is nothing, how could the one of steady mind see this as something to grasp and that as something to reject?

  14. 3.14

    For one who has inwardly let go of all impurities, who is beyond the pairs of opposites and free of hope, pleasure that comes by chance brings neither pain nor satisfaction.
