Chapter 18 · Peace

Chapter 18 · 100 verses

Peace

शान्तिशतकम्

The longest chapter, a hundred verses. Again and again it contrasts the wise one with the deluded one: the fool tries to control the mind, the wise one is naturally at rest; the fool practises, the wise one does what comes. The free person lives like a child, like a dry leaf in the wind, untouched by what the body does.

सबसे लंबा अध्याय, सौ श्लोक। बार-बार यह ज्ञानी और मूढ़ की तुलना करता है: मूढ़ मन को वश में करने का प्रयास करता है, ज्ञानी स्वभाव से शांत रहता है; मूढ़ अभ्यास करता है, ज्ञानी जो सामने आए वह करता है। मुक्त व्यक्ति बच्चे की तरह, हवा में उड़ते सूखे पत्ते की तरह जीता है, शरीर जो करे उससे अछूता।

  1. 18.1

    Salutations to that peaceful radiance, whose nature is bliss alone, with the dawning of whose knowledge delusion at once becomes like a dream.

  2. 18.2

    A person may gain every kind of wealth and win abundant pleasures, yet no one can be happy without giving up everything.

  3. 18.3

    When one's inner being is scorched by the blazing sun of the pain of duties, where is happiness without a downpour of the nectar of calm?

  4. 18.4

    This world is only an imagining; in truth it is nothing. But for those who see clearly into being and non-being, their own nature never ceases to be.

  5. 18.5

    The state of the Self is neither far away nor gained by narrowing down; it is already attained, free of thought, effortless, changeless and stainless.

  6. 18.6

    Simply by ending delusion and simply by grasping their own nature, those whose sight is unveiled live radiant and free of sorrow.

  7. 18.7

    Knowing that everything is mere imagination and that the Self is free and eternal, why would the wise one go on practising like a child?

  8. 18.8

    Having realised that the Self is Brahman and that being and non-being are imagined, what does the desireless one need to know, or say, or do?

  9. 18.9

    Notions like 'this I am' and 'this I am not' fade away for the yogi who, certain that all is the Self, has fallen silent.

  10. 18.10

    For the yogi who has become wholly calm there is no distraction and no focus, no heightened knowing and no dullness, no pleasure and no pain.

  11. 18.11

    Ruling a kingdom or living on alms, gain or loss, among people or in the forest: it makes no difference to the yogi whose nature is free of thought.

  12. 18.12

    Where is duty, where is pleasure, where is wealth, where is discernment, for the yogi freed from opposites like 'this is done, this is not done'?

  13. 18.13

    For the yogi who is free while living, there is nothing at all left to do and no attachment in the heart; he simply lives as life comes.

  14. 18.14

    Where is delusion, where is the world, where is meditation on it, where is liberation, for the great soul who rests at the far edge of all intentions?

  15. 18.15

    Let the one who has seen this world try to deny it. But what is there for one without latent desires to do? Even seeing, he does not see.

  16. 18.16

    One who has seen the supreme Brahman may meditate, 'I am Brahman.' But what is there to think about for one beyond thought, who sees no second?

  17. 18.17

    One who has noticed his own distraction tries to restrain it. But the noble one is not distracted; with nothing to achieve, what should he do?

  18. 18.18

    The wise one, the reverse of ordinary people, though he lives just as they do, sees in himself neither samadhi nor distraction nor any taint.

  19. 18.19

    The awakened one, beyond being and non-being, content and free of latent desire, does nothing at all, though in the world's eyes he acts.

  20. 18.20

    The wise one does not cling to either activity or withdrawal. Whatever comes to be done, he does it and stays at ease.

  21. 18.21

    Free of latent desires, leaning on nothing, moving as he pleases, his bonds undone, he is tossed by the wind of past impressions and drifts about like a dry leaf.

  22. 18.22

    For one who has gone beyond samsara there is no elation and no dejection anywhere. His mind always cool, he shines as though he had no body.

  23. 18.23

    The wise one who delights in the Self, whose inner being is cool and clear, has no wish to give anything up and no sense of loss, anywhere.

  24. 18.24

    His mind empty by nature, acting on whatever comes, the wise one may look like an ordinary person, yet he knows neither pride nor humiliation.

  25. 18.25

    "This action was done by the body, not by me, who am pure by nature." One who keeps to this understanding does nothing, even while acting.

  26. 18.26

    He acts like one who does not profess this teaching, yet he is no simpleton. Free while living, happy and graced, he shines even as he moves through the world.

  27. 18.27

    Worn out by endless reasoning, the wise one has come to rest. He no longer imagines, knows, hears or sees.

  28. 18.28

    Since he is neither in samadhi nor distracted, he is neither a seeker of liberation nor its opposite. Seeing with certainty that all this is imagined, the great-souled one simply remains as Brahman.

  29. 18.29

    One who carries ego within is acting even when he does nothing. For the wise one free of ego, nothing is done and nothing is left undone.

  30. 18.30

    The mind of the free one shines: not agitated and not gratified, not acting, without stirring, without hopes, its doubts gone.

  31. 18.31

    The free one's mind does not set out to meditate or to act; yet, without any reason, it meditates and it acts.

  32. 18.32

    Hearing the truth as it really is, a dull person becomes bewildered; while some who are not deluded draw inward and seem like fools.

  33. 18.33

    The deluded labour hard at concentration and at stilling the mind. The wise, resting in their own state as if asleep, see nothing that needs to be done.

  34. 18.34

    Neither by effort nor by giving up effort does the deluded one find peace. The wise one is at peace simply by being certain of the truth.

  35. 18.35

    The Self is pure, awake, beloved, complete, beyond the world's spread and free of all ill. People absorbed in practice do not know it.

  36. 18.36

    The deluded one does not reach liberation through the action of repeated practice. The blessed one, through understanding alone, is free and remains unchanged.

  37. 18.37

    The deluded one does not reach Brahman, because he wants to become it. The wise one, without even wanting it, already shares the nature of the supreme Brahman.

  38. 18.38

    Without real ground and restless to grasp, the deluded keep feeding samsara. The wise have cut this root of all misery at its root.

  39. 18.39

    The deluded one finds no peace, because he tries to make himself peaceful. The wise one, having settled the truth, is at peace in mind always.

  40. 18.40

    How can one who leans on what is seen ever see the Self? The wise do not see this or that; they see the imperishable Self.

  41. 18.41

    Where is stillness for the deluded one, who forces his mind? For the wise one who delights in himself, it is always natural.

  42. 18.42

    One person holds that things exist, another holds that nothing exists. Rare is the one who holds to neither; he alone is at ease.

  43. 18.43

    People of poor understanding meditate on the Self as pure and non-dual, but in their confusion they never actually know it, and remain unfulfilled all their lives.

  44. 18.44

    The mind of one seeking liberation cannot stand without something to lean on. The mind of the free one is always without support and without desire.

  45. 18.45

    Seeing the tigers of sense-objects, the frightened run for refuge and quickly crawl into a cave, hoping to achieve control and concentration of mind.

  46. 18.46

    Seeing the lion who is free of craving, the elephants of sense-objects quietly flee; those that cannot, stay and serve him like flatterers.

  47. 18.47

    Free of doubt and with a settled mind, he takes up no methods for liberation. Seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, he lives at ease.

  48. 18.48

    Just by hearing the truth, his mind becomes pure and untroubled. He sees no proper conduct, no improper conduct, and no indifference either.

  49. 18.49

    Whatever comes to be done, the simple one does it then and there, good or bad; his actions are like a child's.

  50. 18.50

    Through freedom one finds happiness; through freedom one gains the highest; through freedom one reaches peace; through freedom, the supreme state.

  51. 18.51

    When one recognises one's own Self as neither the doer nor the enjoyer, all the movements of the mind simply wear away.

  52. 18.52

    The wise one's way of being shines, unrestrained and unplanned though it is; not so the forced calm of the deluded one whose mind is full of craving.

  53. 18.53

    The wise, free of imaginings, unbound, their minds free, may enjoy great luxuries or retreat into mountain caves.

  54. 18.54

    Seeing and honouring a learned Vedic priest, a deity, a holy place, a woman, a king or a loved one, the wise one feels no craving in his heart.

  55. 18.55

    Even when servants, sons, wives, grandsons and relatives laugh at him and scorn him, the yogi is not disturbed in the least.

  56. 18.56

    Pleased, he is not pleased; pained, he does not suffer. Only those like him understand that wondrous state of his.

  57. 18.57

    The feeling that something must be done is itself samsara. The wise do not see it, for their very form is emptiness: formless, unchanging and untouched by ill.

  58. 18.58

    The deluded one is anxious and restless everywhere, even when doing nothing. The capable one stays untroubled, even while doing what has to be done.

  59. 18.59

    Happily he sits, happily he sleeps, happily he comes and goes, happily he speaks, happily he eats: the one whose mind is at peace, even in everyday life.

  60. 18.60

    By his very nature he feels no distress, though he deals with the world like anyone else. Calm as a vast lake, his troubles gone, he shines.

  61. 18.61

    For the deluded one, even withdrawing from action turns into activity. For the wise one, even activity bears the fruit of withdrawal.

  62. 18.62

    The deluded one often shows distaste for possessions. But for one whose attachment even to the body has fallen away, where is liking and where is distaste?

  63. 18.63

    The deluded one's outlook always clings to contemplating or not contemplating. The settled one, though he contemplates what comes, has an outlook that is no outlook at all.

  64. 18.64

    The sage who goes about all undertakings without desire, like a child, is pure; no action clings to him, even as it is being done.

  65. 18.65

    Blessed indeed is the knower of the Self who is the same toward all things; seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, his mind is free of thirst.

  66. 18.66

    For the wise one who is always free of mental constructs, like the sky, where is samsara, where is appearance, where is a goal, where a means?

  67. 18.67

    Victory to the one who has renounced all aims, whose very form is the fullness of his own bliss, whose natural samadhi abides in the boundless.

  68. 18.68

    Why say more? The great-souled one who has known the truth wants neither enjoyment nor liberation, and finds no relish in anything, always and everywhere.

  69. 18.69

    The dual world, from mahat onward, is just an unfolding of names. Once that is let go, what is left to do for one who is pure awareness?

  70. 18.70

    Certain that all this is a product of illusion and that nothing truly is, the pure one, whose light cannot be made an object, comes to rest by his very nature.

  71. 18.71

    For one whose nature is pure radiance and who sees no world of objects, where is rule, where dispassion, where renunciation, where even calm?

  72. 18.72

    For one who shines as the infinite and does not see nature at all, where is bondage, where liberation, where joy, where sorrow?

  73. 18.73

    In a samsara that reaches only as far as the intellect, mere illusion plays itself out. The wise one, free of 'mine', free of ego and free of desire, shines.

  74. 18.74

    For the sage who sees the Self as imperishable and free of sorrow, where is knowledge, where is the world, where the thought 'I am the body' or 'this is mine'?

  75. 18.75

    If a dull-minded person gives up practices such as stilling the mind, he falls at once into daydreams and idle chatter.

  76. 18.76

    The dull one, even after hearing that truth, does not give up his delusion. Outwardly he strains to be free of thought, but inwardly he hungers for objects.

  77. 18.77

    One whose actions have fallen away through knowledge, though in the world's eyes he still acts, finds no occasion to do or to say anything at all.

  78. 18.78

    For the wise one who is always unchanging and without fear, where is darkness, where light, where renunciation? There is nothing at all.

  79. 18.79

    For the yogi whose nature cannot be put into words, who has no fixed nature at all, where is steadiness, where discernment, where even fearlessness?

  80. 18.80

    There is no heaven, no hell, not even liberation while living. In short, in the yogi's vision there is nothing at all.

  81. 18.81

    He neither prays for gain nor grieves over loss. The cool mind of the wise one is filled as if with nectar.

  82. 18.82

    Free of desire, he neither praises the peaceful nor blames the wicked. Content, the same in sorrow and joy, he sees nothing that must be done.

  83. 18.83

    The wise one does not hate samsara, nor does he long to see the Self. Free of joy and anger, he is neither dead nor alive.

  84. 18.84

    Without attachment to son, wife and the rest, without desire for sense-objects, unconcerned even about his own body, free of hopes, the wise one shines.

  85. 18.85

    The wise one is content everywhere, living on whatever comes his way, wandering freely from place to place, lying down wherever the sun sets.

  86. 18.86

    Let the body fall or rise; the great soul does not care, for resting on the ground of his own nature he has forgotten all of samsara.

  87. 18.87

    Owning nothing, moving as he pleases, beyond the pairs of opposites, his doubts cut away, unattached to all things, the wise one delights, alone.

  88. 18.88

    The wise one, free of 'mine', shines; to him a clod of earth, a stone and gold are all the same. The knot of his heart is cut through, and restlessness and dullness are shaken off.

  89. 18.89

    He fixes his attention on nothing in particular, and no craving remains in his heart. With whom could such a free and fully content soul be compared?

  90. 18.90

    Knowing, he does not know; seeing, he does not see; speaking, he does not speak. Who could this be but one free of all craving?

  91. 18.91

    Beggar or king, the one who is free of desire shines, the one whose sense of things as good or bad has fallen away.

  92. 18.92

    For the yogi who has become simple and without guile, whose purpose is fulfilled, where is license, where restraint, where even the settling of truth?

  93. 18.93

    What is felt within by one content in resting in the Self, free of hope and free of pain — how could it be told, and to whom?

  94. 18.94

    Asleep, he is not in deep sleep; in dream, he is not asleep; awake, he is not awake. The wise one is content at every step.

  95. 18.95

    The knower, though he has thoughts, is free of thought; though he has senses, is beyond the senses; though he has intellect, is beyond intellect; though he has an ego, is without ego.

  96. 18.96

    He is neither happy nor sad, neither detached nor attached, neither seeking liberation nor liberated; he is neither something nor nothing.

  97. 18.97

    The blessed one is not distracted in distraction, not absorbed in samadhi, not dull in dullness, and not learned in learning.

  98. 18.98

    The free one, at ease with things as they are, at peace having done what was to be done, the same everywhere, free of thirst, does not dwell on what he did or left undone.

  99. 18.99

    He is not pleased when praised, nor angered when blamed. He neither dreads death nor rejoices in life.

  100. 18.100

    The one whose mind is at peace runs neither toward the crowd nor toward the forest. However things are, wherever he is, he remains the same.
