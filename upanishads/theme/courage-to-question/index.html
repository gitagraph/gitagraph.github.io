Courage to question

Seekers who would not accept the first answer.

  • Katha Upanishad · 1.1–1.2Nachiketa and Death

    Given to Death by his angry father, a boy asks what happens after we die.

    For someone young weighing what is worth wanting, or anyone wondering what outlasts death.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 8.7–8.12Indra, Virochana and the reflection in water

    Two kings look into a pan of water. One goes home satisfied; the other keeps asking.

    For anyone who has looked in a mirror and wondered who is doing the looking.
  • Taittiriya Upanishad · 3.1–3.6Bhrigu finds bliss

    A son asks his father what is real. Again and again, the father sends him back.

    For a student wondering what really lies at the center of a life.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 2.4 and 4.5Maitreyi asks for what does not die

    Offered a share of the household, Maitreyi asks: will any of this keep me from dying?

    For anyone curious about why we love the people we love.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 1.10–1.11Ushasti in the famine

    A starving scholar eats a stranger's leftovers, then halts the singing at a king's sacrifice.

    For anyone who does their work by routine and sometimes wonders what it is really for.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 4.3.1–6Janaka asks: what is a person's light?

    The sun sets, the moon sets, the fire dies, the voices stop. What light is left?

    For anyone who has sat in a dark room and wondered how they still know they are there.
  • Prashna Upanishad · 1.1–1.5, 6.1–6.8Six seekers come to Pippalada

    Six learned men bring their questions to a sage. He tells them to wait a year.

    For anyone starting out with a new teacher, coach or mentor, and eager for answers.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 3.6, 3.8Gargi questions Yajnavalkya

    Silenced once before a hall of scholars, Gargi rises again with two questions like arrows.

    For a student who loves asking "and what is that resting on?"
