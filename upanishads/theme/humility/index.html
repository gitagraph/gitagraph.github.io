Humility

The proud are corrected, often by someone unexpected.

  • Chandogya Upanishad · 6.1–6.16Shvetaketu: 'You are that'

    A proud young scholar comes home, and his father teaches him with salt and a seed.

    For a graduate proud of a new degree, or anyone curious about what 'self' really means.
  • Kena Upanishad · 3–4The Yaksha, the gods and Uma

    The fire god boasts he can burn everything. A single blade of grass will not burn.

    For anyone who has wondered where their own talent and strength really come from.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.1–4.3Raikva, the man with the cart

    A famously generous king overhears two swans say a man with a cart outshines him.

    For anyone who has done well in life and quietly wonders what it all adds up to.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 7.1–7.26Narada learns that names are not enough

    He had studied every subject there was, and he was still unhappy. He asked why.

    For anyone who loves learning and wonders where all of it finally leads.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 2.1Proud Balaki and King Ajatashatru

    A scholar comes to teach a king, runs out of answers, and learns from a sleeper.

    For someone proud of their degree, and curious about what it still leaves out.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 5.1The quarrel of the senses

    Speech, sight, hearing and mind each walked out for a year. Then breath stirred.

    For anyone who has ever argued about who on a team matters most, or simply wondered what keeps us alive.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 5.11–5.18Six scholars learn from King Ashvapati

    Six famous scholars each knew one piece of the self. A king showed them the whole.

    For anyone curious how the sky, the sun and the earth could all be part of one self.
  • Prashna Upanishad · 1.1–1.5, 6.1–6.8Six seekers come to Pippalada

    Six learned men bring their questions to a sage. He tells them to wait a year.

    For anyone starting out with a new teacher, coach or mentor, and eager for answers.
