Breath, mind and bliss

Layers of a person, from food and breath to joy.

  • Taittiriya Upanishad · 3.1–3.6Bhrigu finds bliss

    A son asks his father what is real. Again and again, the father sends him back.

    For a student wondering what really lies at the center of a life.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.1–4.3Raikva, the man with the cart

    A famously generous king overhears two swans say a man with a cart outshines him.

    For anyone who has done well in life and quietly wonders what it all adds up to.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 5.1The quarrel of the senses

    Speech, sight, hearing and mind each walked out for a year. Then breath stirred.

    For anyone who has ever argued about who on a team matters most, or simply wondered what keeps us alive.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.10–4.15Upakosala and the fires

    Twelve years of service, no teaching, a heartsick student. Then the fires start to speak.

    For anyone who has wondered who is really looking out through their own eyes.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 1.10–1.11Ushasti in the famine

    A starving scholar eats a stranger's leftovers, then halts the singing at a king's sacrifice.

    For anyone who does their work by routine and sometimes wonders what it is really for.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 1.3.28From what is not to what is

    Three lines murmured during an ancient chant, and the Upanishad's own key to what they mean.

    For anyone who has recited these three lines at school or at home and wondered where they come from.
