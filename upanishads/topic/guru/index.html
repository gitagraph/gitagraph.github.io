Teacher and student

How a teacher tests, waits and teaches.

  • Katha Upanishad · 1.1–1.2Nachiketa and Death

    Given to Death by his angry father, a boy asks what happens after we die.

    For someone young weighing what is worth wanting, or anyone wondering what outlasts death.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.4Satyakama tells the truth

    A boy who does not know his father tells a stranger exactly that, and is accepted.

    For a student starting somewhere new, or anyone who has wondered whether to tell the plain truth about themselves.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 6.1–6.16Shvetaketu: 'You are that'

    A proud young scholar comes home, and his father teaches him with salt and a seed.

    For a graduate proud of a new degree, or anyone curious about what 'self' really means.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 8.7–8.12Indra, Virochana and the reflection in water

    Two kings look into a pan of water. One goes home satisfied; the other keeps asking.

    For anyone who has looked in a mirror and wondered who is doing the looking.
  • Taittiriya Upanishad · 3.1–3.6Bhrigu finds bliss

    A son asks his father what is real. Again and again, the father sends him back.

    For a student wondering what really lies at the center of a life.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 5.2–5.3What the thunder says: Da, Da, Da

    Three kinds of students, one teacher, one syllable, and three different lessons.

    For anyone who has stood under a monsoon sky and listened to the thunder.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.5–4.9Satyakama's four teachers

    Alone with a herd for years, a young man learns from a bull, fire and birds.

    For anyone who has learned as much from quiet, ordinary hours as from any classroom.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.1–4.3Raikva, the man with the cart

    A famously generous king overhears two swans say a man with a cart outshines him.

    For anyone who has done well in life and quietly wonders what it all adds up to.
  • Taittiriya Upanishad · 1.11The teacher's farewell

    A teacher's parting words to a student about to go out into the world.

    For a student finishing school or college and stepping into a life of their own.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.10–4.15Upakosala and the fires

    Twelve years of service, no teaching, a heartsick student. Then the fires start to speak.

    For anyone who has wondered who is really looking out through their own eyes.
  • Mundaka Upanishad · 1.1.1–7, 1.2.12–13Shaunaka's question: two kinds of knowing

    A wealthy householder asks for the one thing that makes everything else make sense.

    For a student wondering whether all this studying adds up to wisdom.
  • Prashna Upanishad · 1.1–1.5, 6.1–6.8Six seekers come to Pippalada

    Six learned men bring their questions to a sage. He tells them to wait a year.

    For anyone starting out with a new teacher, coach or mentor, and eager for answers.
