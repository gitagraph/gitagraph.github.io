Meditation

Holding something in mind as a way to the real.

  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 5.1That is full, this is full

    Take the whole from the whole, and the whole is still there.

    For anyone who has heard this verse chanted at the start of a reading and wondered what 'full' means here.
  • Kena Upanishad · 3–4The Yaksha, the gods and Uma

    The fire god boasts he can burn everything. A single blade of grass will not burn.

    For anyone who has wondered where their own talent and strength really come from.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.5–4.9Satyakama's four teachers

    Alone with a herd for years, a young man learns from a bull, fire and birds.

    For anyone who has learned as much from quiet, ordinary hours as from any classroom.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.1–4.3Raikva, the man with the cart

    A famously generous king overhears two swans say a man with a cart outshines him.

    For anyone who has done well in life and quietly wonders what it all adds up to.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 2.1Proud Balaki and King Ajatashatru

    A scholar comes to teach a king, runs out of answers, and learns from a sleeper.

    For someone proud of their degree, and curious about what it still leaves out.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 4.10–4.15Upakosala and the fires

    Twelve years of service, no teaching, a heartsick student. Then the fires start to speak.

    For anyone who has wondered who is really looking out through their own eyes.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 1.10–1.11Ushasti in the famine

    A starving scholar eats a stranger's leftovers, then halts the singing at a king's sacrifice.

    For anyone who does their work by routine and sometimes wonders what it is really for.
  • Chandogya Upanishad · 5.11–5.18Six scholars learn from King Ashvapati

    Six famous scholars each knew one piece of the self. A king showed them the whole.

    For anyone curious how the sky, the sun and the earth could all be part of one self.
