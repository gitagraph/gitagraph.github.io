Brihadaranyaka Upanishad

Shukla Yajurveda. The 'great forest teaching': Yajnavalkya with Maitreyi, Gargi and King Janaka.

  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 2.4 and 4.5Maitreyi asks for what does not die

    Offered a share of the household, Maitreyi asks: will any of this keep me from dying?

    For anyone curious about why we love the people we love.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 5.1That is full, this is full

    Take the whole from the whole, and the whole is still there.

    For anyone who has heard this verse chanted at the start of a reading and wondered what 'full' means here.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 5.2–5.3What the thunder says: Da, Da, Da

    Three kinds of students, one teacher, one syllable, and three different lessons.

    For anyone who has stood under a monsoon sky and listened to the thunder.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 2.1Proud Balaki and King Ajatashatru

    A scholar comes to teach a king, runs out of answers, and learns from a sleeper.

    For someone proud of their degree, and curious about what it still leaves out.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 1.3.28From what is not to what is

    Three lines murmured during an ancient chant, and the Upanishad's own key to what they mean.

    For anyone who has recited these three lines at school or at home and wondered where they come from.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 4.3.1–6Janaka asks: what is a person's light?

    The sun sets, the moon sets, the fire dies, the voices stop. What light is left?

    For anyone who has sat in a dark room and wondered how they still know they are there.
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad · 3.6, 3.8Gargi questions Yajnavalkya

    Silenced once before a hall of scholars, Gargi rises again with two questions like arrows.

    For a student who loves asking "and what is that resting on?"
